## Art. 1 — Subject matter, definitions and temporary nature

1.  This Code governs the phases, the thresholds, the transitional competences and the launch procedures of the Confederation, as well as the passage from the pre-confederal state to the operational confederal state.

2.  For the purposes of this Code, the following definitions apply:

    a) **transition**: the period between the establishment of the first pilot Circle and the declaration of closure provided for by Article 10;

    b) **phase**: the transitional period in which specific objectives, Codes and procedures are activated, with the related exit thresholds;

    c) **pilot Circle**: a Base Community, corresponding to Circle 1, established pursuant to Articles 4–6 of the Constitution for the initial experimentation;

    d) **Basin**: an aggregation of three or four pilot Circles that are territorially contiguous and ecologically connected, corresponding to the Intermediate Circle or Circle 2 referred to in Article 5 of the Constitution;

    e) **minimum Federation**: an aggregation of at least three Basins;

    f) **Basket 1-2**: the goods and services of the Sufficiency Basket governed by Code 1 that fall within levels 1 and 2 of the hierarchy referred to in Article 32 of the same Code, as specified by the sectoral Codes;

    g) **point of no return**: the resolution that makes irreversible the designated use of ecological or territorial commons;

    h) **Social Start-up Protocol**: Technical Annex A to this Code, adopted in accordance with Article 19 of Code 3, which defines a methodology for verifying the institutional social thresholds without introducing autonomous individual obligations or sanctions;

    i) **risk levels R0-R4**: the levels defined by Code 14. Until that Code is activated, the provisional classification shall be carried out by the Health Council in accordance with Code 6, Article 4;

    j) **First-Year Operating Charter**: Technical Annex C to this Code, adopted in accordance with Article 19 of Code 3, which brings together the minimum rules necessary for the launch of a pilot Circle without introducing autonomous obligations.

3.  This Code is temporary in nature and shall cease to produce normative effects with the declaration of closure referred to in Article 10, except for the memory provisions laid down therein. It may not establish permanent rights, obligations or competences beyond the transition period, nor modify the substantive content of Codes 1–15. Its provisions do not constitute an amendment of the Codes pursuant to Articles 37 and 38 of the Constitution. In the event of conflict, the Constitution prevails.

4.  The transitional classification of a good, a function or a competence does not determine its permanent regime.

5.  During the transition, the references in the Codes to Circle 1 shall be understood as referring to the pilot Circle and those to Circle 2 as referring to the Basin. Until an Extended Circle is constituted, the functions of Circle 3 shall be exercised by the minimum Federation.

6.  During the transition, the Balance provided for by Code 15 operates at the scale of the Basin and of the minimum Federation. The activation of the operational confederal phase does not imply that the Confederation comprises the whole of humanity.

7.  During the transition, the revision of the Constitution and of the Codes follows the founding regime of Article 38 of the Constitution.

## Art. 2 — Principles of the transition

1.  **Continuity of the Basket.** No change of phase shall be resolved if it entails the interruption of levels 1 and 2 of the Basket or a risk of interruption that is not adequately managed.
2.  **Reversibility.** Before a point of no return is adopted, every intervention shall provide, where technically applicable, for a reversibility plan.
3.  **Proportionality and gradualness.** The activation of functions, Codes and constraints shall be commensurate with the competences available, the resources surveyed and the risks actually present.
4.  **Effectiveness.** The merely formal constitution of Circles, Basins or Federations lacking territorial continuity, actual activity or a verifiable census is prohibited.
5.  **Transparency.** The phases, thresholds, censuses, resolutions and plans shall be published in accordance with Article 15 of Code 3 and submitted to a period for observations of at least seventy-two hours. Personal data shall be excluded or aggregated in accordance with Code 7, Article 2.2.
6.  **Non-commodification.** It is prohibited to establish rents on the launch of the Confederation, on the commons conferred during the transition or on life-saving competences.
7.  **Legality and coexistence.** During the transition the Circles shall operate in compliance with the legal order in force in the territory, pursuant to Article 38.6 of the Constitution. Article 37 of Code 4 and Chapter IV of Code 15 apply.
8.  **Voluntariness.** Accession is individual and voluntary. No one may be induced to cease external activities, to confer goods or to waive rights recognised by the legal order in force. This is without prejudice to the proportional contribution on external income provided for by Article 19-bis of Code 15, which shall be made known before accession.

## Art. 3 — Phases of the transition

1.  **Phase 0 — Pre-confederal.** The indicative duration is from zero to six months. In this phase:

    a) at least one pilot Circle shall be constituted with no fewer than thirty persons having joined;

    b) Codes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, Code 6 in its basic version and Chapter IV of Code 15 shall be activated;

    c) an initial census, aggregated and non-profiling, shall be carried out of the available land, of the L1 and L2 competences, of the machines and equipment and of the needs of Basket 1-2;

    d) a provisional minimum Storehouse in accordance with Articles 13–22 of Code 1 and a minimum Knowledge Storehouse in accordance with Article 3 of Code 5 shall be activated;

    e) basic L1 life-saving training in accordance with Code 6 shall be started;

    f) the Guarantee Circle and the Councils provided for by the active Codes shall be established, including the Storehouse Council, the Health Council and the Competences Council, also in merged or shared form or in the three Area Councils provided for by Article 12-bis, paragraph 12, of Code 3;

    g) the pilot Circle shall operate in accordance with the First-Year Operating Charter.

2.  **Phase 1 — Basin.** The indicative duration is from six to eighteen months. In this phase:

    a) three or four contiguous pilot Circles shall aggregate into a Basin;

    b) Codes 7, 8, 9 and 10 and C.1-Annex A shall be activated, in addition to the Codes of Phase 0;

    c) the provisional minimum federated Land Register provided for by Article 7 of Code 10 shall be started;

    d) the Basin food balance and a joint minimum Basket STV of Codes 9 and 10 shall be prepared;

    e) the land eligible to become a sanctuary shall be surveyed on a preliminary basis, in preparation for the activation of Code 13;

    f) the Network, Energy and Water, Agroecological, Habitat and Territory and Production Councils shall be activated;

    g) the Balance provided for by Code 15 shall be activated at the scale of the Basin.

3.  **Phase 2 — Minimum Federation.** The indicative duration is from eighteen to thirty-six months. In this phase:

    a) at least three Basins shall be aggregated;

    b) Codes 11, 12, 13 and 14 shall be activated, in addition to the Codes already activated;

    c) Chapter III of Code 15 and the Balance at the scale of the minimum Federation shall be activated;

    d) at least one sanctuary per Basin shall be established, pursuant to Article 4 of Code 13;

    e) the critical machines, the PPE and the collective protections shall be surveyed in accordance with Code 14;

    f) the Logistics, Care, Biodiversity and Safety Councils shall be activated;

    g) the first version of the Dependency Register shall be established, integrating the registers of the sectoral Codes.

4.  **Phase 3 — Operational Confederation.** The indicative duration is from thirty-six to sixty months. In this phase:

    a) Codes 1–15 and C.1-Annex A shall be activated in full operation, within the limits of the conditions of effectiveness provided for by the individual Codes;

    b) ecological, safety and food autonomy audits shall be carried out by independent L3 members drawn by lot in accordance with Article 36 of Code 4 and the sectoral Codes;

    c) the overcoming of at least fifty per cent of the registered critical dependencies shall be completed, on the basis of verifiable objectives and time limits;

    d) the node of the federated Network provided for by Code 7 shall be completed for each Basin.

5.  **Phase 4 — Closure.** The transition ends with the declaration of closure adopted in accordance with Article 10.

6.  The durations indicated are for guidance and do not replace the exit thresholds of Article 4. No phase may be passed before the related thresholds have been verified.

## Art. 4 — Activation and exit thresholds

1.  **Passage from Phase 0 to Phase 1.** The following are required:

    a) the continuous guarantee of levels 1 and 2 of the Basket for at least three months in the pilot Circle;

    b) a verified census and the functioning of the minimum Storehouse and of the minimum Knowledge Storehouse;

    c) the absence of unmanaged critical violations pursuant to Article 9, paragraph 2;

    d) the documented application of Level 1 of the Social Start-up Protocol;

    e) a Labour Balance, drawn up pursuant to Article 2 of Code 1, documenting for at least one quarter the coverage of levels 1 and 2 of the Basket within the limit of Article 3 of the same Code.

2.  **Passage from Phase 1 to Phase 2.** The following are required:

    a) self-production in the Basin of at least seventy per cent of levels 1 and 2 of the Basket, calculated according to the criteria of the applicable Basket STV;

    b) the functioning of the minimum Land Register;

    c) at least one area that is a candidate sanctuary, attested by the Habitat and Territory Council. In the absence of the Biodiversity Council, the provisional biological assessment shall be given by the Transition Council pursuant to Article 8, paragraph 2, with an explicit indication of its provisional character;

    d) the absence of unmanaged critical violations pursuant to Article 9, paragraph 2, verified in accordance with Codes 4, 9 and 10;

    e) the documented application of Level 2 of the Social Start-up Protocol;

    f) Basin Labour Balances that respect the limit of Article 3 of Code 1 for at least two consecutive quarters.

3.  **Passage from Phase 2 to Phase 3.** The following are required:

    a) the functioning of the provisional minimum Biodiversity Register;

    b) the functioning of the register of general risks;

    c) the establishment of at least one sanctuary per Basin;

    d) Safety Sheets in force for all activities classified R2 or higher;

    e) the absence of R4 incidents that are unmanaged or lack the required analysis of causes;

    f) the documented application of Level 3 of the Social Start-up Protocol.

4.  **Passage from Phase 3 to Phase 4.** The following are required:

    a) a positive federated audit on autonomy, carried out after at least six continuous months of operation without non-substitutable critical dependencies that compromise the continuity of the Basket;

    b) the entry in the Dependency Register of an exit plan with a definite time limit for each critical dependency still present;

    c) the functioning of the nodes of the federated Network in all the Basins;

    d) the documented maintenance of Level 3 of the Social Start-up Protocol;

    e) Labour Balances that respect the limit of Article 3 of Code 1 for four consecutive quarters or, failing that, the submission of a reasoned proposal for revision of that limit pursuant to Article 38 of the Constitution.

5.  The verification of the thresholds shall be carried out by the Transition Council, with the mandatory opinion of the Councils competent for the subject matter. The change of phase constitutes a constitutive decision pursuant to Article 3, paragraph 3, of Code 3 and shall be resolved by the General Assembly in accordance with the convening, quorum and procedure provided for by Codes 3 and 4. The resolution shall be published in aggregate and non-profiling form.

6.  The social opinion provided for by the Social Start-up Protocol may bear exclusively on the institutional verification of the threshold and on the possibility of proposing or resolving the change of phase. It may not produce individual sanctions, exclusions or limitations, nor make conditional the access to Code 4, to the Guarantee, to vital goods or to Basket 1-2.

## Art. 5 — Transitional government

1.  In each phase a distinct Transition Council shall be established, with a duration limited to the phase of reference.

2.  The Council is composed of three to five members with L2 or L3 competences relevant to the phase, including organisation, confederal law, agroecology, territory, safety and biodiversity.

3.  Appointment and duration are governed as follows:

    a) the members shall be drawn by lot from among suitable volunteers who have fulfilled the required CBO and the specific training, with the right to refuse the office, in accordance with Articles 4.3 and 4.4, letter d), of the Constitution;

    b) no member shall perform the function for more than ninety consecutive days or for more than one hundred and eighty days in total in three hundred and sixty-five days;

    c) renewal shall take place by staggered sortition, with a minuted handover, so as to guarantee the continuity of the Council throughout the phase;

    d) between two terms of office in the Transition Council there shall elapse at least twelve months of exclusive activity in ordinary CBO;

    e) simultaneous membership of the Transition Council and of the Storehouse Council in the same phase is prohibited;

    f) each member shall declare his or her conflicts of interest and shall abstain when there is a conflict or a direct coincidence between controller and controlled, in accordance with Code 3, Article 16.

4.  The Council shall operate with a quorum of at least two thirds of its members. Decisions shall be taken by consensus in the absence of reasoned objections, in accordance with Code 3, Article 4. Meetings shall be minuted and the minutes shall be kept for thirty-six months.

5.  The Transition Council shall not resolve the change of phase or the adoption of a point of no return. It may formulate proposals to the General Assembly, to which the decisions fall in accordance with Articles 4 and 6 of this Code.

6.  The Transition Council:

    a) coordinates the censuses;

    b) draws up the transitional plans;

    c) verifies the thresholds;

    d) proposes the activation of the Codes;

    e) feeds the Dependency Register;

    f) coordinates the Councils activated in the phase;

    g) verifies the application of the Social Start-up Protocol solely with regard to the institutional thresholds.

7.  The Transition Council may not exercise competences reserved to other Councils, cede to external parties the control of commons or assume external financial commitments that give rise to critical dependencies.

## Art. 6 — Commons during the transition

1.  The goods conferred during the transition shall be entered in a federated provisional inventory according to the applicable competence:

    a) Code 10, Article 7, for land and territorial goods;

    b) Code 5, Article 3, for essential knowledge;

    c) Articles 13–22 of Code 1 for the goods held in custody in the Storehouses;

    d) C.1-Annex A and Codes 9 and 14 for machines, seeds, PPE and collective protections, from their respective activation.

2.  The nature as inalienable common good of the goods provided for by Article 3.1 of the Constitution is not suspended during the transition. This Article governs exclusively custody and provisional entry.

3.  The transfer to the definitive regime of custody shall take place:

    a) for territorial and ecological goods, at the closure of Phase 2;

    b) for critical technological goods, at the closure of Phase 3.

    The transfer requires a resolution of the General Assembly and the mandatory opinion of the competent Council.

4.  Until the definitive regime is activated, acts of disposal exceeding ordinary maintenance and use in conformity with a Sheet in force are ineffective. Acts of sale and purchase, letting or mortgage of the commons remain prohibited, in accordance with Articles 3.1 and 3.2 of the Constitution.

5.  The provisional Storehouses shall keep separate accounts for goods in transition and for commons already entered in the definitive regime. Traceability and access follow Code 1; for the Knowledge Storehouse, Code 5 applies.

6.  The legal ownership of the goods during the transition is governed by Article 16 of Code 15.

## Art. 7 — Persons, competences and transitional training

1.  L1 life-saving training shall be provided free of charge from Phase 0 in accordance with Code 6 and, from the activation of the respective Codes, in accordance with Codes 13 and 14.
2.  Prior competences shall be recorded in accordance with Code 5, Article 10, paragraph 1. The provisional validation of continuity, the related time limits and the shortening of training paths are governed exclusively by Article 10, paragraphs 2–7, of Code 5. The Transition Council may not extend or derogate from those time limits.
3.  Every function classified R3 or higher requires mentoring by a person with verified L2 or L3 competence, until sufficient internal coverage is available.
4.  The transitional registry of competences shall be maintained in aggregate and non-profiling form, in accordance with Code 7, Article 2.2.
5.  The Transition Council shall consider the Social Start-up Protocol exclusively for the purposes of verifying the social thresholds of institutional sustainability and within the limits established by Technical Annex A.

## Art. 8 — Conflicts and Guarantee during the transition

1.  Code 4 applies from Phase 0.
2.  Until a competent Council is activated, the Transition Council may give, on a provisional basis, the non-binding mandatory opinions concerning the subject matter not yet covered. The opinion shall be noted as provisional and submitted to review within six months of the activation of the competent Council.
3.  The substitute function of paragraph 2 does not apply to the binding opinions of Health and Network provided for by Articles 7-bis, 7-quater and 8 of Code 3. Until the Network Council is activated, decisions requiring a binding Network opinion shall be suspended. The other decisions not dependent on that opinion may proceed.
4.  Suspensions necessary to deal with a serious and immediate R4 risk are immediately enforceable in every phase, in compliance with the procedures of the competent Codes.
5.  An appeal to the Guarantee is permitted within fourteen days in accordance with Article 17 of Code 3 and Article 17 of Code 4. No provision of this Code or of the Social Start-up Protocol may make conditional, delay or limit access to the Guarantee.
6.  During the transition, Chapter V of Code 4 applies within the limits of its Article 37.

## Art. 9 — Prohibitions during the transition

1.  During the transition it is prohibited to:

    a) establish rents on the launch, on the commons entered in the provisional inventory or on L1 life-saving training;

    b) sell, mortgage or encumber the land designated as a candidate sanctuary or as a candidate ecological corridor;

    c) patent, register, file, claim or maintain exclusive titles over wild or local genetic material or over the essential knowledge surveyed;

    d) assume external financial debts or conclude supply agreements that give rise to a critical proprietary dependency pursuant to Article 5 of Code 2, in the absence of an exit plan with a definite time limit entered in the Dependency Register;

    e) cede to external parties the control of the provisional Storehouse, of the provisional Land Register, of the provisional Biodiversity Register or of the Dependency Register;

    f) constitute Circles or Basins that are fictitious or lack the minimum requirements provided for by Article 2, paragraph 4, and by Article 3;

    g) impose paywalls on the STVs and on the Sheets essential for Basket 1-2, biodiversity or general safety.

2.  For the sole purposes of this Code, violations of paragraph 1, letters a), b), c), d) and e), are classified as critical; those of letters f) and g) are classified as substantial.

3.  The ascertainment, the precautionary and restorative measures and any remedies are governed by Code 4. The effects on decisions shall be determined in accordance with Article 18 of Code 3. The technical exclusion of a good or service remains within the competence of the Quality Council pursuant to Code 2.

## Art. 10 — Closure of the transition, memory and precedence

1.  The closure of the transition shall be resolved by the General Assembly by a qualified majority of two thirds, in accordance with Articles 13 and 14 of Code 3. The resolution shall be preceded by:

    a) the mandatory opinion of the federated Councils;

    b) the positive federated audit provided for by Article 4, paragraph 4;

    c) the verification of compliance with the conditions for closure.

2.  The declaration of closure produces the following effects:

    a) this Code shall cease to produce normative effects;

    b) the provisional inventories, the minutes of the Transition Councils and the resolutions on changes of phase shall remain preserved as historical memory in accordance with Code 7;

    c) the goods already transferred to the definitive regime remain subject to the sectoral Codes;

    d) the goods not yet transferred because the thresholds have not been reached shall be returned or transferred by resolution of the General Assembly and with the opinion of the competent Council.

3.  During the transition, in the event of conflict between this Code and Codes 1–15, this Code prevails exclusively to the extent strictly necessary to govern the activation sequence and the thresholds referred to in Article 4, guaranteeing the continuity of Basket 1-2. Such precedence does not create permanent competences and does not apply:

    a) to the Constitution;

    b) to the mandatory rules identified by Article 2, paragraph 2, of Code 3;

    c) to the binding negative opinions provided for by Article 8 of Code 3.

4.  After the closure of the transition, the precedence clauses of the sectoral Codes apply, including those of Code 10, Article 10, of Code 13, Article 11, and of Code 14, Article 10.

5.  The main normative links of this Code are: Constitution, Articles 3.1, 3.2, 4–6 and 32; Code 1, Articles 13–22 and 32; Code 2, Article 5; Code 3, Articles 3, 4, 8, 13–19; Code 4; Code 5, Articles 3 and 10; Code 6, Articles 4 and 11; Code 7, Article 2.2; Codes 8 and 9; Code C.1-Annex A; Codes 10–14; Code 15, Chapters III and IV.
