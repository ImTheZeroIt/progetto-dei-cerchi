# How it can begin: from mutual aid to the Confederation

**What this page is.** It tells in plain words the networks path, written in Code 16, Articles 11–20, and explains the reasons for its choices. It is not a rule: where it says something different from the Code, the Code prevails. The part on the boycott contains a proposal by the author, which applies only if the people of a network make it their own and vote for it (point 5).

## 1. The idea in brief

Change does not start from small separate communities that set an example: it starts from people where they already live. For years, mutual-aid networks and common neighbourhood storehouses are built inside today’s society and within its laws. The network spreads like an oil spot, from neighbourhood to neighbourhood and from country to country. When the people decide so, the networks become Circles, the neighbourhood storehouses become Common Storehouses and the rules come into force for those who accede.

Along the way the people of the networks may choose, by a vote, to stop all together working and consuming for profit: the boycott. It is their choice, not an obligation of the rules.

The pilot Circles, that is, the groups that want to live by the rules already today, remain: they are the workshops where the rules are tested before they serve everyone.

## 2. Why exemplary communities are not enough

Exemplary communities have existed for decades: ecovillages, Christiania, the communes of the 1970s, communities with a common purse. Some have worked well for generations, but none has grown to the point of changing society. Joining one often means moving house and sometimes town; in others, such as those where members keep working outside and put everything in common, the step is smaller, but it remains a choice about one’s whole life, which few people make. And a community on its own does not move the supply chains everyone depends on.

Mutual-aid networks ask for less and spread further: people stay where they are, begin to put part of their things in common, and the networks connect with one another. The communities that already exist are not an alternative to this road: they are its first nodes (point 3). There are concrete examples in Italy too:

- the Mutual Aid Societies of the nineteenth century, through which workers helped one another in sickness, unemployment and funerals before the welfare state existed;
- the Solidarity Purchase Groups, which since 1994 have bought together directly from producers;
- solidarity shops and food banks, which are already small common storehouses;
- the neighbourhood networks born during Covid, which brought shopping to those who could not go out.

## 3. Who already does it: solidarity communities

In Italy there are already communities that live by rules very similar to those of the project. They are the first nodes of the networks path and have decades of experience to learn from (Code 16, Art. 12, paragraph 5).

- **Mondo di Comunità e Famiglia.** It was born in 1978 at Villapizzone, Milan, from two families and a group of Jesuits who went to live together in an abandoned farmhouse. Today it counts dozens of communities in many regions. The families put their incomes into a common purse and each takes what they need; what is left over helps new communities to be born. They take in neighbourhood youngsters, children in foster care, former prisoners and immigrants. In some networks the families stay in their own homes and share the purse, a weekly meeting and mutual aid: this is exactly Phase A of this path.
- **Nomadelfia** (Grosseto), founded by don Zeno Saltini. About 270 people in some fifty families: no money circulates inside, there is no private property, people work for the community without pay. The families form groups that are reshuffled every three years, and over its history it has fostered thousands of children.
- **RIVE – Italian Network of Ecological Villages**, born in 1996. It brings together dozens of ecovillages and decides by a form of sociocracy: it is already a network of communities without a centre.

**What they teach.** A common purse founded on trust lasts for decades; an open door keeps the community tied to its neighbourhood; one can begin without moving house; cooperatives and associations are the legal forms that protect common goods.

**How they join: equivalence at the boundary** (Const. 4.5; Code 15, Arts. 21 and 21-bis). It works like a standard electrical socket: inside the house everyone has the wiring they want, but the plug is the same for all.

- **The fundamental rules apply to everyone:** the rights of every person, no violence, free exit, animals and environment, freedom of religion, rotating offices without permanent leaders, the Inviolable Core.
- **The rest is free.** An associated Community keeps its own way of organising itself, provided that at the boundary with the larger Circles it guarantees: the rights of every person and recourse to the common bodies; inventory and Balance in the common format; a share of work for the common functions; persons in the lists for drawing lots; the individual and secret vote in the General Assemblies; the common standards for exchanged goods; transit and hospitality for members of other Communities.
- **A common purse in money remains possible** during the transition, for external expenses, without giving access to Storehouses or decisions (Code 16, Art. 2, paragraph 9).
- **Conflicts:** internal ones are settled by the Community in its own way; serious offences (Grades 4–6) always go to the Guarantee Tribunal.
- **If something is missing at the boundary**, a Bridging Plan with a timetable is agreed, which changes only what is needed at the boundary. The verification is done within ninety days, without political or ideological assessments.
- **Those who want more** may also adopt the Codes, whole or one tool at a time.

## 4. Principles that do not change

1. **Free membership.** People join one by one and can always leave (Const. 4.8 and 38.1). No one is forced, neither by a majority nor by a State.
2. **Never violence.** Defence is civil and unarmed. Sabotage, damage, unlawful blocking of traffic and unlawful occupation are excluded in every case (Code 16, Art. 11, paragraph 2, letter c).
3. **No one is left without the Basket.** The neighbourhood storehouse gives the Emergency Basket to every participant who needs it, for whatever cause, and to those who depend on them, whatever they have chosen or voted (Art. 11, paragraph 2, letter e; Art. 14, paragraph 7).
4. **No leaders.** Offices drawn by lot, short and rotating from the start (Const. 4.3–4.4). A network without leaders is not stopped by arresting someone.
5. **Essential services never stop.** Those who farm, those who care, those who keep water, energy and transport running keep doing so and serve everyone (Art. 16).
6. **Until people choose, no one stops paying.** In Phases A and B, and always for those who do not take part in the boycott, rents, bills, debts and taxes are paid as usual (Art. 11, paragraph 3).

## 5. The phases

### Phase A — Seeds

Where: in every neighbourhood, village or building where someone begins. It lasts years (Code 16, Art. 12).

- **Mutual-aid networks:** shared shopping, help for those in difficulty, exchange of time and skills. The network decides in assembly, by the individual vote of each person.
- **Neighbourhood storehouses:** common stocks of long-life food, water, tools, spare parts; medicines only in the forms allowed by law.
- **Legal forms from the start:** storehouses, stocks and funds are held by a non-profit association or cooperative. Their assets are separate from those of the persons, so they stay protected however things go. The entity does not accept goods or money that take something away from the creditors of whoever gives them.
- **Alliance with the communities that already exist** (point 3).
- **Agreements with producers:** farmers, craftspeople, small workshops that sell directly to the network.
- **Fewer outside dependencies:** own energy (panels, energy communities), stocks, spare parts and the know-how to repair.
- **Pilot Circles:** where a group wants to live by the rules already today, it does so, and offers the network what it has learned.

What of the rules can already be used: the minimum Storehouse, the Labour Balance, drawing lots and rotation of offices, decisions in assembly, the voluntary restorative procedure (Art. 11, paragraph 4).

### Phase B — Oil spot

The neighbourhood network connects with others: first in the municipality, then across municipalities, regions and countries, through written and revocable agreements and without creating a centre (Art. 13).

- **Common structures on a small scale:** a knowledge archive, open standards, reserve stocks between neighbourhoods, transport agreements (point 8).
- **Map of the supply chains:** where food, water, energy and medicines come from, how much is in stock, who can do what.
- **Pacts with those who work in essential supply chains:** farmers, truck drivers, utility technicians, health staff commit to keep doing their work and to serve everyone, whatever happens. The pact is voluntary, revocable, without penalties, confidential, and obliges to nothing contrary to the law or to the employment contract.
- **Training and replacements:** for every essential job, other people are trained in advance so that they can take over.
- **Lawful trials:** the network checks whether it holds up through trials in which no one stops paying anything. For example, a weekend in which the neighbourhood lives only from the network’s storehouses, or a month in which people buy only from the network’s producers. Within thirty days the results are published on the portal.

**The preparedness threshold** (Art. 14). A network is ready when two conditions are met together:

1. **Emergency Basket guaranteed for six months** to all participants: the stocks in the storehouses, plus what the network’s producers have undertaken in writing to deliver, are enough for everyone for six months, and the funds cover the remaining costs.
2. **Pacts signed in water, energy, food and health care** in the territory, by enough people to keep those services running for everyone.

It is verified by three people drawn by lot from other networks, with the figures public on the portal and at least seventy-two hours for anyone’s comments. The verification only counts the numbers: it neither decides nor recommends anything.

**The vote.** When the threshold has been verified, the participants may vote on the boycott and on its date. The proposal is submitted by at least one tenth of the participants; it stays public for fourteen days and the vote lasts at least seven days. Everyone from the age of fourteen votes, each for themselves, in secret. It passes only if **a majority of all participants** votes yes, counting those who do not vote too. Before the first vote in a country, a legal opinion is sought and published.

**A country that starts alone.** A common date is sought, voted by the networks of several countries. If no other country approves the same date, the network can start only with a higher threshold: twelve months of Emergency Basket, an imports plan and the declared support of networks in other countries (Art. 14, paragraph 3).

### Phase C — Boycott

**What the rules say** (Art. 15). The boycott means stopping working and consuming **for profit**, not for needs: what serves only the market stops, such as advertising, useless production and finance, and everything that serves to live goes on. It is not a rule of the Code: it is a political, non-violent and collective choice that belongs to the people of the networks through their vote. Each person decides for themselves whether to make it and answers for it personally under the law; those who do not make it lose nothing. The entities of the networks, the promoting groups and the pilot Circles do not decide on it, do not fund it and do not use common goods for unlawful acts: they carry on only lawful activities, that is, the storehouses, the Emergency Basket, the solidarity funds, legal assistance and the services open to everyone.

**A possible proposal.** What follows is the proposal of the project’s author. A network can take it, change it and put it to the vote of Article 14, paragraph 6; until someone votes for it, it remains an idea, with its reasons.

1. **Once only, all together.** No trial days or weeks with rents and bills: they would expose people to debts and evictions without achieving anything, and would warn in advance those who want to stop the change. Trials are done before, and only lawful ones.
2. **Rent strike.** It is the strongest lever: evictions take months and become impossible when many stop paying. A precedent: Glasgow, 1915, when the rent strike led the British government to freeze increases by law.
3. **Bill strike**, where the network already has its own energy and water stocks. Workers in the electricity, gas and water networks keep doing their job according to their contract: the pact asks nothing different of them. A precedent: the «autoriduzione» (self-reduction) of bills in 1970s Italy.
4. **Suspension of bank debts:** mortgages and loans are suspended all together, never alone. Until that day they are paid as usual.
5. **Consumption and work only for needs:** people buy only what is necessary, if possible from the network; those who do not work in an essential service stop and put their time into the network; people stay at home. Staying at home is open to anyone, even to those who cannot expose themselves in the streets, and it is hard to repress. Precedents: Gandhi’s *hartal* in 1919 and the «silent strike» in Myanmar in December 2021.
6. **No run on banks or supermarkets.** Banks do not have cash for everyone and close their counters within hours (Cyprus 2013, Greece 2015); shelves empty, as in March 2020, and those who arrive late are left without. This is why stocks are built beforehand, in Phases A and B.

**Risks for those who take part.** If the boycott does not go all the way, those who have stopped paying risk debts, interest, lawsuits and being reported as bad payers. This must be known before voting. The solidarity funds and the network’s legal assistance help those in difficulty, for whatever cause and without asking how they voted (Art. 15, paragraph 4). The common goods, held by the entities from Phase A, remain separate from those of the persons.

**Essential workers.** Those who keep water, energy, food, health care and transport running must not carry all the weight (Art. 16): they receive the Emergency Basket first, for themselves and those who live with them, without taking anything from others; they work short shifts, relieved by trained people; if they risk their job or a lawsuit, the legal fund supports them; after the conversion their work counts as CBO.

**This is where the Circles are truly born.** While people stay at home, neighbours pool their shopping, those who farm bring food to the neighbourhood, the nurse looks after those who are ill in the building. A neighbourhood that helps itself to resist is already a Community.

### Phase D — Conversion

The conversion is decided by the participants of a network with a vote, with the same guarantees as the vote on the boycott. **It does not require the boycott or the threshold:** a network can convert even without them (Art. 17, paragraph 1). It applies only to those who accede, one by one.

- The networks become **Circles 1**, starting from what exists, neighbourhoods and municipalities, from 30 to 500 people; a larger network forms several neighbouring Circles. The first offices are drawn by lot among those who accede.
- The neighbourhood storehouses, and the shops and supermarkets whose owners accede and confer them, become **Common Storehouses**. At first they give at least the Emergency Basket, with the fair rationing of Code 1.
- **No one loses the home they live in.** Those who confer it to the Circle keep its use; a home with a mortgage or a lease is not conferred while the tie lasts, and those who live in it keep it under the law. The Circle asks for no one’s eviction and gives housing to anyone who loses their home (Art. 17, paragraph 5).
- **Those who do not accede** remain free and are not treated as adversaries. They keep their home, their goods and their rights, and can join whenever they want or ask for the Autonomous Coexistence Pact (Const. 4.9 and 4.9-bis). They do not work in the CBO and do not take from the Storehouses, but essential services stay open to them too, as do the vital guarantees of Const. 1.3.
- **The State.** The State is made of people: if the majority has chosen, there are two roads that lead to the same point. If the majority wins in the institutions too, through elections, a referendum or a constituent assembly, the law can recognise the Communities, entrust public goods to them and keep guaranteeing services until the Circles cover them; the Circles use that law (Art. 17, paragraph 6). If the institutions resist, the old system loses strength as people work in the new organisation.

**Money.** The real change happens when those who produce, transport and distribute work in the new organisation. From that moment food, energy and goods are no longer sold: they are taken from the Common Storehouses according to need. Inside, money buys nothing any more, with no need to confiscate anything. The passage can be gradual, sector by sector.

**Imports** (Art. 18). As long as there are countries that have not changed system, means of payment are still needed for energy, medicines and components that are not produced. Three roads together: reduce them first, with own energy, stocks and spare parts; the Common Fund, fed also by savings that people pay in freely, only for purchases outside (those who do not pay in lose nothing); the sale outside of surpluses, without profit, and direct exchanges with networks in other countries.

### Phase E — Confederation

From the conversion, every Circle born from a network is a pilot Circle in Phase 0 of Code 16. The Circles form Basins and Federations, the common structures pass to the Councils of Circles 2 and 3, and the plans to exit dependencies are completed, up to the closure of the transition (Art. 17, paragraph 8). No threshold is shortened.

## 6. The promoting groups

Change holds better if it happens in several countries together: foreign supplies, the reactions of other States and capital flight do not stop at one border. This is why promoting groups may be born in every country, connected to one another (Art. 19).

The risk to avoid is well known: in history many groups born to lead a revolution became the new power, or split over questions of command. This is why the promoting groups follow from the start the same rules as the system they prepare.

1. **They prepare, they do not govern:** they help networks and storehouses to be born, map the supply chains, collect the pacts, spread knowledge. They do not decide for the networks.
2. **No leaders:** offices drawn by lot among volunteers, ninety days at most, rotating. No leader, no permanent spokesperson, no party.
3. **Open and transparent:** anyone can take part; decisions, accounts and documents are public, except data that put people at risk.
4. **Autonomous in every country**, connected by common rules and not by a centre. Where organising is dangerous, the other groups support them without exposing people.
5. **They do not decide the boycott or the date:** these belong to the people of the networks, through the vote.
6. **They dissolve themselves** when the networks of their territory become Circles. No one keeps a role or an advantage for having «made the revolution».

## 7. The portal

To give birth to networks a common tool is needed: a portal, and later an app, where people get informed, join and organise (Art. 20). It serves to learn the rules in all languages, find nearby networks, join even without a public name, organise offers, needs, inventories and shifts, hold assemblies and votes, and count: how many people take part, how many months of Emergency Basket the network guarantees, how many pacts are signed.

People who take part can become a target, especially where organising is dangerous. This is why the portal:

1. **has no centre:** it is made of many connected servers, run by different networks; if one is shut down, the others go on (Const. 23.1);
2. **collects little data,** only what is necessary and for the time necessary; counts are public only in aggregate form and all profiling is forbidden (Code 7, Art. 7);
3. **is encrypted,** and votes follow the guarantees of electronic voting of Code 7 (Art. 7-bis);
4. **has open code:** anyone can check it, copy it and run it in their own country;
5. **also works offline:** if the internet is cut, networks organise and vote in person.

To begin, nothing has to be built from scratch: federated, open-source tools for forums, chat and events already exist.

## 8. The common structures

Not a government, but structures that build and run the networks and the exchange of resources between territories. Real examples that work this way: the Universal Postal Union (since 1874), railway standards, internet standards decided in open groups. They have technical tasks and common rules, but no power over people.

In the Constitution, Circles 2 and 3 coordinate logistics, railways and energy «without general power of command», and Circle 4 is a protocol of open standards that «is not a government» (Const. 4.2 and 5.1).

| Network | What it does | Where it is in the texts |
|---|---|---|
| Transport | Railways, ports and corridors between territories; local last mile | Const. 11.2; Code 11, Art. 6-quater; Code 15, Art. 10 |
| Energy | Links between territories: those who produce more help those who produce less | Const. 15.1; Code 3, Art. 12-bis, paragraph 6-bis |
| Reserves and Pool | Common stocks for drought, famine, disasters; moving surplus where it is lacking | Code 15, Arts. 4, 5, 8, 9 and 9-bis |
| Data and knowledge | Federated Network, Knowledge Storehouse, AI of Circles 2 and 3 | Const. 22–23; Code 7, Art. 4 |
| Technical standards | Technical sheets under a free licence: a spare part made in one place works everywhere | Code 2; Code 3, Art. 14-bis |

Rules for all of them:

1. **Closed mandate.** Each structure has a written task: to build, maintain and run a network. It does not decide on the lives of people or Communities and cannot punish anyone.
2. **Who runs them.** Technical Councils drawn by lot, with short and rotating offices (Const. 6.5; Code 3, Art. 12-bis).
3. **Those who use decide.** Important choices, such as a new line or a decommissioning, are voted by the people of the Communities served (Const. 32.1–32.2; Code 3, Art. 14).
4. **Everything public,** except data that put people or infrastructure at risk.
5. **No blackmail.** A network cannot be interrupted to obtain a decision from a Community (Code 15, Arts. 11 and 20).
6. **More than one road.** No Community depends on a single node or a single line for the goods of the Basket.

## 9. Defending oneself without violence

The path must be absolutely non-violent: disarmament is part of the Inviolable Core of the Constitution (Const. 16 and 37.4, letter c). But non-violence does not mean being unable to defend oneself.

**What is allowed:**

- **protecting oneself and others:** stopping an ongoing attack with strictly necessary force, without weapons and without harming beyond what is needed to end it (Const. 17.9; Code 14, Art. 8-sexies; Code 4, Art. 26-bis). Until the conversion the State’s law on self-defence applies and emergency services are called;
- **interposing:** standing between the attacker and the person attacked;
- **accompanying:** never leaving the most exposed people alone;
- **documenting:** violence done in public is paid for in consent;
- **withdrawing:** leaving, protecting places, offering no targets.

**What is not allowed:** weapons, attacks, revenge, harm to people and to things. In the networks path sabotage, damage, unlawful blocking of traffic and unlawful occupation are excluded in every case (Code 16, Art. 11, paragraph 2, letter c). The «non-destructive sabotage of the infrastructures of aggression» of Const. 16.4 is something else: it concerns only the defence of the Confederation, once it exists, against an armed aggression.

**Why this limit is also a practical choice.** Violence justifies repression, unites those in uniform against those who use it and drives away the majority. Non-violence is what makes the oil spot possible.

## 10. The reaction of States

A whole people cannot be forced to work: it would take an armed person behind every worker, and soldiers, police officers and officials are people too, with families who take part in the networks. The more the oil spot spreads, the harder it is to find anyone willing to repress it.

The researchers Erica Chenoweth and Maria Stephan studied more than three hundred campaigns between 1900 and 2006: non-violent struggles succeeded about twice as often as armed ones. Two factors weigh more than the others: how many people take part, and whether the armed forces and the police stop obeying orders to use force.

What States can do: repress, arrest those who organise, replace some workers with soldiers, block supplies and imports, divide through propaganda and emergency laws.

The answers of the path:

1. **Never violence**, not even in the face of violence.
2. **No leader to arrest:** offices drawn by lot and rotating, decisions in assemblies.
3. **Essential services do not stop:** if people keep having food, water and care, the propaganda of chaos does not work.
4. **Talk to those in uniform:** soldiers and police officers are people to win over, not enemies.
5. **Nothing to repress beforehand:** until people choose, everything is lawful; afterwards, it starts once only and with many.

## 11. Frequently asked questions

**When is a network strong enough?** When it has the Emergency Basket for six months for all participants and pacts signed in water, energy, food and health care, verified by people from other networks. Twelve months if the country starts alone.

**How are energy, medicines and components that are not produced paid for?** By reducing them first, with the Common Fund fed by those who want to, and by selling surpluses outside without profit.

**What happens to someone with a mortgage or a rent?** Until the choice, people pay as usual. The home stays with whoever lives in it; if someone risks losing it, the network gives legal assistance and the Circle gives housing.

**And those who do not want to join?** They remain free and keep everything they have. Essential services stay open to them too; they do not work in the CBO and do not take from the Storehouses.

**Do those who work in essential services carry all the weight?** No: they receive the Basket first, work short shifts relieved by trained people, and have the legal fund if they risk their job.

**And if it goes badly?** Storehouses and common goods are held by associations and cooperatives from the start, separate from those of the persons. Solidarity funds and legal assistance help those in difficulty. No one is left alone.

**Is the boycott compulsory?** No. A network can convert into a Circle even without it, and those who do not take part lose nothing.
