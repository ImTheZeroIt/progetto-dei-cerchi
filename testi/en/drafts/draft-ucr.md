# UCR Technical Annex and first balance of hours

*Working translation. The Italian text is the authoritative one.*

**Calibration draft 0.2, not adopted.** Code 3 (Art. 9) entrusts to a Technical Annex the method for calculating the reproduction cost of goods and services, measured in Reproduction Cost Units (UCR). That annex has never been written. This page proposes a first version of it and uses it straight away for the question on which the whole system depends: are fifteen hours a week enough? Version 0.2 (27 October 2026) brings the draft into line with the rules in force on that date.

**The answer, in brief.** Counted on need, the Basket costs between 8 and 11 hours a week of internal labour. The manufactured goods required, also counted on need (goods that last, are repaired and are shared), add 1.4 to 2.9 hours. In all, between 9 and 14 hours: below the limit, with a margin of 1 to 6 hours. It is a first count, full of declared assumptions. The most complete independent study instead measures today’s way of producing: adding to the minimum scenario the items it lacks, an estimate by the authors leads to about 27 hours. The difference between 14 and 27 is what the project promises to eliminate: it is not proven, and only an experiment can confirm it.

## 1. What the UCR is

One UCR corresponds to one hour of ordinary Base Contribution. Materials, energy and logistics are converted into hours (Code 3, Art. 9, para. 1). The UCR is not a price, is not attributed to persons and is not a means of exchange (Art. 9, paragraphs 2 and 7). It serves three purposes: comparing alternatives, making visible how much labour a choice costs, establishing which body decides (Art. 7).

It serves to estimate costs, not to measure anyone’s CBO. In particular it is not used to calculate the CBO share of the Pool: Code 15 forbids deriving it by an automatic conversion between units of product and hours, and requires the work actually necessary and documented to be counted (Art. 7, para. 4).

**Who approves the Annex.** The calculation method is common to all the Communities of Circle 2: it is prepared by the Accounting and Quality Councils in federated coordination, proposed by Circle 2 and approved by its General Assembly. Each Community, through its own Assembly, then approves its own values according to that method: coefficients, measured hours, prices and lifetimes of goods (Code 3, Art. 9, para. 3, and Art. 19, para. 3, as corrected on 27 October 2026). In this way the UCR of different Communities can be compared, as the Pool, the Balance and exchanges between Storehouses require.

## 2. The method

The Code lists seven points that the Annex shall govern (Art. 9, para. 4). The draft proposes a rule for each.

| Point | Proposed rule |
|---|---|
| a) Time | One hour of labour is worth one UCR, whoever performs it and whatever the task. It is counted to the quarter hour. Preparation, tidying up and specific mandatory training are included. The increases for unpleasant tasks (Code 1, Art. 10) are recognition in CdR and do not change the UCR. |
| b) Materials | A material produced by the Community is worth the UCR required to produce it. A recovered material is worth the hours of recovery. A material purchased outside is worth the hours actually worked to produce it, when they are known; otherwise its price multiplied by the hours-per-euro coefficient of the country in which it is produced (see below). A durable good is spread in equal shares over the years of its useful life. |
| c) Energy | Energy produced by the Community is worth the UCR of the plant and its maintenance, divided by the kilowatt-hours produced over its useful life. Purchased energy is worth price times coefficient. |
| d) Digital services | Hours of maintenance of the Network plus shares of the devices, divided among those who use the service. |
| e) Time | No discount rate and no interest: an hour is worth an hour, today and in ten years. The hours-per-euro coefficient is updated every year. A value is recalculated when the data change by more than a fifth. |
| f) Certification | The Accounting Council certifies the value on the basis of the STV or the SVS (Art. 9, para. 5) and publishes, for each datum, the source and an uncertainty class: measured, estimated, assumed. |
| g) Revision and challenge | Annual revision of the method in Circle 2 and of the values in each Community. Anyone may challenge a value by bringing a datum; the Council replies with public reasons. |

**Logistics.** Hours of transport, plus the share of the vehicle, plus the energy consumed.

**An hour is worth an hour, whoever works it and wherever.** For external purchases a way is needed to go from euros to hours. Price alone is not enough, because it reflects wages and not hours: a good produced where labour is poorly paid costs few euros and contains many hours. Converting it with the European average would mean exchanging one hour of one’s own labour for many hours of someone else’s labour. The draft therefore proposes three rules, in order.

1. **Actual hours.** If the hours worked to produce a good are known, those are used.
2. **Coefficient of the country of production.** If they are not known, the price is multiplied by the hours of labour per euro of the country in which the good is produced. If it is produced in several countries, each part of the price has its own coefficient.
3. **European average.** If it is not known where it is produced, the coefficient of the economy in which the Community is located is used, and the value is certified as “assumed”.

The coefficient is the ratio between the hours worked and the output of an economy. In the European Union, in 2024: 353.7 billion hours for 18,043 billion euros, that is **0.0196 hours per euro**: 51 euros of output contain on average one hour of labour. For other countries the draft derives it from output per inhabitant, assuming the same hours worked per inhabitant as in the European Union. The coefficient uses the country’s average productivity, whereas exported goods usually come from more productive sectors: the hours in imported goods are therefore probably overestimated.

| Country of production | Hours per euro | One hour of labour every |
|---|---|---|
| European Union | 0.020 | 51 euros |
| China | 0.064 | 16 euros |
| Brazil | 0.082 | 12 euros |
| Indonesia | 0.17 | 5.8 euros |
| Vietnam | 0.18 | 5.6 euros |
| Côte d’Ivoire | 0.31 | 3.2 euros |
| India, Bangladesh | 0.33 | 3.1 euros |
| Ethiopia | 0.75 | 1.3 euros |
| Rwanda | 0.80 | 1.2 euros |

An example of actual hours: in Rwanda coffee growing requires about 251 working days per hectare and yields on average 1.69 kg of cherries per plant. With 2,500 plants per hectare, seven-hour working days, six kilos of cherries per kilo of green coffee and 1.19 kilos of green per kilo of roasted (assumption), one kilo of roasted coffee contains about 3 hours of plantation labour. Converted with the European average, the same kilo would contain 0.3.

**What makes up the cost: labour, means of production, transport.** The real cost of a good has three parts: the hours of those who produce it, the share of the means used to produce it (machines, materials, energy, converted into hours) and transport. A country’s coefficient does not measure distance: it estimates how many hours are needed there, with the means of production used there. The coffee contains three hours because it is picked by hand, with few means; with better tools it would contain fewer, wherever it was grown. Transport is counted separately and weighs little: in May 2026 a container from Shanghai to Genoa cost about 4,100 dollars, that is about 20 cents per kilo of goods with a twenty-tonne load: less than one hundredth of a UCR.

These are indicative conversions (Art. 9, para. 6), to be improved with supply-chain data.

**What the UCR does not measure.** It does not measure environmental impact, which is assessed by the STV in accordance with Code 2. It does not include domestic work carried out in the Care Nuclei. It does not include civic functions, which appear in the Labour Balance and not in the cost of individual goods.

### 4.3 Manufactured goods, counted on need

Machines, tools, panels, building materials, medicines, textiles, electronics: the Community does not produce them itself. Here they are counted like food, starting from what is needed and not from what is consumed today. For each good: how much it costs, how many years it lasts, how many persons share it. Euros become hours with the coefficient of section 2.

That is 1.4 hours a week per contributor. The inventory is converted with the European average. With the rule of section 2 a good made half in China contains about twice the hours: doubling the whole inventory gives 2.9 hours. The factor of 2 is an assumption of the project, without a source.

**Housing.** The floor area comes from Code 1, Art. 47-bis, para. 2, in force since 19 October 2026: a living room and a separate bedroom, 30 m² for one person, 45 for two, 60 for three, then 15 more per person up to the fifth and 10 beyond. If the Circles 0 are like Italian households today (assumption), the average is about 22 m² per person. With the 15 m² of the first version of this draft and without Bridge Houses the inventory was worth 6,650 hours: still 1.4 hours a week, 2.7 with the factor for imported goods. The Bridge Houses alone are worth 76 hours a year.

**The Network.** The minimum provision of Code 7 (Art. 3, para. 9) is an access point in every dwelling that requests it, a personal device for those who request it from the age of twelve and, in every Circle 1, an assisted access point with shared devices. There is no computer per Nucleus: every person already has a personal AI Node (Art. 5-bis).

**What this inventory is worth.** Prices and lifetimes are drafting assumptions, listed one by one in the program: they are to be replaced with real quotations. A wrong price changes little, but the margin has narrowed: tripling the entire inventory, the complete count reaches 12.0 hours in scenario A and 15.2 in scenario B, just above the limit. For comparison, today’s European manufacturing employs about 14,200 hours for 150 persons, food industry excluded: the inventory on need asks for less than half of that.

The count says how many hours would be needed if the Confederation produced them.

### 4.5 What the count does not include

1. **Housework in the Nuclei.** According to the European time-use survey (data collected between 2008 and 2015), each person devotes on average between three and four hours a day to home and family; the 30–50 minutes for preparing meals are an estimate of the project, not of that source. The count brings into the CBO only one collective meal a day, the nursery, the laundry and the cleaning of common spaces: 1.8 hours a week per contributor. All the rest remains in the Nuclei and is not counted.
2. **Some items not estimated:** repair of tools, mandatory training. Civic protection and the common functions of the Basin have only a minimum estimate, to be replaced with the first real Balances.
3. **Start-up.** Building the Community costs more than maintaining it.
4. **What the project eliminates.** Commerce, advertising, finance, insurance: they are not in the count, because in the system of Circles they do not exist. It is a choice of the project, not a datum.

**Where the difference between 14 and 27 hours comes from.** The count of this draft and the study measure two different things. The study measures the world as it is: in its minimum scenario 29% of the hours are agricultural work (according to an assumption of the project, which the study does not verify, largely performed where an hour of work produces little) and 40–45% are services; the items that the authors add in the discussion weigh more than all the rest. This count measures the system as the project designs it: agriculture with European yields and equipment, administration limited to one tenth of the hours, no trade, no finance, no insurance, goods that last and are shared. The difference is therefore the project’s wager, put into numbers. The authors of the study reach a similar conclusion: none of their scenarios is realistic, reducing consumption is not enough, the way of producing must change.

### 4.7 What can be drawn from it

1. **Counted on need, the count adds up.** Between 9 and 14 hours a week, everything included, against a limit of 15.
2. **The margin is 1 to 6 hours, and the uncertainty is of the same order.** Many items are assumptions. In scenario B, tripling the manufactured goods, the count slightly exceeds the limit: it is not a measurement.
3. **The result holds for the system as it is designed, not for today’s way of producing.** With that, the independent estimate gives about 27 hours. The fifteen hours depend on what the project changes: no waste, no intermediation, durable goods, light administration. These are the things to verify first.
4. **Everything depends on where the boundary between CBO and Nucleus runs.** If all today’s housework entered the CBO, on its own it would be worth about 34 hours a week per contributor: an order of magnitude, calculated with three hours a day for all 150 persons. The texts leave each Community to choose what to make collective: it is the decision that weighs most on the limit.
5. **Health and education cannot be sustained with 150 persons.** Scenario A asks for 0.7 health workers and 1.9 teachers full-time by today’s standards: numbers too small to guarantee a service. They are to be shared within the Basin, as the Constitution provides for the hospital (Article 5.1).
6. **The limit is each person’s, not the sum’s.** Specialist hours are divided among those who are qualified (Code 1, Art. 6, para. 3). At 780 hours a year each, health care at European level requires 9 qualified persons out of 93; the minimum level, between one and two. And agricultural work is seasonal: the harvest of nuts is concentrated in a few weeks, whereas the permitted peak is 25 hours for two weeks (Code 1, Art. 6, para. 5).

Code 1 already provides for what happens if the hours are not enough: deferrable activities are reduced, documentation is simplified, recourse is had to volunteering, to mutualism between Circles and to the Pool, and finally the activation of the Solidarity Protocol is requested (Art. 2, para. 8, letters a–d). And it indicates the quarterly Labour Balances as the basis for verifying the limit (Art. 2, para. 9). This draft is the count made beforehand; those Balances will be the count made afterwards.

## 5. Robots and automation

The count in section 4 uses today’s machines. This section says how an automation is counted, where it is most needed and how much it can change the result. The rules in force are in Code 7, Article 6, which applies to every automation, and in Article 6-bis for robots ([proposal 8](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/proposals.html), accepted on 19 October 2026).

### 5.1 How it is counted

A robot is a means of production, so it enters the count like any other. Article 6-bis, paragraph 7, requires that, before it is introduced, the hours needed to build it, power it, maintain it, train those who use it and dispose of it be compared in UCR with the hours of work it saves. The comparison is calculated under Code 3 (Arts. 5 and 9) and certified by the Accounting Council. It is worthwhile when the hours saved are more. Code 7 also requires that the time saving be documentable (Art. 6, para. 1).

An example: a machine costing 60,000 euros that lasts fifteen years costs 78 UCR a year, 157 if it is produced half where labour yields less. The training of those who use it (40 hours, example) is worth 2.7 UCR a year and the end of life (5% of the price, example) 3.9. With fifty hours of maintenance that is between 135 and 217 hours a year. It is worthwhile if it saves more than that.

The saving does not go to whoever owns the machine, because no one owns it: the time freed remains with the Community, which decides how to use it (Code 7, Art. 6, para. 6). Article 6-bis confirms this rule for robots too (para. 7).

**Risky tasks.** When a robot of the Community can safely perform a task classified for risk, no person may be assigned to it, except for tests of the manual procedure and breakdowns (Code 7, Art. 6-bis, para. 6; Code 1, Art. 8, para. 3). Where the robot exists, those hours leave the work of persons and the supervision hours remain; the CdR supplement for risk no longer applies either.

**Raw materials.** A Community of 150 persons does not dig mines: metals and minerals come from outside, inside the manufactured goods. The Constitution prohibits any activity that causes irreversible damage to soil and water (Article 14.2): read literally, it prohibits any new mine. Two changes accepted on 19 October 2026 ([proposals 6 and 7](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/proposals.html)) qualified the subsoil as a common good and asked each Community for an inventory of what can be recovered from buildings, vehicles and landfills. Whether and when to permit extraction remains an open question.

### 5.3 How much the count can change

In scenario A the labour that machines can lighten (fields, processing, energy and water, maintenance, logistics) is about 16,700 hours: just over half of the internal labour. Labour between persons (cooking, care, learning) is about 13,200 hours, and the project chooses not to automate it.

| If machines remove | Hours a week per contributor | Counting that machines cost one fifth of what they save (assumption) |
|---|---|---|
| nothing more than today | 7.7 | 7.7 |
| a quarter of those hours | 6.7 | 6.9 |
| half | 5.8 | 6.2 |
| three quarters | 4.8 | 5.4 |

Every ten per cent less is worth 23 minutes a week for each person.

### 5.4 Outlook

1. **There is a limit, and it is intended.** Even automating everything that can be automated, the work between persons remains: between three and four hours a week. The project does not aim at zero hours: it aims to free persons from dangerous and repetitive work. For dangerous work this is already a rule: where a robot can do it safely, no one is assigned to it.
2. **Machines are not free.** They cost hours, energy and raw materials, including training and disposal. The real limit is ecological before it is technical.
3. **Who gains is the difference from today.** Today a machine that replaces labour takes income away from someone and gives it to whoever owns it. Here it takes the Basket away from no one and reduces everyone’s hours.
4. **No promises.** The count of this draft holds with the machines that exist. Better robots would improve it; the project does not depend on them.

## 6. The thresholds of Code 3

Until 19 October 2026 Article 7 of Code 3 distinguished operations of low value (below 500 UCR), medium value (from 500 to 2,500) and high value, for every Community. With the proposed coefficient, for a Community of 150 persons 500 UCR were 1.3% of the annual labour required (scenario A) and 2,500 UCR 6.7%; for a Community of 30 persons, 6.7% and 34%. [Proposal 11](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/proposals.html), accepted on 19 October 2026, linked the thresholds to the size of the Community (Art. 7, para. 3-bis): the low threshold is 100 UCR plus one per person, the high threshold 500 UCR plus five per person, with 500 and 2,500 as the maximum.

| Community | Low threshold | High threshold |
|---|---|---|
| 30 persons | 130 UCR: about 6,600 euros, 1.7% of the annual labour | 650 UCR: about 33,200 euros, 8.7% |
| 150 persons | 250 UCR: about 12,800 euros, 0.7% | 1,250 UCR: about 63,800 euros, 3.4% |
| 500 persons | 500 UCR: about 25,500 euros, 0.4% | 2,500 UCR: about 127,600 euros, 2.0% |

The percentages are always calculated on the same basis: the hours required in scenario A, as in the register of parameters. All the persons of the Community at the start of the quarter are counted, minors included, and guests are not (para. 3-bis). The General Assembly recalibrates the values, but may not remove the proportion to the number of persons (para. 3-ter). Operations of medium risk with a value between the high threshold and 2,500 UCR are in any case subject to paragraphs 2 and 4 of Article 7-ter (para. 3-ter); those of high or critical risk, even below the low threshold, follow the special procedure of Article 7-bis and the decision of the Assembly of Circle 1 (para. 3).

## What is needed to adopt it

**Data to be checked, and who could do it.**

- **Hours worked and output of the European Union, and the coefficient of 0.0196 hours per euro:** an expert in economic statistics, who checks the Eurostat data and says how VAT and imports weigh.
- **Country coefficients and the factor of 2 for imported goods:** an economist familiar with input-output tables (for example EXIOBASE). Today the factor of 2 has no source.
- **Age structure, parents, 5% reductions, eighteen-year-olds:** an expert in demography; after the start, the Community’s data.
- **Yields, hours per hectare, losses, oil yield:** an agronomist or someone who farms organically in the area.
- **Meals per hour, laundry, cleaning, logistics, food processing:** people who work in canteens, laundries and warehouses, with measured hours.
- **Hours of health care, care of the elderly, nursery and school:** people who work in these services; for health care, the Health Council.
- **Prices and lifetimes of manufactured goods:** real quotations from suppliers and craftspeople; for panels and heat, an energy technician.
- **Housing of 22 m² per person:** a building technician who counts the real floor area of the Circles 0.
- **Civic protection and common functions of the Basin:** the first real Labour Balances.

**Open choices.**

1. **How to convert external purchases:** country coefficient, European average or input-output tables. The draft recommends input-output tables where they exist and the country coefficient, certified as “estimated”, in other cases.
2. **Where the boundary between CBO and Nucleus runs** (how many collective meals, how much laundry): it is the choice that weighs most, from 6.6 to 9.8 hours. The Annex shows the effect; the Labour Balance of each Community decides.
3. **52 or 48 weeks a year.** The draft recommends 48, to allow breaks, or else stating openly that the count does not provide for them.
4. **Which scenario to use to verify the limit.** The draft recommends scenario B, the more prudent one.
5. **A category of its own for learning** in the Labour Balance, with an amendment of Code 1, or else “other activities”.
6. **The rule for the Basin share:** the draft proposes dividing the hours of the common functions of the Basin by the persons of the Basin and multiplying them by the persons of each Community, with the same criteria for associated Communities too (Code 15, Art. 21-bis, para. 2, letter c).
7. **No discount rate, and recalculation when the data change by more than one fifth:** the draft recommends keeping both rules.

**Approval procedure.**

1. The Accounting and Quality Councils of the Communities, in federated coordination, prepare the common calculation method (Code 3, Art. 9, paragraphs 3 and 4).
2. Circle 2 proposes it and its General Assembly approves it (Art. 9, para. 3; Art. 14, paragraphs 6 and 7). Before Circle 2 exists, the Assembly of Circle 1 decides or, if there is more than one Community, their joint deliberation (Art. 14, para. 10); during the transition Circle 2 is the Basin (Code 16, Art. 1, para. 5).
3. Each Community approves its own values according to that method through its own Assembly, on a proposal from its Accounting Council and its Quality Council, after at least 72 hours of publication and by the consensus method (Code 3, Art. 9, para. 3; Art. 4; Art. 12-bis, para. 9).
4. The Annex indicates version, date, scope, the persons technically responsible and the arrangements for revision, and may not change thresholds, competences or guarantees (Art. 19, paragraphs 4 and 5).
5. The Accounting Council certifies each value with its uncertainty (Art. 9, para. 5). The UCR cost of Storehouse goods (Code 1, Art. 20, para. 6) and the comparison for robots (Code 7, Art. 6-bis, para. 7) can be published only afterwards.
6. The thresholds in UCR are calibrated by the General Assembly within six months of the entry into force of Code 3 and then every year (Code 3, Art. 7, para. 3-ter; Art. 14, paragraphs 2 and 4).
7. Before the final deposit of Code 3, the verification on 8–10 concrete cases is required (Art. 21).
8. Code 3 is active from Phase 0 (Code 16, Art. 3, para. 1, letter b). The Labour Balances are a threshold for passing between phases (Code 16, Art. 4, para. 1, letter e, and para. 2, letter f): this draft is the count made beforehand, those Balances the check made afterwards.

## How to correct it

Anyone who has better data can change the assumption in the program, redo the count and [open an issue](https://github.com/ImTheZeroIt/progetto-dei-cerchi/issues/new), or write to [zerocirclesproject@gmail.com](mailto:zerocirclesproject@gmail.com). The most useful data are measured ones: actual hours of collective kitchens, vegetable gardens, workshops, existing communities.
