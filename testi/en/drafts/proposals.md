# Change proposals (archive)

*Working translation. The Italian text is the authoritative one.*

This page collects the eleven change proposals published between 5 and 19 October 2026. **They were all accepted on 19 October 2026** and their text is now in the Codes; the entries are in the [register of changes](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/changes.html). The page remains as an archive, because for each change it explains the problem, the reason, the cost and the points still open.

**How the rules change now.** From 19 October 2026 changes no longer go through a period of discussion: when a correction has been verified it is applied directly and recorded with the date, the text and the reason. Anyone can report a problem or propose a change, as explained in [How to contribute](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/contributing.html), and any change can be withdrawn if it proves wrong.

The first version of the proposals, of 5 October 2026, had been put through an [adversarial review](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/proposals-review.html): none had held up as it was. Version 2, of 6 October, rewrote them. Proposal 2 was rewritten again on 12 and 19 October (versions 3 and 4). The final version was checked with the [coherence review](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/registro/verifica-coerenza.html) of 19 October (in Italian).

The first four proposals arise from the [draft Annex on the Sufficiency Basket](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/draft-basket.html). The fifth, ninth and tenth concern the Recognition Contribution. The sixth and seventh concern the subsoil and raw materials. The eighth concerns robots. The eleventh concerns the economic thresholds for decisions.

## What applies to all of them

Three things that the first version did not say.

- **What enters the Basket rises in priority.** When time is not enough, the distribution of the goods of the Basket comes before the care of frail persons and the maintenance of water and energy (Code 1, Art. 32). For this reason proposal 1 adds a rule to that Article: the goods of the Basket indicated as deferrable come after. It applies to clothing, to hygiene products that are not needed for health and to the devices of proposal 3. It does not apply to the two values of proposal 4, hot water and heating, which remain in the minimum share.
- **A good of the Basket bought outside is a critical dependency.** It requires an Exit Plan with a definite time limit (Code 15, Art. 12). Deferrable goods do not, however, enter the “levels 1 and 2” on which Code 16 measures self-production of seventy per cent (Art. 1, letter f, and Art. 4): they do not slow down the changes from Phase 0 to Phase 1 and from Phase 1 to Phase 2. They do, however, weigh on the objectives of Phase 3 and on the change to Phase 4, which require overcoming half of the critical dependencies and an Exit Plan with a definite time limit for each of those remaining (Code 16, Art. 3, para. 4, letter c, and Art. 4, para. 4, letter b).
- **Administrative time is not estimated.** Code 3 (Art. 12-bis, para. 13) requires every new rule to state how much administrative time it requires, and limits it to one tenth of the Base Contribution. No proposal has this estimate yet: it is indicated where it weighs most.

## <span id="proposta-1--vestiario-e-igiene-nel-paniere-materiale"></span>Proposal 1 — Clothing and hygiene in the Material Basket

**Outcome:** accepted on 19 October 2026; no comments received. The text has been inserted into the Codes (C.1-Annex A, Art. 4, paras. 1 and 3, and new para. 1-bis; Code 1, Art. 32, new para. 1-bis).

**Text and article:** C.1-Annex A, Art. 4, paragraphs 1 and 3, and new paragraph 1-bis; Code 1, Art. 32, new paragraph 1-bis.

**What changes from version 1.** Added: personal ownership of garments, the limit on single-use products and on materials of animal origin, the confidentiality of withdrawals, the venue (the Textile Storehouse and the Storehouse Council), the priority. Removed “suited to the tasks performed”, which overlapped with protective equipment.

**Problem.** The Material Basket names linen, crockery, furniture and tools, but not clothes or hygiene products. No other Basket names them.

**Proposed text.** In C.1-Annex A, in Article 4, paragraph 1, after letter g), the following are added:

> h) clothing and footwear suited to the climate and the season;
>
> i) personal hygiene products, including menstrual products and those for early childhood and for incontinence.

After paragraph 1 the following is inserted:

> 1-bis. The following rules apply to the goods of letters h) and i):
>
> a) by way of derogation from Article 1, paragraph 4, from delivery they are personal-use goods pursuant to Article 10.3 of the Constitution;
>
> b) Article 4 of Code 2 on the precedence of reuse and Articles 13.5 and 14.1 of the Constitution apply: washable or compostable products are preferred, and single-use products are permitted only under the conditions of Article 14.1;
>
> c) they are procured and distributed through the Textile and Housing Storehouse provided for by Article 14 of Code 1 and are included in the annual Technical Annex of Article 18, paragraph 2, of that Code;
>
> d) data on the withdrawal of the products of letter i) are neither published nor communicated in a form that identifies the person;
>
> e) they are deferrable within the meaning of Article 32, paragraph 1-bis, of Code 1, except for the products that the Health Council indicates as necessary for health; in the order of paragraph 5 they follow letter b).

In paragraph 3, after the words “in days of autonomy and safety margin” the words “; for the goods of letter h) of paragraph 1, as an allocation per person per year” are added. The words “for PPE and for goods intended for frail persons” become “for PPE, for hygiene products and for goods intended for frail persons”. At the end, the following is added: “For letters h) and i) of paragraph 1 the proposal lies with the Storehouse Council.”

In Code 1, in Article 32, after paragraph 1 the following is inserted:

> 1-bis. The goods of the Basket that the Code or the Annex providing for them indicates as deferrable come after letter e) of paragraph 1.

**Reason.** These are goods everyone needs. The Constitution guarantees material subsistence “unconditionally” (Art. 1.3), but lists it as food, water and energy (Art. 1.2): without a rule, clothes and hygiene depend on the Common Fund. The study by Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020) on a decent life counts them among basic needs: 4 kg of new garments per person per year. They are, however, goods that can wait a few days: for this reason they do not go ahead of care and maintenance.

**Other points to align.** Versions: C.1-Annex A from V1.3 to V1.4; Code 1 from V1.5 to V1.6, together with the other proposals on Code 1 that are accepted.

**What it costs.** In the [first balance of hours](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/draft-ucr.html), clothing and hygiene products are worth about 940 hours a year for 150 persons, if bought in Europe: eleven minutes a week per contributor. At the start they are almost all external purchases: critical dependencies, with an Exit Plan. Every product category requires a Technical Validation Sheet (Code 2, Art. 3).

**Open points.** Annex A is activated only in Phase 1 (Code 16, Art. 3): in Phase 0 these goods remain a charge on the Common Fund (Code 15, Art. 17, para. 3). For some products, such as nappies, a washable or compostable alternative exists; for others it has to be verified.

## <span id="proposta-2--standard-dellalloggio-adeguato"></span>Proposal 2 — Standard of adequate dwelling

**Outcome:** accepted on 19 October 2026; no comments received. The text has been inserted into the Codes (Code 1, new Art. 47-bis).

**Text and article:** Code 1, new Art. 47-bis.

**What changes from version 1.** It now says what counts as “adequate” for the other rules and sets a time limit for upgrading. Added: a minimum private space, space for minors from fourteen years of age, the prohibition on falling below the first values, compliance with the prohibition on land take, a rule for the early phases. For temperature it refers to Code 8. Removed the reference to an “emergency accommodation” that the texts do not provide for.

**What changes in version 3 (12 October 2026).** Fifteen square metres per person gave anyone living alone or as a couple a dwelling too small to live in. Now every dwelling has at least a living room, a bedroom and a bathroom, and the minimum floor area depends on how many people live in it: thirty square metres for one, forty-five for two, sixty for three. The private space rises from eight to nine square metres.

**What changes in version 4 (19 October 2026).** The coherence review of 19 October found two defects. Letter d) of paragraph 1 allowed a shared bathroom within Circle 0, while paragraph 2 required a bathroom in every dwelling. And paragraph 2 used “adequate” in a sense different from paragraph 5. Now bathrooms are counted on persons: at least one for every four, which may be shared among the dwellings of the same Circle 0, and beyond the first a WC and washbasin are enough. A studio flat “does not meet the standard”, instead of “is not adequate”. In the first phases the minimum composition and the number of bathrooms also apply.

**Problem.** Housing is a constitutional right (Const. 1.2, letter a). Code 1 speaks of “adequate dwelling” (Art. 47, para. 3) without saying what makes it so.

**Proposed text.** After Article 47 the following is inserted:

> **Art. 47-bis — Adequate dwelling**
>
> 1. The dwelling provided for by Article 1.2, letter a), of the Constitution is adequate when it ensures at least:
>
> a) a private space that can be closed for every person from fourteen years of age; two persons share it only if both request it;
>
> b) protection from cold, heat and damp sufficient not to harm health;
>
> c) access to the Water Basket and to the Energy Basket provided for by Code 8;
>
> d) spaces for cooking and for hygiene, own or shared within Circle 0;
>
> e) the accessibility the person needs, in accordance with his or her individual care plan provided for by Code 12.
>
> 2. The quantitative standards are determined by the Assembly on a joint proposal from the Storehouse Council and the Energy and Water Council, with the opinion of the Health Council and of the Habitat and Territory Council. On first application every dwelling comprises at least a living room and a separate bedroom; a studio flat does not meet the standard. Each Circle 0 has at least one bathroom for every four persons or fraction thereof; beyond the first, bathrooms may be facilities with a WC and washbasin. Bathrooms may be shared among the dwellings of the same Circle 0. When a Circle 0 lives in more than one dwelling, the floor area is calculated on the total number of its persons. The habitable floor area is not less than thirty square metres for one person, forty-five for two and sixty for three; it increases by fifteen square metres for each additional person up to the fifth and by ten for each person beyond the fifth. Each private space provided for by letter a) of paragraph 1 is not less than nine square metres. Subsequent determinations may not fall below the first-application values without the favourable opinion of the Health Council.
>
> 3. The standards of paragraph 2 do not apply to the equipped stopping areas provided for by Article 6-ter, paragraph 6, of Code 11.
>
> 4. A dwelling that does not comply with paragraph 1 or with the standards of paragraph 2 may not for that reason be revoked or refused. The Community includes its upgrading in housing planning, with a time limit not exceeding thirty-six months, which may be extended once only by a reasoned resolution of the Assembly. An appeal to the Guarantee Circle lies against failure to upgrade. As regards temperature, Articles 4 and 10 of Code 8 remain in force.
>
> 5. For the purposes of Article 47, paragraph 3, of this Code and of Articles 29 and 34 of Code 4, a dwelling that complies with paragraph 1 is adequate. If none is available, the best available dwelling is assigned, and its upgrading takes precedence in planning.
>
> 6. Upgrading takes place in compliance with Articles 2 and 5 of Code 10: the reuse and recovery of buildings precede any new construction. Until Codes 8 and 12 are activated, letters a), b) and d) of paragraph 1 and, of paragraph 2, the floor area, the minimum composition and the number of bathrooms apply.

**Reason.** Version 2 used fifteen square metres per person, from the study by Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020): it is a minimum conceived for the whole world population, and for one or two people it falls even below the minimums of the Italian rule on habitability (Ministerial Decree of 5 July 1975: twenty-eight square metres for one person, thirty-eight for two). Version 3 starts from what is needed to live there: a place to be, a place to sleep behind a closed door, a bathroom. The measurements are drafting choices, to be discussed; beyond the fifth person they grow more slowly, because kitchen and living room do not multiply with the number of people. The nine metres of private space are the minimum single bedroom of the same rule. The thirty-six months are a drafting assumption. Paragraph 4 prevents a standard conceived as a guarantee from becoming a ground for eviction; paragraph 5 prevents the protective measures of Code 4 from remaining unenforceable where no compliant dwelling exists. One bathroom for every four persons is the standard of a family home: beyond four persons a single one is not enough at peak times. For bathrooms beyond the first, a WC and washbasin are enough, because the queue is for the WC more than for the shower, and each extra bathroom costs materials, water and maintenance.

**Other points to align.** With the new Article, “adequate dwelling” acquires a content in Art. 47, para. 3, of Code 1 and in Arts. 29 and 34 of Code 4. Minimum temperature is a matter for Code 8, which today sets no value: proposal 4 sets it. Version: Code 1 from V1.5 to V1.6, together with the other proposals on Code 1 that are accepted.

**What it costs.** In Communities that start from existing buildings, upgrading and insulation works, not yet counted in the balance of hours. Where space is lacking and building is not possible, the extension in paragraph 4 is not enough: this has to be said beforehand. With the new measurements the average floor area rises from fifteen to about twenty-two square metres per person, if households are like Italian ones today (assumption): in the [draft UCR Annex](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/draft-ucr.html) the materials for housing go from about 550 to about 740 hours a year for 150 people, less than a tenth of an hour a week per contributor. The extra bathrooms for Circles 0 from the fifth person on are plumbing works, not yet counted.

**Open points.** It is not stated whether the standards apply to the Bridge Houses. Verifying the floor area requires entering dwellings: this has to be done without violating the inviolability of Circle 0 (Const. 5.1). It is not stated how to treat buildings made only of studio flats: joining them requires works that paragraph 4 leaves to planning.

## <span id="proposta-3--connettività-e-materiali-per-apprendere"></span>Proposal 3 — Connectivity and learning materials

**Outcome:** accepted on 19 October 2026; no comments received. The text has been inserted into the Codes (Code 7, Art. 3, new para. 9; Code 5, Art. 2, new para. 4-bis).

**Text and article:** Code 7, Art. 3, new paragraph 9; Code 5, Art. 2, new paragraph 4-bis.

**What changes from version 1.** The personal device is now distinct from the Node, which under the Constitution is offline. The age goes from ten to twelve years, as in Code 5. Devices must comply with the prohibitions of Code 2; if no lawful device exists, shared access is sufficient. Removed the computer for every Nucleus, because every person already has a Node. The materials guaranteed are only those for literacy and safety. The paragraph of Code 7 is numbered 9 and not 6-bis, so as not to be confused with Article 6-bis of proposal 8.

**Problem.** Connectivity is a right (Const. 1.2, letter c), but Code 7 does not say with what it is exercised. For education Code 5 sets the contents (Art. 6, para. 8), not the means.

**Proposed text.** In Code 7, in Article 3, after paragraph 8 the following is added:

> 9. **Minimum allocation.** The access to the Network provided for by paragraph 6 is part of the Sufficiency Basket and may not be made conditional on the CBO or on CdR. It comprises at least:
>
> a) a Network access point in every dwelling that requests it or, where this is not technically possible, in Circle 0;
>
> b) for every member who requests it from twelve years of age, a personal communication device, distinct from the Node of Article 5-bis; before that age, with the participation of the reference figures;
>
> c) in every Circle 1, an assisted access point with shared devices.
>
> Personal devices comply with Article 5 of Code 2; if no compliant device is available, access is ensured by letter c). For shared devices the residual dependency of Article 5, paragraph 2, letter b), of Code 2 is permitted, with a substitution plan. Devices are supplied and repaired through the Storehouses, with the support of the Network Council. The right to disconnection and the non-digital procedures of Article 5-bis, paragraphs 7 and 8, remain in force. The allocations are proposed by the Network Council and are included in the annual Technical Annex of Article 18, paragraph 2, of Code 1. The allocations of letters a) and b) are deferrable within the meaning of Article 32, paragraph 1-bis, of Code 1.

In Code 5, in Article 2, after paragraph 4 the following is inserted:

> 4-bis. The materials, tools and spaces necessary for the literacy provided for by Article 6, paragraph 8, and for the training necessary for safety form part of the Sufficiency Basket: access may not be made conditional on the CBO or on CdR. For other pathways, materials are supplied according to the availability of the Storehouses.

**Reason.** The study by Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020) counts one telephone per person among the needs of a decent life. The personal Node remains what the Constitution wants: an offline computing tool, owned by the person. The communication device is something else, and must be subject to the prohibition of proprietary dependencies.

**Other points to align.** Paragraph 9 refers to Article 32, paragraph 1-bis, of Code 1, which is in proposal 1: if that proposal is not accepted, paragraph 1-bis is inserted with this one. Versions: Code 7 from V1.6 to V1.7, together with proposal 8; Code 5 from V1.3 to V1.4.

**What it costs.** In the balance of hours, telephones are worth from 120 to 260 hours a year for 150 persons, depending on where they are produced. They are components that a Community does not produce: a critical dependency, with an Exit Plan. With the telephones on sale today, compliance with Code 2 could leave only shared access for years: this is an intended limit.

**Open points.** The proposal concerns the federated Network. It does not say whether access to the internet is included, which for Code 2 is an external service: this has to be decided.

## <span id="proposta-4--valore-di-prima-applicazione-del-paniere-energia"></span>Proposal 4 — First-application value of the Energy Basket

**Outcome:** accepted on 19 October 2026; no comments received. The text has been inserted into the Codes (Code 8, Art. 4, para. 4).

**Text and article:** Code 8, Art. 4, paragraph 4.

**What changes from version 1.** The list of services has been removed: it repeated paragraph 1 and forgot part of it (cooling, Health Storehouse, Network node), so that it looked like a step backwards. Only the two new values remain. Temperature is set here and no longer in Code 1. It is stated who decides when the lack of eighteen degrees is an emergency.

**Problem.** The Water Basket has a first-application value (50 litres). The Energy Basket lists the services guaranteed (Art. 4, para. 1) but gives no value: until the Assembly decides, it is not known how much hot water and how much heating are “minimum”.

**Proposed text.** At the end of paragraph 4 the following is added:

> Without prejudice to all the services of paragraph 1, on first application domestic hot water is not less than twenty litres per person per day and minimum winter heating ensures in the inhabited rooms a temperature of not less than eighteen degrees Celsius. On a proposal from the Health Council, the Assembly may set a higher value for age groups. A temperature that the Assembly, on a proposal from the Health Council, sets as dangerous to health constitutes an emergency within the meaning of paragraph 3; until that resolution, the value indicated by the Health Council applies. Article 2, paragraph 2, applies: reducing the requirement precedes increasing production.

**Reason.** The twenty litres of hot water come from the study by Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020); the eighteen degrees from the WHO Housing and health guidelines (2018). The kilowatt-hours depend on the climate and the building: they remain to be calculated in the Energy Basket STV (paragraph 6).

**Other points to align.** Proposal 2 refers to Code 8 for temperature. Version: Code 8 from V1.3 to V1.4.

**What it costs.** In cold climates it is the heaviest item: heating. The two values enter the minimum share, which Code 8 places at the first level of priority (Art. 2, para. 3) and requires to be guaranteed within six months (Art. 10), admitting on a transitional basis even fossil sources for vital functions (Art. 5, para. 3). For this reason the text recalls insulation before production. Where buildings cannot hold eighteen degrees, six months may not be enough: this has to be said beforehand.

**Open points.** The temperature “dangerous to health” has no first-application value: the Health Council proposes it and the Assembly sets it; until then, the value indicated by the Health Council applies.

## <span id="proposta-5--cdr-per-le-attività-culturali-offerte-alla-comunità"></span>Proposal 5 — CdR for cultural activities offered to the Community

**Outcome:** accepted on 19 October 2026; no comments received. The text has been inserted into the Codes (Code 1, Art. 43, new para. 1-bis).

**Text and article:** Code 1, Art. 43, new paragraph 1-bis.

**What changes from version 1.** It has been narrowed. Only activities performed before the Community remain, and only those who perform or lead them accrue CdR. Removed workshops, which are teaching and therefore already Base Contribution (Code 5, Art. 2, para. 3). Removed free research, which remains unrecognised. Removed works, which remain the property of those who make them. Once the activity is validated, only the hours count. The additional recognition by the Assembly is excluded. There is an annual limit. In place of the general prohibition for “affective, spiritual and contemplative” activities there is a more precise exclusion: worship and what is reserved to a part of the members.

**Problem.** The Constitution (Art. 7.2) excludes cultural, artistic and recreational activities from the Base Contribution: no one can be obliged to do them. It does not say whether the Community may recognise them with CdR when they are offered to everyone. Article 43, paragraph 1, speaks of “services for the Community” without naming them.

**Proposed text.** In Article 43, after paragraph 1 the following is inserted:

> 1-bis. The services for the Community referred to in paragraph 1 include cultural, artistic and recreational activities offered in the presence of the Community and open to all its members, such as performances, concerts, readings and celebrations. The following rules apply:
>
> a) CdR accrue to those who perform or lead the activity, not to those who attend or take part in it;
>
> b) the hours performed in presence are counted and, for preparation, no more than the same number again;
>
> c) validation takes place pursuant to Article 9.2 of the Constitution; those who use the service are represented by those who attended. If it is positive, the CdR depend solely on the hours. If it is negative, the suspension provided for by Article 9, paragraph 4, of Code 2 does not apply;
>
> d) paragraph 3 does not apply;
>
> e) the public table sets an annual limit per person; on first application, sixty CdR;
>
> f) activities of worship, activities reserved to a part of the members and time already recognised as CBO pursuant to Article 2, paragraph 3, of Code 5 are excluded.

**Reason.** What a person offers to everyone is a contribution, and Article 8.1 of the Constitution recognises it. But recognition must not become a ranking of artists: for this reason, once the activity is validated, only the hours count, within a limit. Worship stays outside as a choice of secularism: the Community does not decide which faith to recognise (see Const. 25.2).

**Other points to align.** The Constitution does not change. Version: Code 1 from V1.5 to V1.6, together with the other proposals on Code 1 that are accepted.

**What it costs.** Every event becomes a record to be proposed and validated: administrative time, not estimated. The [simulation of the cap](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/draft-cdr.html) assumes that half of the persons do not accrue CdR: with this proposal the assumption has to be revised. CdR also serve to call volunteers in times of need (Code 1, Art. 6, para. 6): the annual limit prevents pleasant activities from weakening that call.

**Open points.** The one-to-one ratio between preparation and presence and the sixty CdR are drafting assumptions. None of the sixteen Competence Circles concerns the arts: it remains to be said which Council proposes the award, who brings the relevant competence to the validation and who distinguishes a cultural activity from one of worship.

## <span id="proposta-6--sottosuolo-bene-comune"></span>Proposal 6 — Subsoil as a common good

**Outcome:** accepted on 19 October 2026; no comments received. The text has been inserted into the Codes (Code 10, Art. 1, paras. 1, 2 and 3; Art. 9, para. 1, letter a)).

**Text and article:** Code 10, Art. 1, paragraphs 1, 2 and 3; Art. 9, paragraph 1, letter a).

**What changes from version 1.** The first version amended the Constitution in two places. Both amendments are withdrawn.

- **Art. 3.1.** Article 37.4 removes the regime of the Commons from “any revision procedure”, without distinguishing between extending and reducing. There is no need to touch it: Article 3.1 itself already includes “every other good classified as common by the Constitution or by the Federative Codes”. The subsoil is classified in Code 10.
- **Art. 14.2.** The exception that admitted extraction “on an exceptional and temporary basis” is not proposed again. The review showed that it contradicted the environmental non-regression written in the same paragraph, that it left “the site” without limits and that it clashed with the prohibition of landfill (Art. 14.5). The question remains open: see at the end.

**Problem.** Article 3.1 of the Constitution lists among the Commons land, water, air and energy sources. No text names the subsoil and deposits.

**Proposed text.** In Article 1, paragraph 1, the following letter is added:

> h) the subsoil, deposits and the inventory of recoverable materials.

In Article 1, paragraph 2, the following letters are added:

> f) **mineral resources:** means metals, minerals and rocks in their natural state, underground or outcropping, and those contained in landfills and in waste deposits from past extraction, until they are recovered; they do not include water, which is governed by Code 8;
>
> g) **deposit:** means a concentration of mineral resources that has been ascertained and entered in the Land Register.

In Article 1, paragraph 3, the following letter is added:

> f) the subsoil and mineral resources. This qualification does not restrict the meaning of the words “land” and “soil” used by the Constitution.

In Article 9, paragraph 1, letter a) becomes:

> a) generate rent from common fertile land, from the subsoil, from mineral resources, from ecological corridors or from buffer zones;

**Reason.** Whoever lives above a deposit does not own it and cannot draw rent from it: this is the rule that already applies to fertile land. Writing it now, before anyone needs it, costs little and avoids a dispute.

**Other points to align.** Letter h) of Article 1, paragraph 1, names the inventory, which is governed by Article 8-bis of proposal 7: if that proposal is not accepted, the words “and the inventory of recoverable materials” are not inserted. Version: Code 10 from V1.3 to V1.4, together with proposal 7. The Constitution remains at V.29.

**What it costs.** Nothing in hours.

**Open point: extraction.** Article 14.2 of the Constitution prohibits “any activity that causes irreversible damage” to soil and water. A new quarry or mine almost always causes it. But panels, networks and tools are made of metals: whoever does not extract buys from whoever extracts. The Confederation will have to decide whether to keep the prohibition or admit an exception. It cannot do so now, for three reasons that emerged from the review: until the close of Phase 2, acts that exceed the ordinary maintenance of territorial goods are ineffective (Code 16, Art. 6, para. 4); the sanctuaries and the Biodiversity Council exist only from Phase 2; as long as no Circle 2 exists, the functions of the General Assembly are exercised by the Assembly of the Community itself (Code 3, Art. 14, para. 10). A future proposal will have to answer all the objections listed in the review.

## <span id="proposta-7--materie-prime-recupero-e-inventario"></span>Proposal 7 — Raw materials: recovery and inventory

**Outcome:** accepted on 19 October 2026; no comments received. The text has been inserted into the Codes (Code 10, new Art. 8-bis and Arts. 3 and 7; Code 15, Art. 12, new para. 5-bis).

**Text and article:** Code 10, new Art. 8-bis; Code 15, Art. 12, new paragraph 5-bis.

**What changes from version 1.** The part on extraction is withdrawn, together with the constitutional exception of proposal 6. What remains is what applies in any case: the inventory of what can be recovered, the custody of deposits, the guarantees for recovery excavations. The Article states expressly that it does not authorise new extraction. The rules on reuse and purchases are not repeated: they already exist in Code 2, in Code 15 and in Annex A. The limit on Exit Plans is written in Code 15, which is where it belongs.

**Problem.** No Code says where, in the territory, the materials that can be recovered are located, nor who has custody of a deposit. And the excavation of an old landfill or of a deposit of mining waste can pollute as much as a mine, but today it has no guarantees of its own.

**Proposed text.** In Code 10, after Article 8 the following is inserted:

> **Art. 8-bis — Raw materials: recovery and inventory**
>
> 1. The Habitat and Territory Council keeps in the Land Register, and updates every year, the inventory of recoverable materials present in the territory: disused buildings, vehicles and installations, landfills, waste from past extraction and materials deriving from the conversion provided for by Article 16.2 of the Constitution. The inventory is made available to the Production Council for the purposes of Articles 2 and 5 of C.1-Annex A.
>
> 2. Deposits are Commons entrusted to the custody of the Community in whose territory they are located. Custody gives no entitlement to rent.
>
> 3. The excavation of a landfill or of a deposit of waste from past extraction, in order to recover its materials, requires jointly, without prejudice to the matrix of Article 7 of Code 3:
>
> a) the mandatory opinions of the Habitat and Territory, Biodiversity, Energy and Water and Health Councils;
>
> b) a General Safety Sheet pursuant to Code 14;
>
> c) a restoration plan approved before the start, whose hours are counted together with those of the recovery, and a plan for the non-recoverable part in conformity with Article 14.5 of the Constitution;
>
> d) the resolution of the Assembly of the Community in whose territory the site is located; if the excavation may affect waters or ecological corridors common to several Communities, the General Assembly decides.
>
> 4. The excavation of paragraph 3 is prohibited in ecological sanctuaries and in areas that are candidates to become such, in wetlands and in strategic infiltration areas. It is not permitted before Codes 13 and 14 are activated. Excavation in conformity with paragraph 3 is use in conformity with a Sheet in force for the purposes of Article 6, paragraph 4, of Code 16. This Code and Code 13 remain in force.
>
> 5. This Article does not authorise new extraction or the extension of existing extraction. Article 14.2 of the Constitution remains in force.

In Code 15, in Article 12, after paragraph 5 the following is inserted:

> 5-bis. For raw materials, the Exit Plan indicates reduction of the requirement, reuse, recycling and recovery. It may not indicate new extraction. Paragraph 5 applies.

**Reason.** Before asking whether to dig, one needs to know what is already on the surface: disused buildings, vehicles, landfills. It is useful work in any case, and it touches no prohibition. Paragraph 5-bis closes a door that the review found open: without it, the obligation to exit external dependencies would push towards the mine.

**Other points to align.** In Code 10: the inventory has to be added to the tasks of the Habitat and Territory Council (Art. 3) and to the contents of the Land Register (Art. 7, para. 1), and the Production Council has to be added to those who may consult it (Art. 7, para. 2). Versions: Code 10 from V1.3 to V1.4, together with proposal 6; Code 15 from V1.3 to V1.4.

**Text of the alignments** (added on 10 October 2026). In Code 10, in Article 3, paragraph 3, after letter g) the following is added:

> h) keep the inventory of recoverable materials provided for by Article 8-bis.

In Article 7, paragraph 1, the words “pollinator refuges and agricultural water infrastructure” become “pollinator refuges, agricultural water infrastructure, deposits and recoverable materials”. In Article 7, paragraph 2, letter b), after the words “Agroecological and Biodiversity Councils” the words “and, for the inventory of recoverable materials, to the Production Council,” are inserted.

**What it costs.** The annual inventory is new work for every Habitat and Territory Council: administrative time, not estimated. It partly overlaps with the inventory of degraded soils (Art. 5, para. 4) and can be kept together with it.

**Open points.** Paragraph 5-bis leaves a dependency without a definite time limit: the metals that recycling does not cover will continue to be bought outside, and the same Article 12 requires a time limit. It is a real contradiction between the prohibition on extracting and the obligation to exit dependencies: this proposal makes it visible, it does not resolve it. The reasoned review of paragraph 5 prevents the transition from being blocked, but it does not give a time limit.

## <span id="proposta-8--robot-e-macchine-autonome"></span>Proposal 8 — Robots and autonomous machines

**Outcome:** accepted on 19 October 2026; no comments received. The text has been inserted into the Codes (Code 7, Art. 1, para. 2, new letter f), and new Art. 6-bis; Code 1, Art. 8, para. 3).

**Text and article:** Code 7, Art. 1, paragraph 2, new letter f); new Art. 6-bis.

**What changes from version 1.** The definition is narrower: the first one also included a washing machine. Devices for individual use remain personal. The prohibitions on arming and on monitoring apply to every machine, even a remote-controlled one, and respect the limits of Article 18 of the Constitution. The stop brings the machine into a safe state, it does not halt it abruptly. The time saved follows the rule that already exists: the first version contradicted it. Removed the paragraphs that repeated rules in force. Added the consequences of a violation and a time limit for bringing existing machines into conformity. Removed the reference to work in mines.

**Problem.** The Constitution regulates AI (Arts. 19–23) and Code 7 the automation of tasks (Art. 6). No text names machines that move and act by themselves among people: missing are the stop independent of the software, the limits near people and the prohibition on arming them.

**Proposed text.** In Article 1, paragraph 2, the following letter is added:

> f) **robot:** means a machine that moves or manipulates objects in a space where persons are or may be present, choosing its trajectory or action by itself. Fixed-cycle appliances and protected fixed installations are not robots.

After Article 6 the following is inserted:

> **Art. 6-bis — Robots and autonomous machines**
>
> 1. Article 6 and the provisions of this Article apply to robots.
>
> 2. Robots used in the CBO or in production for the Storehouses are means of production and Commons within the meaning of Article 10.1 of the Constitution: they may not be the object of private ownership or a source of rent. The Circle that has custody of them owns the hardware and holds the schematics and a verifiable copy of the code, pursuant to Articles 19.3 and 19.4 of the Constitution. Aids and devices for exclusively individual use remain personal-use goods within the meaning of Article 10.3 of the Constitution.
>
> 3. Every robot has a physical stop independent of the software, which brings it into a safe state. The stop is accessible in accordance with the General Safety Sheet of Code 14; anyone may activate it in the cases of Article 2, paragraph 6, of that Code. The robot operates under the supervision of a qualified person on shift.
>
> 4. The limits of force, speed and distance from persons are set by the Machinery STV of Code 14, after hearing the Network Council. In case of doubt, physical separation between the machine and persons prevails. Until Code 14 is activated, the stop and the limits are set in the Safety Sheet of Article 27 of Code 1.
>
> 5. It is prohibited:
>
> a) to arm a machine or use it to coerce, restrain or strike a person, pursuant to Article 16 of the Constitution and Article 2, paragraph 6, of this Code;
>
> b) to use it to monitor or profile persons, or to follow them without their consent. The recognition of the qualified operator, the anonymous detection of presence for safety purposes and the search for persons in danger remain permitted, by non-biometric means and within the limits of Article 18 of the Constitution. Article 8-quinquies of Code 14 and Article 33 of Code 4 remain in force;
>
> c) to entrust to a robot the activities excluded by Article 6, paragraph 2; Article 1, paragraph 4, remains in force;
>
> d) to make a vital function dependent on a robot without a documented and tested substitute manual procedure.
>
> The prohibitions of letters a) and b) apply to every machine, including one controlled remotely.
>
> 6. Where a robot available in the Community can safely perform a task that Article 8, paragraph 1, of Code 1 classifies by risk, no person may be assigned to it, by any means, save for periodic tests of the manual procedure and cases of breakdown.
>
> 7. Before a robot is introduced, the hours needed to build it, power it, maintain it, train those who use it and dispose of it are compared, in UCR, with the hours of work it saves. The comparison is calculated in accordance with Articles 5 and 9 of Code 3 and certified by the Accounting Council. The time saved is allocated in accordance with Article 6, paragraph 6, and may not translate into an advantage for some.
>
> 8. The system that governs a robot is an automation within the meaning of Article 1, paragraph 2, letter a). If it uses an AI, the AI is local and limited in domain within the meaning of Article 19.5 of the Constitution and is validated by the Network Council. In no case does it adopt the decisions reserved to persons by Article 2, paragraph 8.
>
> 9. A robot that does not comply with paragraphs 3, 4 and 5 is suspended or deactivated pursuant to Article 9, paragraphs 2 and 3. The qualifications of Articles 16 and 18.6 of the Constitution remain in force. Robots already in use shall be brought into conformity within twelve months of the entry into force of this Article.

**Reason.** A machine that acts by itself in physical space must be able to stop without going through the software, stay away from people when in doubt, and never become an instrument of force or control. Robots that work for everyone belong to everyone, like every means of production. The time they free up stays with the Community, which decides how to use it: this is the rule the Code already has (Art. 6, para. 6).

**Other points to align.** Paragraph 6 derogates, for risky tasks, from the assignment of Article 8, paragraph 3, of Code 1, which has to be aligned with a cross-reference. Version: Code 7 from V1.6 to V1.7, together with proposal 3.

**Text of the alignment** (added on 10 October 2026). In Code 1, in Article 8, paragraph 3, the words “Unpleasant, arduous or night tasks shall be assigned” become “Without prejudice to Article 6-bis, paragraph 6, of Code 7, unpleasant, arduous or night tasks shall be assigned”.

**What it costs.** For every type of robot a Technical Sheet; for every activity a Safety Sheet; for every machine a certified comparison in hours. It is administrative time, not estimated, and the balance of hours already puts civic functions at the limit. Robots contain metals and components that the Confederation does not produce: dependencies to be registered (Code 15, Art. 12).

**Open points.** Code 7 knows two types of AI, personal and management: the one that guides a robot is neither, and paragraph 8 gives it only a minimal rule. Robots that assist frail persons, such as a hoist, fall within the definition: it has to be verified that paragraphs 3 and 5 are enough.

## <span id="proposta-9--decrescenza-dei-cdr-quando-comincia-e-quali-si-impiegano-per-primi"></span>Proposal 9 — Decay of the CdR: when it begins and which are used first

**Outcome:** accepted on 19 October 2026; no comments received. The text has been inserted into the Codes (Code 1, Art. 44, para. 3; Art. 45, paras. 2 and 3).

**Text and article:** Code 1, Art. 44, paragraph 3; Art. 45, paragraphs 2 and 3.

**What changes from version 1.** The first version made the value fall all at once at the end of each quarter and presented it as “the only reading” possible. It was not: it was the most generous one. Now the reduction is gradual, in proportion to time. Added two rules that were missing: how much a reduced CdR is worth and what is meant by accrual. The order of use has been moved to the Article on use.

**Problem.** The paragraph says that CdR retain their value for twelve months, then “they are reduced by one quarter of the initial value for each quarter and are extinguished at the end of the twenty-fourth month”. It does not say how the value falls. There are three possible readings.

| Reading | Full value for | Extinction | Hours a week to reach the cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gradual: from the twelfth month it falls in proportion to time** | **12 months** | **24th month** | **3.1** |
| The whole quarter at the end of each quarter (version 1) | 15 months | 24th month | 2.8 |
| The whole quarter at the start of each quarter | 12 months | 21st month | 3.4 |

Only the first respects both the twelve months and the twenty-fourth. Moreover, no Article says which CdR are used first, nor how much a reduced CdR is worth when it is used.

**Proposed text.** In Article 44, paragraph 3 is replaced by the following:

> 3. Unused CdR retain their value for twelve months from accrual. From the completion of the twelfth month the value is reduced in proportion to time, by one twelfth of the initial value per month, until it is extinguished at the completion of the twenty-fourth month. Accrual coincides with the day on which the activity was performed. For the purposes of use and of the cap, each CdR counts for the value it has at that moment.

In Article 45, paragraph 2, the words “one CdR corresponds to one UCR” become “one CdR at full value corresponds to one UCR”. At the end of paragraph 3 the following is added:

> The CdR with the earliest accrual are used first.

**Reason.** One quarter per quarter is one twelfth per month: gradual reduction says the same thing as the text in force, without steps. Using the oldest CdR first is the rule most favourable to the person.

**Other points to align.** The [public CdR table](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/draft-cdr.html) and the simulation have been redone with the gradual curve. Version: Code 1 from V1.5 to V1.6, together with the other proposals on Code 1 that are accepted.

**What it costs.** Compared with the reading used so far, at steady state 18 months of accrual are retained instead of 19.5: the cap is reached with 3.1 hours a week beyond the Base Contribution instead of 2.8. No effect on necessary work.

## <span id="proposta-10--la-maggiorazione-dei-compiti-sgradevoli-per-chi-è-al-tetto"></span>Proposal 10 — The supplement for unpleasant tasks for those at the cap

**Outcome:** accepted on 19 October 2026; no comments received. The text has been inserted into the Codes (Code 1, Art. 44, para. 2).

**Text and article:** Code 1, Art. 44, paragraph 2.

**What changes from version 1.** The solution is reversed. The first version awarded the supplement also to those at the cap, extinguishing their oldest CdR for an equal value. The review showed that it did not achieve what it claimed: the balance remained identical, the advantage was only a longer duration of the CdR, the quarterly data were inflated and the disparity moved elsewhere. Now the proposal confirms the rule in force, writes it expressly and defines how the balance is measured.

**Problem.** The paragraph says that, once the cap has been reached, “further activities do not give rise to CdR”. It does not say whether this also applies to the supplement for unpleasant tasks performed within the Base Contribution, which are not “further activities”. Nor does it say how the balance is measured, now that CdR lose value over time.

**Proposed text.** In paragraph 2, the third sentence is replaced by the following:

> The balance is the sum of the current values of the unused CdR, verified at the moment of the award. CdR are awarded up to the cap; beyond it no further CdR are awarded, including the supplements of Article 10 and of Article 43, paragraph 2.

**Reason.** The Constitution wants a personal cap (Art. 8.2): whoever has reached it cannot receive more without losing something. Every substitution mechanism complicates the register without changing the balance. Whoever is at the cap already has the maximum priority the system allows.

**Other points to align.** The public table and the simulation already apply this rule. Version: Code 1 from V1.5 to V1.6, together with the other proposals on Code 1 that are accepted.

**What it costs.** Whoever is at the cap and is drawn by lot for an unpleasant shift does not receive the supplement. It is the price of a cap without exceptions, and it has to be said. The sortition excludes for one quarter whoever has performed the most unpleasant hours (Art. 8, para. 3), but not whoever is at the cap for other activities. Whoever uses their own CdR goes back below the cap and starts accruing them again.

## <span id="proposta-11--soglie-economiche-in-proporzione-alla-dimensione-della-comunità"></span>Proposal 11 — Economic thresholds in proportion to the size of the Community

**Outcome:** accepted on 19 October 2026; no comments received. The text has been inserted into the Codes (Code 3, Art. 7, para. 3 and new paras. 3-bis and 3-ter; Art. 7-bis, para. 1; Art. 21, para. 3).

**Text and article:** Code 3, Art. 7, paragraph 3 and new paragraphs 3-bis and 3-ter; Art. 7-bis, paragraph 1.

**What changes from version 1.** The proportion is no longer pure. A pump or a vehicle costs the same in a Community of thirty persons and in one of five hundred: with fully proportional thresholds, in small Communities almost every purchase would have ended up in the Assembly. Now each threshold has a fixed part and a per-person part. Added: the guarantees that medium- and high-risk operations have today, which are not lost when the threshold falls; who is counted and when; newly formed Communities; operations common to several Communities; the test cases.

**Problem.** The matrix of Article 7 uses two fixed thresholds: 500 and 2,500 UCR, that is, hours of work. But a Base Community ranges from thirty to five hundred persons (Const. 5.1), and the same threshold weighs very differently. In the first balance of hours a Community works about 238 hours a year per inhabitant.

| Persons | 500 UCR are | 2,500 UCR are |
|---|---|---|
| 500 | 0.4% of a year’s work | 2.1% |
| 150 | 1.4% | 7% |
| 30 | 7% | 35% |

In a Community of thirty persons, a low-risk operation that commits one third of a year’s work can be decided without the Assembly.

**Proposed text.** In the matrix of paragraph 3 the row headings become:

> **Low: below the low threshold** — **Medium: from the low threshold to the high threshold inclusive** — **High: above the high threshold**

After paragraph 3 the following are inserted:

> 3-bis. The low threshold is equal to 100 UCR plus one UCR for each person belonging to the Base Community, and does not exceed 500 UCR. The high threshold is equal to 500 UCR plus five UCR for each person, and does not exceed 2,500 UCR. All persons belonging to the Community at the beginning of the quarter are counted, minors included; guests are not counted. For a newly constituted Community the number in the act of accession applies. For operations common to several Communities and for the bodies of the higher Circles, the persons of the Communities concerned are added together.
>
> 3-ter. The General Assembly calibrates the values of paragraph 3-bis pursuant to Article 14, paragraph 2, letter b). The calibration may not eliminate the proportion to the number of persons. Paragraphs 2 and 4 of Article 7-ter apply in any event to medium-risk operations with a value above the high threshold and not exceeding 2,500 UCR. In Article 3, paragraph 2, of this Code and in Articles 15 and 23 of Code 2, “the threshold” means the high threshold.

In Article 7-bis, paragraph 1 is replaced by the following:

> 1. The special procedure applies to operations of low value and high or critical risk. Paragraphs 2 to 5 apply in any event to high- or critical-risk operations with a value below 500 UCR.

**Reason.** Thresholds serve to decide who resolves. They must grow with the Community, but not start from zero. With these values the largest Community remains as it is today and the others go down.

| Persons | Low threshold | High threshold | Of a year’s work |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 130 UCR | 650 UCR | 1.8% and 9.1% |
| 150 | 250 UCR | 1,250 UCR | 0.7% and 3.5% |
| 400 and over | 500 UCR | 2,500 UCR | 0.5% and 2.6% or less |

**Test cases.** Article 21 of Code 3 requires the matrix to be tested on eight to ten concrete cases. These are seven: the test is partial. “CC” is the Competence Circle, “CQ” the Quality Council.

| Operation | Today, in every Community | With the proposal: 30 persons | 150 persons | 500 persons |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 UCR, low risk | CC | CC | CC | CC |
| 200 UCR, high risk: a pump to be replaced urgently | special procedure, Assembly within 48 hours | Assembly within 48 hours, with the same guarantees | special procedure, Assembly within 48 hours | special procedure, Assembly within 48 hours |
| 400 UCR, low risk | CC | CQ and CC | CQ and CC | CC |
| 700 UCR, high risk | Assembly | Assembly with special guarantees | Assembly | Assembly |
| 900 UCR, medium risk | reinforced procedure | Assembly, with binding opinion and audit | reinforced procedure | reinforced procedure |
| 2,000 UCR, low risk | CQ and CC | Assembly | Assembly | CQ and CC |
| 3,000 UCR, high risk | Assembly with special guarantees | unchanged | unchanged | unchanged |

No case goes down a level and none loses the guarantees it has today. Only operations that are large relative to the Community go up a level. Missing are the cases with a negative opinion, a conflict of interest and an appeal.

**Other points to align.** The rules against splitting (Art. 11) and on the calculation of value (Art. 5) do not change. In Article 21, paragraph 3, “below threshold” becomes “of low value”. Code 15 (Arts. 17 and 18) applies the matrix to the Fund of every Circle: for this reason paragraph 3-bis says how persons are counted above Circle 1. Version: Code 3 from V1.4 to V1.5.

**What it costs.** In Communities below four hundred persons more decisions go up a level: more work for the Quality Council and more items on the Assembly’s agenda. How many is not calculated. The fixed parts and the coefficients are drafting assumptions.

**Open points.** Code 1 (Art. 40, para. 4) requires a comparative report for purchases “above the thresholds established by Code 3”: already today it does not say which of the two, and this remains to be decided. Recurring supplies are added together, under the rules against splitting: a year’s medicines for thirty persons are worth about 176 UCR and fall within medium value. It remains to be said whether a supply already approved with the quarterly requirement has to go through the matrix again. The thresholds in UCR depend on the calculation method of the UCR Technical Annex, which is still a draft.

## What happens next

If by 20 October 2026 no objection shows that a proposal contradicts other Articles, worsens a test case or costs more than the system can sustain, the proposal enters the Code concerned, which changes version, and is recorded in the [change log](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/changes.html) with its reason. If no one replies, the register will say so: “no comments received”, not “approved after discussion”. Proposals that are rejected or withdrawn remain on this page, with the reason.
