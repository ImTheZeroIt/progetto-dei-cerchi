# Annual Technical Annex of the Sufficiency Basket

*Working translation. The Italian text is the authoritative one.*

**Calibration draft 0.1, not adopted.** Code 1 (Art. 18, para. 2) states that the content of the Basket is defined each year in a Technical Annex, approved by the Assembly on a joint proposal from the Health Council and the Storehouse Council. That annex has never been written. This page proposes a first version: it is meant for discussion and for doing the sums, and binds no one. The quantities will become rules only when a Community approves them through the prescribed procedure.

## How to read it

- **Planning quantities, not rations.** The figures say how much the Storehouse must have available for each person. Withdrawal remains free, without charge and according to actual need (Code 1, Art. 18, para. 1, letter a).
- **Reference person:** one adult. For children, pregnancy, elderly people and illness the quantities are adapted by the Health Council (Code 1, Art. 18, para. 3).
- **Origin:** an external source, where there is one; otherwise “drafting choice”, that is, a figure set in order to be able to do the sums, without data to support it.
- **A model to adapt.** Climate, soil and buildings change the quantities. Each Community starts from here and corrects.

The Sufficiency Basket is made of seven parts that the Codes already name: Food, Water, Energy, Material, Health, Care, Logistics. To these are added the rights of Article 1.2 of the Constitution that no Basket yet quantifies: housing, education, connectivity, personal AI Node.

## 1. Food Basket

Seat: Code 9, Art. 4. The Basket is guaranteed on a plant basis. Animal products collected without harm are not planned into requirements (Code 9, Art. 6-bis, para. 4, letter d).

The starting point is the reference diet of the EAT-Lancet Commission (2019), calculated for 2,500 kcal a day. That diet allows animal foods to be zero. Here their calories are replaced by cereals, legumes and nuts, staying within the ranges the Commission indicates.

| Item | Per person per day | Per person per year | Reference diet | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cereals, preferably wholegrain (dry weight) | 275 g | 100 kg | 232 g | Drafting adaptation |
| Legumes (dry weight) | 100 g | 36.5 kg | 75 g (0–100) | Upper limit of the range |
| Nuts and oilseeds | 75 g | 27 kg | 50 g (0–75) | Upper limit of the range |
| Vegetables | 300 g | 110 kg | 300 g (200–600) | EAT-Lancet |
| Fruit | 200 g | 73 kg | 200 g (100–300) | EAT-Lancet |
| Potatoes and other tubers | 50 g | 18 kg | 50 g (0–100) | EAT-Lancet |
| Unsaturated vegetable oils | 40 g | 14.6 kg | 40 g (20–80) | EAT-Lancet |
| Added sugars | no more than 31 g | no more than 11 kg | 31 g | EAT-Lancet |
| Iodised salt | no more than 5 g | no more than 1.8 kg | — | WHO |
| Vitamin B12 (supplement) | 4 micrograms | — | — | German Nutrition Society |

The total is about 2,500 kcal a day. For comparison: the minimum value used in emergencies by UNHCR and WFP is 2,100 kcal; the Millward-Hopkins study on decent living uses 2,000–2,150 kcal as the average for a whole population, children included.

**Three caveats.**

1. **Vitamin B12 is not optional.** In a diet without animal foods it must always be supplemented: the German Nutrition Society states that this is necessary. It is a good that the Community cannot produce at the outset: it must be entered in the Dependency Register of Code 15.
2. **The table must be checked by people who work in nutrition.** In particular for protein, iron, calcium, zinc, iodine, omega-3 and vitamin D, and for children, pregnancy and breastfeeding.
3. **Foods for weaning and for frail persons** (Code 9, Art. 4, para. 1) are not quantified here: the Health Council defines them.

**Stocks.**

| Where | Quantity | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| Care Nucleus | 72 hours of vital goods | Code 1, Art. 39, para. 2 |
| Storehouse: cereals, legumes, oil, nuts | requirements until the next harvest, plus three months | Drafting choice |
| Storehouse: other foods | thirty days | Drafting choice |

**Land.** A study on the United States (Peters et al., 2016) estimates for vegetarian diets, including the vegan one, 0.13–0.14 hectares of agricultural land per person per year. It is an order of magnitude: it depends on yields, climate and water. With that value a Community of 150 people would need about 20 cultivated hectares, one of 500 about 65–70.

## 2. Water Basket

Seat: Code 8, Art. 4-bis.

| Item | Per person per day | Per person per year | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drinking water for drinking, cooking, personal and domestic hygiene | at least 50 litres | about 18 m³ | Code 8, Art. 4-bis, para. 3. For the WHO 50 litres is intermediate access, at low risk to health; 100 litres is optimal access. |
| of which hot water | 20 litres | about 7 m³ | Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020) |

A Community of 150 people needs at least 7.5 m³ a day for personal uses. Water for health and care services and agricultural water are counted separately.

## 3. Energy Basket

Seat: Code 8, Art. 4. The Code lists the services that the minimum share must cover, but does not set a value. The draft proposes defining the Basket by services, and calculating the kilowatt-hours afterwards, climate by climate and building by building.

| Service (Code 8, Art. 4, para. 1) | Proposed level | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| Preserving food | one fridge-freezer per Nucleus | Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020) |
| Cooking food | one hob per Nucleus | Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020) |
| Domestic hot water | 20 litres per person per day | Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020) |
| Basic lighting | all inhabited rooms, for the hours of darkness in which they are used | Drafting choice |
| Recharging essential devices | telephone, computer, AI Node, health aids | Drafting choice |
| Minimum heating | at least 18 °C in inhabited rooms; more for elderly people, children and the sick | WHO, Housing and health guidelines (2018) |
| Cooling | as much as is needed to protect health in heatwaves | To be defined with the Health Council |
| Health Storehouse and Network node | continuity without interruptions | Code 8, Art. 4, para. 1, letter g |

**Benchmark.** The Millward-Hopkins study estimates that a decent life requires on average 15.3 gigajoules of final energy per person per year (from 13 to 18.4 depending on the country): about 4,250 kWh a year, that is 11.6 kWh a day. The value includes everything: home, food, transport, health care, school, production of goods. The domestic share of the Energy Basket is only one part of it, still to be calculated.

## 4. Housing

Seat: Const. 1.2, letter a). Housing is a right, but no Code sets its standard.

| Item | Proposed level | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| Floor area | 15 m² per person, common spaces included | Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020) |
| Indoor temperature | at least 18 °C in the cold season | WHO (2018) |
| Privacy | a lockable space for each adult person or couple | Drafting choice |
| Emergency minimum | 3.5 m² of covered space per person | Sphere standards. It applies only to emergency reception, not as a dwelling. |

## 6. Health Basket

Seat: Code 6, Art. 10. It comprises life-saving goods.

| Item | Proposed reference | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| Medicines | the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines, adapted by the Health Council | WHO, 24th list |
| Staff | 4.45 doctors, nurses and midwives per 1,000 people | WHO, health workforce strategy to 2030 |

**Consequence.** With that threshold a Community of 150 people does not reach one full-time health worker. Health care must be organised in the Basin: three Communities of 500 people require six or seven. This is consistent with the district hospital that the Constitution entrusts to Circle 2 (Art. 5.1).

## 7. Care Basket

## 11. What is missing to close the account

This draft says what is needed. It does not yet say how much labour it costs. This is the step on which the fifteen-hour limit of the Base Contribution depends (Code 1, Art. 3).

| What must be calculated | With what |
|---|---|
| Hours of labour to produce each item of the Basket, in a typical Community | First calculation in the [draft UCR Annex](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/draft-ucr.html): between 9 and 13 hours a week, manufactured goods included |
| Land and yields for the Food Basket, by climate | Local agronomic data; Agroecological Council |
| Kilowatt-hours of the Energy Basket, by climate and building | Energy Basket STV (Code 8, Art. 4, para. 6) |
| Nutritional adequacy of the food table | Check by a nutritionist |
| List of goods that the Community cannot produce | Dependency Register (Code 15, Art. 12) |

## 12. Gaps in the texts found while writing the draft

They are not corrected here. For each one there is an [open amendment proposal](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/proposals.html), with the text of the new paragraphs: it remains under discussion at least until 20 October 2026.

1. **Clothing and hygiene products** do not appear in any Basket. The Material Basket names linen, not clothes.
2. **Housing** is a constitutional right, but no Code sets its floor area, temperature or privacy.
3. **Connectivity** is a right without quantities. For education Code 5 sets the content, but does not say that materials and spaces are part of the Basket.
4. **The Energy Basket** has a list of services, but no first-application value, unlike the Water Basket.

## How it is corrected

The Annex is reviewed every year. Anyone who has data, an experience or a criticism can [open an issue](https://github.com/ImTheZeroIt/progetto-dei-cerchi/issues/new) or write to [zerocirclesproject@gmail.com](mailto:zerocirclesproject@gmail.com). The figures on this page are also collected in the [register of parameters to be verified](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/parameters.html).
