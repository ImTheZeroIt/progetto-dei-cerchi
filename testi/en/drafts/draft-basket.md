# Annual Technical Annex of the Sufficiency Basket

*Working translation. The Italian text is the authoritative one.*

**Calibration draft 0.2, not adopted.** Code 1 (Art. 18, para. 2) states that the content of the Basket is defined each year in a Technical Annex, approved by the Assembly on a joint proposal from the Health Council and the Storehouse Council. The proposal brings together the lists prepared by the sectoral Councils, including the Health Basket (Code 6, Art. 6, para. 1) and the Material Basket (C.1-Annex A, Art. 4); the lists take effect only when the Assembly approves them, and the proposal is published and may be challenged. That annex has never been written. This page proposes a first version: it is meant for discussion and for doing the sums, and binds no one. The quantities will become rules only when a Community approves them through the prescribed procedure, described at the end. Version 0.2 (27 October 2026) brings the draft into line with the rules in force on that date.

## How to read it

- **Planning quantities, not rations.** The figures say how much the Storehouse must have available for each person. Withdrawal remains free, without charge and according to actual need (Code 1, Art. 18, para. 1, letter a).
- **Whom to plan for.** For residents, plus guests: up to 15% of residents, who receive the Basket from the Storehouse that hosts them (Code 11, Art. 6-ter, paragraphs 5 and 8). Drinking water and the energy essential to health are due to anyone who is in the territory, member or not (Code 8, Art. 4-bis, para. 2, and Art. 4, para. 2).
- **Reference person:** one adult. For children, pregnancy, elderly people and illness, individual needs are assessed by the Health Council (Code 1, Art. 18, para. 3).
- **Origin:** the Code, where it sets a value; an external source, where there is one; otherwise “drafting choice”, that is, a figure set in order to be able to do the sums, without data to support it.
- **Level 1–2 or deferrable.** Each item states whether it belongs to the Basket of level 1–2 or is deferrable (Code 1, Art. 32, paragraphs 1-bis and 1-quinquies). When resources are not enough, deferrable goods come after the maintenance of critical infrastructure. The distinction is also used to verify the thresholds for passing between phases (Code 16, Art. 4, para. 1, letters a and e; para. 2, letter a) and the equivalence at the boundary of associated Communities (Code 15, Art. 21-bis, para. 2, letter a). Where the text does not decide, the column says “proposal”.
- **A model to adapt.** Climate, soil and buildings change the quantities. Each Community starts from here and corrects. The provisions “per Nucleus” come from a study that reasons on households of four people (Millward-Hopkins et al., 2020), while a Care Nucleus can have up to fifteen (Const. 5.1): for large Nuclei they must be adapted.

The Sufficiency Basket is made of seven parts that the Codes already name: Food, Water, Energy, Material, Health, Care, Logistics. To these are added the rights of Article 1.2 of the Constitution that the Codes now partly quantify: housing, education, connectivity, personal AI Node.

## 1. Food Basket

Seat: Code 9, Art. 4. The Basket is guaranteed on a plant basis. Animal products collected without harm are not planned into requirements (Code 9, Art. 6-bis, para. 4, letter d). All items are of level 1 (Code 9, Art. 2, para. 5, letter a).

The starting point is the reference diet of the EAT-Lancet Commission (2019), calculated for 2,500 kcal a day. That diet allows animal foods to be zero. Here their calories are replaced by cereals, legumes and nuts, staying within the ranges the Commission indicates. In October 2025 the Commission published a new report, which updates the diet: this table, based on the 2019 report, must be compared with the new one.

| Item | Per person per day | Per person per year | Reference diet | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cereals, preferably wholegrain (dry weight) | 275 g | 100 kg | 232 g | Drafting adaptation |
| Legumes (dry weight) | 100 g | 36.5 kg | 75 g (0–150) | Within the range: dried legumes 50 g (0–100) plus soya 25 g (0–50) |
| Nuts and oilseeds | 75 g | 27 kg | 50 g (0–75) | Upper limit of the range |
| Vegetables | 300 g | 110 kg | 300 g (200–600) | EAT-Lancet |
| Fruit | 200 g | 73 kg | 200 g (100–300) | EAT-Lancet |
| Potatoes and other tubers | 50 g | 18 kg | 50 g (0–100) | EAT-Lancet |
| Unsaturated vegetable oils | 40 g | 14.6 kg | 40 g (20–80) | EAT-Lancet |
| Added sugars | no more than 31 g | no more than 11 kg | 31 g | EAT-Lancet |
| Iodised salt | no more than 5 g | no more than 1.8 kg | — | WHO |
| Vitamin B12 | requirement of 4 micrograms; the supplement dose, which is higher, is set by a nutritionist | — | — | German Nutrition Society (requirement) |

The total is about 2,500 kcal a day. For comparison: the minimum value used in emergencies by UNHCR and WFP is 2,100 kcal; the Millward-Hopkins study on decent living uses 2,000–2,150 kcal as the average for a whole population, children included.

**Three caveats.**

1. **Vitamin B12 is not optional.** In a diet without animal foods it must always be supplemented: the German Nutrition Society states that this is necessary. The 4 micrograms in the table are the daily requirement, not the supplement dose: the body absorbs only a small part of each dose, and supplement doses are usually much higher. It is a good that the Community cannot produce at the outset: it must be entered in the Dependency Register of Code 15.
2. **The table must be checked by those who deal with nutrition.** In particular for protein, iron, calcium, zinc, iodine, omega-3 and vitamin D, for the share of fats, and for children, pregnancy and breastfeeding.
3. **Foods for weaning and for frail persons** (Code 9, Art. 4, para. 1) are not quantified here: the Health Council defines them.

**Stocks.**

| Where | Quantity | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| Care Nucleus | 72 hours of vital goods and energy | Code 1, Art. 39, para. 2. For energy it is the most demanding target: storage is needed. Minimum autonomy, resilience and reserve must be distinguished later (para. 3) |
| Storehouse: cereals, legumes, oil, nuts | requirement until the next harvest, plus three months | Drafting choice; the days of stock are still to be set (Code 9, Art. 4, para. 2) |
| Storehouse: other foods | thirty days | Drafting choice |

**Land.** A study on the United States (Peters et al., 2016) estimates for vegetarian diets, including the vegan one, 0.13–0.14 hectares of farmland per person per year. It is an order of magnitude, on conventional yields: for farming without synthetic chemicals it is an optimistic minimum. With that value a Community of 150 people would need about 20 cultivated hectares, one of 500 about 65–70. The count of the UCR draft, with Italian yields reduced by one fifth, reaches 24 hectares.

## 2. Water Basket

Seat: Code 8, Art. 4-bis. Level 1–2 (Code 1, Art. 32, para. 1-quinquies, letter d).

| Item | Per person per day | Per person per year | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drinking water for drinking, cooking, personal and domestic hygiene | at least 50 litres | about 18 m³ | Code 8, Art. 4-bis, para. 3. For the WHO (2020) 50 litres is intermediate access, at medium risk to health; the risk is low only with optimal access, above 100 litres. The 50 litres are a guaranteed minimum, not a target. |
| of which hot water | at least 20 litres | about 7 m³ | Code 8, Art. 4, para. 4 (first application); cross-check: Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020) |
| Water for health and care services | to be quantified | — | Code 8, Art. 4-bis, para. 1: it is part of the Water Basket |

A Community of 150 people needs at least 7.5 m³ a day for personal uses; with guests up to 15% of residents, about 8.6 m³. Water for health and care services is part of the same Basket and in case of scarcity has the same precedence (Art. 4-bis, para. 5): it must be quantified separately, in the same sheet. Agricultural water and water for animals follow the priorities of Code 9, Art. 10, para. 8.

## 3. Energy Basket

Seat: Code 8, Art. 4. Level 1 (Code 8, Art. 2, para. 3, letter a). The Code lists the services that the minimum quota must cover and sets two first-application values: at least 20 litres of hot water per person a day and at least 18 °C in inhabited rooms in winter (Art. 4, para. 4). On a proposal from the Health Council the Assembly may raise them for age groups, and it sets the temperature that is dangerous to health. For the other services the draft proposes defining the Basket by services, and calculating the kilowatt-hours afterwards, climate by climate and building by building. Reducing demand, for example by insulating buildings, comes before increasing production (Art. 4, para. 4). The energy essential to health and survival is due to anyone who is in the territory (Art. 4, para. 2).

| Service (Code 8, Art. 4, para. 1) | Proposed level | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| Preserving food | one fridge-freezer for every four persons of the Nucleus or fraction thereof | Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020), adapted to large Nuclei: proposal |
| Cooking food | one hob for every four persons of the Nucleus or fraction thereof | as above |
| Domestic hot water | at least 20 litres per person a day | Code 8, Art. 4, para. 4; cross-check: Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020) |
| Basic lighting | all inhabited rooms, for the hours of darkness in which they are used | Drafting choice |
| Recharging essential devices | personal communication device, AI Node, health aids | Drafting choice |
| Minimum heating | at least 18 °C in inhabited rooms; more for the age groups the Assembly indicates | Code 8, Art. 4, para. 4; cross-check: WHO, housing and health guidelines (2018) |
| Cooling | as much as is needed to protect health during heatwaves; the dangerous temperature is set by the Assembly on a proposal from the Health Council | Code 8, Art. 4, paragraphs 1 and 4 |
| Health Storehouse and Network node | continuity without interruptions | Code 8, Art. 4, para. 1, letter g |

**Point of comparison.** The Millward-Hopkins study estimates that a decent life requires on average 15.3 gigajoules of final energy per person per year (from 13 to 18.4 depending on the country), in a scenario for 2050: about 4,250 kWh a year, that is 11.6 kWh a day. It is final energy, not only electricity, and it covers everything: home, food, transport, health care, school, production of goods. The domestic share of the Energy Basket is only part of it, still to be calculated by climate and building in the Energy Basket STV (Art. 4, para. 6).

## 4. Housing

The quantitative standards are set by the Assembly on a joint proposal from the Storehouse Council and the Energy and Water Council, with the opinion of the Health Council and of the Habitat and Territory Council (Art. 47-bis, para. 2). The table gives the Code’s first-application values: later determinations may not fall below these values without the concurring opinion of the Health Council.

## 5. Material Basket

Seat: C.1-Annex A, Art. 4. The Code lists nine categories; the list with the quantities and the stock thresholds is a matter for an STV, which the Assembly approves on a proposal from the Production Council, with the opinion of the Health Council for PPE, hygiene and goods for vulnerable persons. For clothing and hygiene the proposal lies with the Storehouse Council (Art. 4, para. 3). The quantities below are drafting choices, except where indicated.

| Category (Annex A, Art. 4, para. 1) | Proposed allocation | Level 1–2 or deferrable |
|---|---|---|
| a) Conservation and cooking of food | for every four persons of the Nucleus or fraction thereof: fridge-freezer, hob; pots and containers | 1–2 |
| b) Minimum furnishings | one bed with mattress per person; per Nucleus a table, one seat per person, containers for personal effects | bed 1–2; the rest, proposal: deferrable |
| c) Essential linen and tableware | two changes of bed and bath linen per person; one set of tableware per person | 1–2 |
| d) Basic tools for domestic life | one kit per Nucleus for cleaning, small repairs and sewing | proposal: deferrable, except cleaning |
| e) Basic PPE | according to the Safety Sheets of the tasks performed | 1–2 |
| f) Components for the maintenance of the functions of levels 1 and 2 | stock expressed in days of autonomy (Art. 4, para. 3); “levels 1 and 2” in the sense of Code 1, Art. 32, para. 1-quinquies | 1–2 |
| g) Essential spare parts for the microgrid and for the Network node | stock expressed in days of autonomy | 1–2 |
| h) Clothing and footwear suited to the climate and the season | 4 kg of new garments per person per year, first of all reused and repaired; washing for 80 kg of laundry per year | deferrable (Art. 4, para. 1-bis, letter e), except what the Health Council indicates as necessary to health |
| i) Personal hygiene products, including menstrual products and products for early childhood and incontinence | soap, oral hygiene, menstrual products, nappies, incontinence products, according to need; washable products preferred | as above; the draft proposes that the Health Council indicate soap and menstrual, early-childhood and incontinence products as necessary to health |

**Clothing and hygiene** (Art. 4, para. 1-bis). From delivery they are goods for personal use. Reuse comes first, and washable or compostable products are preferred; single-use products only under the conditions of Const. 14.1. They pass through the Textile and Housing Storehouse. Data on withdrawals of hygiene products remain confidential. For clothing the threshold is expressed as an allocation per person per year (Art. 4, para. 3). The quantity of 4 kg comes from Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020): it is little for a European today, acceptable with repair and reuse.

## 6. Health Basket

Seat: Code 6, Art. 6, para. 1, and Art. 10; Code 6, Art. 8-bis, para. 3. Level 1–2: these are life-saving goods (Art. 10, para. 1). The list and the minimum stock of the Health Storehouse are proposed by the Health Councils in federated coordination, by means of an STV (Code 2, Art. 3-bis), and approved by the Assembly with this Annex.

| Item | Proposed reference | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| Essential life-saving medicines | the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines, from which the Health Councils choose the list | Code 6, Art. 6, para. 1, letter a); WHO, 24th list |
| First-aid material | according to the health STV | Code 6, Art. 6, para. 1, letter b) |
| PPE within the health remit | according to the health STV | Code 6, Art. 6, para. 1, letter c) |
| Basic diagnostic tools | according to the health STV | Code 6, Art. 6, para. 1, letter d) |
| Validated health documentation and STVs | — | Code 6, Art. 6, para. 1, letter e) |
| Palliative care and pain relief | they are part of the Health Basket | Code 6, Art. 8-bis, para. 3 |
| Staff | 4.45 doctors, nurses and midwives per 1,000 people | WHO, health workforce strategy to 2030: a minimum threshold for identifying countries with severe shortages, not a European standard |

**Consequence.** With that threshold a Community of 150 people does not reach one full-time health worker. Health care must be organised in the Basin: three Communities of 500 people require six or seven. This is consistent with the district hospital that the Constitution entrusts to Circle 2 (Art. 5.1). The threshold is a minimum designed for the poorest countries: in European countries the density of health workers is much higher, and health care in the Basin will need more. Every medicine that the Community does not produce is an external dependency, to be entered in the Dependency Register.

## 11. The Emergency Basket of the networks

Code 16 provides, for the networks that prepare the transition, an Emergency Basket: food, drinking water, basic energy for cooking and heating, basic medicines and hygiene goods (Art. 1, para. 2, letter p). It is something other than this Annex: the list is adopted by the linked networks, with the opinion of persons competent in nutrition and health (Art. 14, para. 2), and this Annex does not set it. The quantities of the draft can, however, serve as a reference. For six months, per person, the diet of section 1 amounts to about 50 kg of cereals, 18 of legumes, 14 of nuts and 7 of oil, plus vitamin B12.

## 13. Gaps in the texts found while writing the first version

Historical note. While writing version 0.1 four gaps emerged. For each there was an [amendment proposal](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/proposals.html), accepted on 19 October 2026: the text is now in the Codes, and version 0.2 of this draft reports it in sections 3, 4, 5 and 9.

1. **Clothing and hygiene products** did not appear in any Basket. They are now in the Material Basket (Annex A, Art. 4, para. 1, letters h and i).
2. **Housing** was a constitutional right without a standard. The standard is now in Code 1 (Arts. 47-bis and 47-ter).
3. **Connectivity** was a right without quantities, and for education Code 5 did not say that materials and spaces are part of the Basket. The minimum provision is now in Code 7 (Art. 3, para. 9) and the materials are in the Basket (Code 5, Art. 2, para. 4-bis).
4. **The Energy Basket** had no first-application value. It now has two: 20 litres of hot water and 18 °C (Code 8, Art. 4, para. 4).

## What is needed to adopt it

**Data to be checked, and who could do it.**

- **The food table:** a nutritionist, who compares it with the 2025 EAT-Lancet report and checks protein, iron, calcium, zinc, iodine, omega-3, vitamin D and fats, and the needs of children, pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- **The dose of the vitamin B12 supplement:** a nutritionist or a doctor.
- **The 2,500 kcal per adult and the Community’s average by age**, with the correction for cold (about 100 kcal more for every 5 °C below 20): a nutritionist.
- **The land required and the yields:** an agronomist from the area.
- **The days of food stock** (harvest plus three months; thirty days): the Agroecological Council, with someone who runs a food store.
- **Whether 20 litres of hot water are enough for a shower and how much water health care and care need:** a plumbing technician and the Health Council.
- **The kilowatt-hours by climate and building, and the energy for the 72 hours of autonomy:** an energy technician.
- **The floor areas and bathrooms of housing:** a building technician, with an updated comparison with the Italian rules (Ministerial Decree of 5 July 1975).
- **Clothing (4 kg and 80 kg of laundry):** the Storehouse Council, with data on reuse and repair.
- **Health staff and the list of medicines:** a doctor and the Health Councils; the WHO list must be updated with the next edition, expected in 2027.
- **The hours of care:** the Care Council, with the first real cases.
- **School spaces and the kilometres of mobility:** the Competences Council; the values come from a study and have not been adapted.

**Open choices.**

1. **Basis for planning: the adult or the Community average.** The draft recommends the adult for distribution and the average by age, with the correction for cold, for stocks and for the land count.
2. **The 75 g of nuts.** They account for more than a third of the land and more than half of the field hours. The draft recommends a range, for example 30–75 g, with legumes and oilseeds as substitutes.
3. **Allocations for a large Nucleus.** The draft already expresses them “for every four persons or fraction thereof”, like the bathrooms: this must be confirmed.
4. **Water for health care and care:** 50 litres for personal uses plus a health share quantified separately, both written in the same sheet.
5. **Energy by services or in kWh.** The draft recommends services as the guarantee and kWh as the estimate of the Energy Basket STV, plus a separate value for the 72 hours.
6. **Which items are deferrable, beyond those set by the texts.** The choice belongs to the Assembly. The draft recommends: level 1–2 for everything needed for survival, health, basic hygiene, shelter and cooking; the rest deferrable. The personal AI Node must be classified.
7. **Minimum or European health staff.** The draft recommends indicating both, like scenarios A and B of the UCR draft, and deciding in the Basin.
8. **The relationship with the Emergency Basket of the networks:** a non-binding reference; the list remains the networks’ (Code 16, Art. 14, para. 2).

**Approval procedure.**

1. Each Council prepares its own part, publishes it for at least 72 hours and adopts it by the consensus method (Code 3, Art. 12-bis, para. 9):
   - Food: the Agroecological Council, with the opinion of the Health Council; Food Basket STV (Code 9, Art. 4, para. 7).
   - Water: the Energy and Water Council, with the opinion of the Health Council (Code 8, Art. 4-bis, para. 3).
   - Energy: the Energy and Water Council, with the opinion of the Health Council for vulnerable persons; Energy Basket STV (Code 8, Art. 4, paragraphs 4–6).
   - Housing: the Storehouse Council and the Energy and Water Council, with the opinions of the Health Council and of the Habitat and Territory Council (Code 1, Art. 47-bis, para. 2).
   - Material: the Production Council; for clothing and hygiene the Storehouse Council; opinion of the Health Council (C.1-Annex A, Art. 4, para. 3).
   - Health: the Health Councils in federated coordination, by means of an STV (Code 6, Art. 6, para. 1).
   - Care: the Care Council, with the opinion of the Health Council; Care Basket STV (Code 12, Art. 4, para. 6).
   - Logistics: the Logistics Council, with the opinion of the Health Council; Logistics Basket STV (Code 11, Art. 4, para. 6).
   - Network: the Network Council (Code 7, Art. 3, para. 9).
   - Education: the materials and spaces of Code 5, Art. 2, para. 4-bis.
2. The Health Council and the Storehouse Council bring the lists together in a joint proposal, published and open to challenge (Code 1, Art. 18, para. 2).
3. The Assembly of Circle 1 approves the Annex by the consensus method (Code 3, Art. 4). In the cases provided for, a Council may give a binding negative opinion (Code 3, Art. 8).
4. The Annex is reviewed every year.
5. **During the transition.** In Phase 0 only Codes 1–6 are active (Code 16, Art. 3, para. 1, letter b); Codes 7–10 and C.1-Annex A are activated in Phase 1, Codes 11–14 in Phase 2. Until then references apply “in so far as applicable” with the bodies already active (Code 16, Art. 1, para. 9). The non-binding opinions of Councils that do not yet exist are given by the Transition Council (Art. 8, para. 2); decisions that require the binding negative opinion of a Council not yet active remain suspended (Art. 8, para. 3). For housing, Code 1, Art. 47-bis, para. 6, applies.
6. **Why it is needed early.** To pass to Phase 1, three months of guaranteed Basket of level 1–2 and a Labour Balance are required (Code 16, Art. 4, para. 1, letters a and e): without an Annex that says what the Basket 1–2 is, the threshold cannot be verified. Phase 1 also requires the joint minimum Basket STV of Codes 9 and 10 (Code 16, Art. 3, para. 2, letter d).

## How it is corrected

The Annex is reviewed every year. Anyone who has data, an experience or a criticism can [open an issue](https://github.com/ImTheZeroIt/progetto-dei-cerchi/issues/new) or write to [zerocirclesproject@gmail.com](mailto:zerocirclesproject@gmail.com). The figures on this page are also collected in the [register of parameters to be verified](https://imthezeroit.github.io/progetto-dei-cerchi/en/register/parameters.html).
