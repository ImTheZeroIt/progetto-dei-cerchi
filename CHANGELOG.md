# Change log

This is the first public version: Constitution V.29 and aligned Codes, published on 5 October 2026.

From here on, every accepted change will be recorded on this page with its date, the text and article concerned, and the reason.

# Changes

## 26 October 2026 — Code 16: the networks path

By the author’s decision, Code 16 now has two paths towards the Confederation. Phases 0–4, the thresholds and Annexes A, B and C remain as they were and become the pilot Circles path, laboratories where the rules are tested. Alongside it comes the networks path, in which people begin where they live.

- **Two paths** (Code 16, Arts. 1.1, 1.2 k–q, 1.3, 3.8 and 11). The networks path has five phases: A Seeds, B Oil spot, C Boycott, D Conversion, E Confederation. The two paths meet at the conversion: the Circle 1 born from a network enters Phase 0 and goes through Phases 0–4 like every pilot Circle, with no shortened thresholds; Phase E comprises Phases 1–4.
- **Networks and legal forms** (Arts. 11.3–4 and 12). Mutual-aid networks and neighbourhood storehouses; common goods registered from the start in the name of associations or cooperatives. Until the choice of the persons, no one stops paying anything. The solidarity communities that already exist are the first nodes and do not have to change (Const. 4.5; Code 15, Arts. 21 and 21-bis).
- **Connection, pacts and lawful trials** (Art. 13). Map of the essential supply chains, personal and revocable pacts with those who work in water, energy, food and health care, training of those who provide relief, trials in which no one stops paying, with public results.
- **Threshold, verification and vote** (Art. 14). Six months of Emergency Basket for all participants and pacts signed in the four supply chains (twelve months and a plan for imports for a country that starts alone); verification by three persons drawn by lot from other networks, with public data; individual and secret vote, approved by the majority of the participants entitled to vote in each network, with a common date across several countries and a legal opinion before the first vote.
- **The boycott is not a rule** (Arts. 2.7 and 15). It is a political, non-violent and collective choice of the persons of the networks, for which each person answers personally; the entities do not resolve on it. The Code governs only what is lawful: solidarity funds, legal assistance, services open to everyone.
- **Essential workers and services open to everyone** (Art. 16; Code 15, Art. 19.3). Those who work in essential services continue to serve everyone; they receive the Emergency Basket first, work short shifts with relief and, after the conversion, their work counts as CBO.
- **Conversion** (Art. 17). Individual accession; networks → Circles 1, storehouses → Common Storehouses; fair rationing of the Emergency Basket; no one loses the home they live in; recognition by law where the majority wins in the institutions; those who do not accede remain free with their goods and rights.
- **Imports, promoting groups, portal** (Arts. 18–20). Three routes for imports; promoting groups without leaders, open, autonomous, connected by a protocol, which dissolve themselves; a federated, encrypted, open-source portal that collects little data and also works offline, with the vote of Code 7, Art. 7-bis.

Reason: The author’s decisions of 26 October 2026 on the draft “Transition through mutual aid and boycott”. The Constitution, including Art. 38.6 and the Inviolable Core, is not touched. The new thresholds are hypotheses of the project (PARAMETERS).

Versions: Code 15 from V1.7 to V1.8, Code 16 from V1.7 to V1.8; Constitution unchanged (V.34).

## 26 October 2026 — Associated Communities and equivalence at the boundary

By the author’s decision, the system of Circles does not aim to transform communities that already exist, such as solidarity communities with a common purse or ecovillages, but to bring them together by offering management tools.

- **Two ways of joining and fundamental rules** (Const. 4.5; Code 15, Arts. 21.1 and 21-bis.1). A community may join as an associated Community, which keeps its own internal order, or by also adopting the Codes. Titles I, IV, V and VII of the Constitution and the Inviolable Core are binding on all; the management rules (CBO, CdR, Storehouses, internal bodies, Councils and assemblies, the justice of Code 4 for internal conflicts, the phases of Code 16) are tools, which the associated Community may adopt one at a time whenever it wishes. The principles of the Core that concern management remain in force internally as well: the contribution of every able-bodied adult (Const. 2.3), sortition and rotation of offices (Const. 4.3, 4.4, 6.5, 31.2), no proxy voting (Const. 36.1).
- **Equivalence at the boundary** (Code 15, Art. 21-bis.2–3). The associated Community guarantees: the rights of every person, including the minimum Basket, care, free exit and appeal to the common bodies; inventory and Balance in the common format; a share of work for the common functions of Circles 2 and 3, counted for the Community as a whole and not per person, during the transition also in goods or through the Common Fund; persons on the sortition lists of the common bodies; the individual and secret vote of every member in the General Assemblies; common standards (STV) for the goods exchanged. When it uses a common tool (Pool, exchange between Storehouses, Network) it follows its rules.
- **General reference rule** (Const. 38.1; Code 15, Art. 21-bis.4). The management rules of the Codes apply to the associated Community only if it adopts them; those that implement the fundamental rules apply as to rights, prohibitions and limits, with equivalent internal bodies; references to the CBO and to the bodies of Circle 1 are read as references to the internal institutions, and internal work contribution counts as CBO for Const. 2.3, 4.4 and 7.3-bis. The CBO and Code 4 remain binding on the members of the Communities that adopt the Codes.
- **Conflicts and appeals** (Code 15, Art. 21-bis.5). Other internal conflicts are resolved under the internal order (for Grades 4–6 see below); those with other Communities follow Code 4; anyone who claims that one of their rights has been violated appeals to three persons drawn by lot from the Guarantee Circles of other Communities. The measures of Const. 1.3-bis and 7.3-bis and those restricting personal liberty remain with the common bodies.
- **Verification and Bridging Plan** (Const. 4.5; Code 15, Arts. 21.2 and 21-bis.6–7). Circle 2 (during the transition, the Transition Council of the receiving unit) verifies only the equivalence at the boundary, within ninety days and without political or ideological assessments. If something is missing, a Bridging Plan with defined time limits is agreed, which changes only what is needed at the boundary and never internal life; it is not imposed: without equivalence the community remains external, connected through agreements.
- **Phases of the transition** (Code 16, Arts. 1.3 and 3.7 f). The associated Community does not go through the phases internally: it enters a Basin when the equivalence has been verified, counts there as a pilot Circle, and for the thresholds what it provides at the boundary is counted. The references to Art. 21 of Code 15 introduced on 20 October (Code 16, Art. 1.3; Code 3, Art. 14-bis.4) are coordinated.
- **Pooled external income** (Code 16, new Art. 2.9; Code 15, Art. 19-bis.8). During the transition the members of an associated Community may voluntarily pool the personal income and assets they hold outside the Confederation (Const. 3.5-ter) and redistribute them among themselves for personal external expenses. The fund is not part of the Common Fund of the Circle (Const. 3.5-bis), gives no access to Storehouses, CdR, Common Goods or decisions, does not pay for work or buy consent; the proportional contribution to common external expenses remains; the option ceases when external means of payment cease.
- **Serious offences** (Code 15, Art. 21-bis.4 d and 21-bis.5). Offences of Grades 4–6 committed in an associated Community always fall to the Guarantee Tribunal, without prejudice to Code 4, Art. 37 (crimes remain with the State during the transition); other internal conflicts remain with the internal order.

Reason: The author’s decision of 26 October 2026 (“equivalence at the boundary”), with the author’s choices on internal offices (principles of the Core valid internally as well, forms free), on pooled external income and on offences of Grades 4–6. The amendments to the Constitution (Arts. 4.5 and 38.1) do not touch the Inviolable Core and count as text of the first adoption (Const. 38.1).

Versions: Constitution from V.33 to V.34, Code 15 from V1.6 to V1.7, Code 16 from V1.6 to V1.7.

## 21 October 2026 — low-severity items of the consistency review (C29–C70)

The forty-two low-severity items of the consistency review are corrected directly, by decision of the author. For each item: what changes and why.

- **C29. Drawing by lot from the lists** (Code 4, Art. 36.3). No one may be drawn from the federated lists who belongs to the Community of the person concerned, of the injured person or of the audited activity, or who has worked there in the last twenty-four months; the local members whom the Codes draw within the Community, such as those of Art. 27.1–27.2, remain. Reason: “Circle concerned” could also mean the Circle 2 from which the Tribunal is drawn.
- **C30. Activation and review of the Civic Protection Corps** (Code 14, Arts. 8-bis.1 and 8-quater.2). Scheduled interventions are resolved by the Assembly; in urgency the reference person on duty acts and informs the Circle at once. The review within twenty-four hours is carried out by the six persons drawn by lot from the two neighbouring Communities, by majority in each; if it is not concluded in time, the activation is not validated. Reason: it was not said who activates the Corps or who carries out the review.
- **C31. Binding opinion and change of members** (Code 3, Arts. 8.2 and 12-bis.10). Removing or replacing the members of the Council does not override the negative binding opinion, which remains effective; the new Council re-examines it only on the resubmitted proposal. Reason: removal could be used to get round the opinion.
- **C32. Conformity check and deactivation** (Code 4, new Art. 13-bis). A single procedure for the references of Codes 12, 13 and 14 to Code 4: technical check by the competent Council, adaptation plan, deactivation resolved by the Assembly with a replacement plan, without interrupting food and essential care. Reason: the Codes referred to a procedure that Code 4 did not contain.
- **C33. “Vital guarantees”** (Const. 7.3 and 30.2). During the suspension of CdR the rights of Art. 1.2 remain intact; no measure touches the vital guarantees of Art. 30.4. Reason: the term was used in two different senses.
- **C34. Review Panel** (Const. 31.3 d and e; Code 4, Arts. 35.1 and 35-bis.4). The Constitution names the Review Panel among the justice bodies; in appeals on offences of Grades 1–3 it includes a person with legal training taken from the Tribunal list, instead of a member of the Quality Council; the annual summary is prepared in turn by a Guarantee Circle of the Basin. Reason: the Panel was not in the Constitution and the Quality Council judges goods and services, not offences. Material correction (Const. 37.5) outside the Core.
- **C35. Majority in the Safety Council** (Code 14, Art. 3.3). Only technical acts, opinions and validation of sheets by majority; proposals and discretionary decisions by consensus and seventy-two hours of publication. Reason: it is the rule for all Councils (Code 3, Art. 12-bis.9).
- **C36. Appeal on a decision with an opinion of the Guarantee Circle** (Code 3, Art. 17.1). If the Guarantee Circle gave an opinion or authorisation on the decision, the appeal is decided by persons drawn by lot in other Communities. Reason: whoever gave the opinion cannot judge it.
- **C37. Solidarity Protocol** (Code 1, Arts. 2.8 d, 3.3, 30.1 d; Code 16, Art. 1.5; Annex C, Art. 5.3). A single name; the Community requests the Protocol and does not activate it; before the minimum Federation the declaration falls to the General Assembly of the affected Basin, within the limits of Const. 36.4. A Community not yet part of a Basin does not activate it on its own. Reason: Const. 36.2 has it activated by Circle 3 or by ten Circles 1 together.
- **C38. Health Basket** (Code 6, Arts. 6.1 and 9.5; Code 1, Art. 18.2). It is proposed by the Health Councils of the Communities as branches of the Medicine and Health Competence Circle and approved by the Assembly; the “federated Health Council” disappears. Reason: that body does not exist.
- **C39. Reduction of the CBO** (Code 1, Art. 11.1 d and e). Pregnancy, bereavement, crisis and the other situations of the Health and Care Codes give, on the indication of the Health Council or the Care Council, a temporary reduction without debt, not cumulative with others for the same hour; the diagnosis remains confidential. Reason: Code 6 referred to a reduction that Code 1 did not contain.
- **C40. Health Storehouse** (Code 1, Art. 14.1 d). A single name. Reason: “Care Storehouse” appeared only here.
- **C41. Cap on administrative time** (Code 1, Art. 2.7 e). The tenth of the CBO counts Councils, guarantee, facilitation, accounting and mandatory documentation; Corps shifts and other civic functions are counted separately. Reason: aligns Code 1 with Const. 6.5 and Code 3, Art. 12-bis.13.
- **C42. CdR parameters** (Code 1, Art. 44.4). Cap and decay are set every year by the General Assembly of Circle 2; that of Circle 3 may set common limits. Reason: “General Assembly” denotes two bodies (Code 3, Art. 14.1).
- **C43. Certification in UCR** (Code 4, Art. 4.6 d). Reference corrected to Code 1, Arts. 20.6 and 40.4. Reason: Art. 40 deals with open standards.
- **C44. Annex A and Code 9** (C.1-Annex A, Arts. 2.3, 4.5 a, 6.2, 6.3, 6.5, 10.3; Code 9, Art. 6.7). The production order is a five-level scale with the same formula for the Material Basket; every Circle 2 has at least one Advanced Workshop for every three or four Communities, activated by the shared Production Council; “cluster” and “basin” disappear. Reason: undefined terms and a “level 5” that did not exist.
- **C45. Level 2 of competences** (Code 5, Art. 4.2 b). It is confirmed by two persons already validated at least at Level 2 in the same competence, also from other Communities; the Competences Council records it. Reason: the “practice Circle” did not exist.
- **C46. Abbreviations L1–L3 and classes R0–R4** (Code 5, new Art. 4.2-bis). The abbreviations denote the Levels of Code 5; a table links the risk classes of Code 14 to the required Levels; safety training is added to validation. Reason: the two systems were not linked.
- **C47. Three reference figures** (Const. 29.3). They are for minors; adults learn with ordinary accompaniment. Reason: aligns the Constitution with Code 5, Art. 6.7 and paragraphs 29.4–29.5.
- **C48. Guarantee Circle: other functions and appeals above Circle 1** (Code 4, Arts. 4.2 d and 27.6; Code 6, Art. 2-bis.4 d; Code 3, new Art. 17.1-bis). The Guarantee Circle also performs the functions given by the other Codes; the appeal against decisions of Circles 2 and 3 is decided by three members drawn by lot from the Guarantee Circles of different Communities, and the decision applies to all of them. Reason: Const. 31.3 d) knows only the Guarantee Circle of the Community.
- **C49. Minors: leaving the Confederation and choice of residence** (Const., new Art. 26.5; Code 12, new Art. 7-ter; Code 4, Art. 37.6). From sixteen the minor declares departure personally, after an interview with the Care Council within fourteen days; before that the declaration is made by those who hold responsibility for their care, solely in their interest and after hearing them, and it does not extend to the children of a person who leaves; the Guarantee Tribunal may be seised within fourteen days. From fourteen an autonomous minor may choose where to live, with the assessment of the Care Council and the Competences Council within thirty days and appeal to the Tribunal. The guarantees of Const. 1.3 and 4.9-bis always remain. Reason: Const. 4.8 and 26.2 did not say how they apply to minors; the ages are the author’s choice (sixteen as for consent to care, fourteen as for the vote).
- **C50. Minimum energy** (Code 8, Art. 2.4). It is reduced only in case of material impossibility, for a limited time, with mutual aid and notice to the Guarantee Circle; “extreme emergency” alone is not enough. Reason: Const. 15.2 and 36.4.
- **C51. Water scarcity: who decides** (Code 10, Art. 8.2). The Energy and Water Council applies the order of Code 9, Art. 10.8; taking away water or other Basket goods is rationing and is decided by the Assembly, or by the urgent procedure. Reason: a Council does not decide in place of the Assembly (Const. 6.6).
- **C52. Bee buffer zone** (Code 10, Art. 2.6; Code 9, Art. 9.1 g; Code 13, Art. 6.3 c). Systemic products are never allowed; in extreme emergency the Assembly allows for a short time only non-persistent, non-systemic products; rescue of colonies follows Code 9 only. Reason: two procedures for the same thing.
- **C53. Essential energy for everyone** (Code 8, Art. 4.2). Guaranteed to whoever is in the territory, member or not, like drinking water. Reason: Const. 15.2 is not limited to members.
- **C54. Preventing births of companion animals** (Code 13, Art. 7.10). Sterilisation for the animal’s welfare or for the capacity of care, indicated by a veterinarian and decided by those who live with the animal, is not reproductive manipulation; no one imposes it on all. Reason: interpretative rule decided by the author on Const. 13.2–13.3.
- **C55. Wildlife** (Code 13, new Art. 5-bis and Art. 3.2 c). Against damage and risks only non-cruel methods: prevention, deterrence, fencing and, if needed, painless capture and relocation; the Biodiversity Council decides, after hearing the Agroecological Council for damage to fields; the method is set out in a new STV. Reason: the “non-cruel removal” of Const. 13.4 was not regulated.
- **C56. Euthanasia of sanctuary animals** (Code 13, Art. 4-bis.7). The same conditions as for companion animals, with the sanctuary sheet and information to those who care for them. Reason: the reference led to tools that exist only for companion animals.
- **C57. Transfer of an animal** (Code 13, Art. 7.6 d). No reimbursement between members; with outsiders only documented care expenses, through the Common Fund. Reason: Const. 3.5 and 3.5-bis.
- **C58. Facilities “every three or four Communities” and “territorial Basin”** (Code 13, Arts. 4.1, 4-bis.5, 11.3 c; Code 12, Art. 6.3; Code 9, Art. 6.4; Code 10, Art. 1.2 g and new h). Sanctuaries, care facilities and agricultural infrastructure are counted in every Circle 2; “territorial Basin” is defined as the territory of Circle 2. Reason: the “basin” was not defined.
- **C59. “Technical assembly” for dispatching** (Code 8, Art. 3.3 d). It is the Energy and Water Council; local manual override remains under the existing rules. Reason: the term of Const. 15.3 had not been implemented.
- **C60. Water below 50 litres per person** (Code 8, new Art. 4-bis.6). Allowed only for temporary technical impossibility, decided by the Assembly or by the urgent procedure, with a request for help, a request for the Solidarity Protocol where needed and notice to the Guarantee Circle; drinking water and water for health are reduced last. Reason: the same guarantees as for energy (Code 8, Art. 2.5).
- **C61. Admission of low-value goods** (Code 2, Art. 15.1 a). The Quality Council investigates and proposes; the body of the matrix of Code 3 decides; it is stated which Council is the “Competence Circle” for a good. Reason: three rules named three different bodies (the first part of the item, in Code 3, Art. 3.2 a, dates from 20 October).
- **C62. Use check of goods** (Code 2, Art. 3.2 and new Art. 14.1-bis). The provisional STV is published for at least seventy-two hours; for goods that are not simple or not safe some users are also heard; in a trial in a pilot Pantry opinions are collected; the Storehouse Council gives a non-binding opinion. Reason: Const. 9.2 wants users and those who run the Storehouse in the validation.
- **C63. Map of the World Pool** (Code 15, Art. 8.1). It is drawn up by the AI of each Intermediate Circle for its own territory; maps are exchanged through open protocols; no system merges them into a single model. Reason: Const. 22.2.
- **C64. Mandatory review of the AI** (Code 7, Art. 5-ter.5). Three refusals by the same Circle on the same matter, by any body, without the twelve-month limit. Reason: Const. 19.6 is Core and does not provide for those limits.
- **C65. Electronic voting and signatures for the Objection** (Code 7, new Art. 7-bis; Code 3, Arts. 12.2, 13-bis.1, 13-bis.4, 13-bis.6). Register of those entitled kept separate from the ballot box, open software, no log linking person and vote, counting by Community, never closed systems; the Objection may be signed by anyone entitled to vote, even without having voted, with 10% of voters and anonymous signatures; after confirmation by two thirds it cannot be raised again on the same part. Reason: Const. 34.2 and 34.4, with the author’s choices.
- **C66. Personal and common AI Node** (Const. 3.1, 3.4, 5.1 a, 8.2, 14.5; Code 7, Art. 5-bis.1). Common Goods are the Community and Circle AI Nodes; Circle 0 has a personal Node for each member; “AI-Logistics” becomes “Community and Circle AIs”; CdR and waste traceability use the Community Node; the personal Node is personal property (Const. 10.3). Reason: 3.1 and 5.1 a) contradicted 1.2 d) and 19.1. The amendments to 3.1 and 5.1 a), within the Core, are pure alignments of wording and therefore material corrections (Const. 37.5).
- **C67. Annex B: phases and Legal Fund** (Annex B, Arts. 5.7, 6.1, 8.6). Each element is activated in the phase in which Code 16 provides for it; when the Legal Fund runs out only the Annex’s initiatives stop, without counting as a missed threshold. Reason: the Annex mixed elements of different phases.
- **C68. Perceived safety below 80%** (Annex B, Art. 8.3 a and 8.5). Only the procedure of Annex A, Art. 4.6 is followed; if only the transition proposal had stopped, it restarts without another resolution. Reason: two procedures on the same condition.
- **C69. Point of no return** (Code 16, Art. 5.5). It refers to reversibility (Art. 2.2), to the rules on common goods (Art. 6) and to the external L3 opinion (Annex A, Art. 4.7). Reason: the reference led to an article about something else.
- **C70. References of Code 16 and Annexes** (Code 16, Arts. 1.2 i, 3.1 b, 10.3 b and new 1.8 and 1.9). The R0–R4 scale is that of Code 14; Code 6 applies in full from Phase 0; mandatory rules are those that no body may derogate from; each Annex, or its part for a phase, is approved by the Assembly of the scale to which it applies; references to Codes not yet active apply in so far as applicable, without prejudice to constitutional guarantees. Reason: wrong or vague references and an approval of the Annexes designed only for Circle 1.

Reason: These are the corrections proposed by the consistency review for the low-severity items, with the author’s choices on C49 (choice of residence from fourteen), C37 (a Community not yet in a Basin does not activate the Protocol on its own), C66 (Const. 3.1 and 5.1 a as well, as a material correction) and C70 (Annexes approved by the Assembly of the scale to which they apply). The other amendments to the Constitution (Arts. 3.4, 7.3, 8.2, 14.5, 26.5, 29.3, 30.2, 31.3) do not touch the Inviolable Core and count as the text of the first adoption (Const. 38.1). With this entry all 76 items of the consistency review are resolved.

## 20 October 2026 — medium-severity items of the consistency review (C8–C28)

The twenty-one medium-severity items of the consistency review are corrected directly, by decision of the author. For each item: what changes and why.

- **C8. Accompaniment Community** (Code 4, Art. 30.3). It is an open Community, with no protected perimeter and no supervision by the Civic Protection Corps, distinct from the Containment Community; the person is obliged to remain there and has daily accompaniment, is not held by force and, if they leave, the Guarantee Tribunal decides. The maximum duration (five years for Grade 5, ten for Grade 6) is a firm limit. Reason: without a definition the measure could become High-Security Containment without reoffending, which Const. 1.3-bis prohibits.
- **C9. Justice in Phase 0** (Code 4, Art. 37.5; Code 16, Art. 8.6). The panel drawn by lot outside the pilot Circle also applies when no Basin exists yet, also for the second Tribunal and for the substitute Guarantee Circle, with express derogations from the rules on the same Community; whoever carries out a review may not be whoever decided. Reason: the paragraph covered only an incomplete Basin.
- **C10. Recordings of the Corps** (Code 14, Art. 8-quater.3). The person on whom the Corps intervenes is also redacted; the full version is seen only by those who validate, by the competent justice bodies and by the person, for the part concerning them. Reason: the person is presumed not responsible (Const. 17.6; Code 4, Art. 14.3).
- **C11. Binding opinions** (Const. 6.6; Code 3, Arts. 7-ter.2, 7-quater.2 a, 8.1, 12-bis.9; Code 4, Art. 4.6 b; Code 9, Arts. 6-bis.3 c, 4 c, 5 d, 7.3; Code 10, Art. 4.4 d; Code 13, Arts. 3.2 c, 3.3, 3.5, 6.6; Code 14, Art. 3.6; Code 16, Art. 8.3). Article 8 of Code 3 becomes the closed list of binding negative opinions: Health, Network, Safety and, for the ecological limits that Const. 6.6 now names, Agroecological and Biodiversity. The welfare of animals that live or collaborate with people no longer goes through a “binding opinion” but through the authorisation that Const. 13.3 already gives to the Biodiversity Council. Reason: Code 3 forgot Safety and other Codes gave binding opinions that the Constitution did not provide for.
- **C12. Hierarchy of priorities** (Code 1, Arts. 2.8 a, 32.1-ter, 32.1-quater, 32.4; Code 6, Art. 7.2–7.3; Code 8, Art. 2.3; Code 11, Art. 2.4–2.5; Code 12, Arts. 2.4, 4.7). Article 32 of Code 1 applies to everyone; the scales of the sectoral Codes order only their own sector and level 1 is always the highest. The care of frail persons comes before perishable goods, but not before Basket food; spare parts come after everyone’s Basket, except for the urgent derogation of Art. 32.3. Reason: the sectoral Codes rewrote the hierarchy in different ways.
- **C13. “Levels 1 and 2 of the Basket”** (Code 1, Art. 32.1-quinquies; Code 16, Art. 1.2 f; Code 16, Annex C, Art. 6.1; Code 11, Art. 1.4 d–e; C.1-Annex A, Art. 2.3 a). A single definition, in Code 1, for energy, food, care and the other goods of the Basket; the other texts refer to it. Reason: the definition was only in Code 16, was incomplete, and “level” also referred to the Circles.
- **C14. Eighteen-year-olds who have not yet chosen** (Code 5, Art. 6.2, 6.6 and new 6.6-bis; Code 4, Art. 29.3 e). Until the choice of Const. 26.4 the CBO remains voluntary and the refusal procedure may not be started; the Competences Council informs the person at least three months before they turn eighteen and the time limit does not run without the information. Reason: no Code implemented Const. 26.4.
- **C15. Travel within the CBO** (Code 1, Arts. 25.5 and 43.2). Travel imposed by the CBO counts as hours of CBO and gives no CdR; the supplement applies only to travel beyond the CBO. Reason: within the CBO, Const. 8.1 admits CdR only for unpleasant, arduous or night tasks.
- **C16. Surpluses** (Code 1, new Art. 15.1-bis; Code 15, Arts. 6.2 and 15.3). When the Balance is active, the federable surplus goes first to the Pool; direct donation, reuse and disposal outside apply to the rest. Reason: Codes 1 and 15 gave two different rules for the same goods.
- **C17. Pool and Balance** (Code 15, Arts. 3.1–3.3, 9.2 c and new Art. 9-bis; Code 3, Art. 12-bis.2; Code 16, Arts. 3.2 g and 3.3 c). Each Circle 2 and 3 has a Storehouse Council drawn by lot that decides the allocations of the Pool within the annual criteria of the General Assembly, within 24 hours for vital needs; the Balance is no longer declared at “level 4”. Reason: no body decided above Circle 1.
- **C18. The home of those who leave** (Const. 3.3; Code 1, Art. 47.3 and new 47.3-bis). The dwelling is a Personal-Use Good for as long as the ownership of use lasts; on transfer, on leaving the Confederation or on termination of status the home is vacated, within the time limits already provided for, and those living in the same Circle 0 may stay. Reason: it was not said whether those who leave keep the home.
- **C19. Housing allocation** (Code 1, new Art. 47-ter). Annual plan proposed by the Storehouse Council and approved by the Assembly; allocation by need, never by hours of CBO, CdR or offices; public, anonymous waiting list; no one is left without shelter; appeal to the Guarantee Circle. ## 20 October 2026 — Constitution, new Art. 17.9; Code 14, Art. 1, para. 1, and new Art. 8-sexies; Code 4, Art. 25, para. 1; Art. 26, para. 3; new Art. 26-bis (personal defence)

Personal defence. Every person may defend themselves, other persons and animals without weapons, by getting away, bringing to safety, interposing themselves and, if that is not enough, with only the force strictly necessary and proportionate to end the danger. This applies against an ongoing or imminent physical or sexual aggression, where the danger is concrete and immediate and not merely feared, and against ongoing ill-treatment that injures an animal or makes it suffer; disagreements on how an animal is cared for or trained do not justify force and go to the Biodiversity Council. Objects found on the spot may be used only where there is danger to life or of serious injury and only to stop the aggression. Attack, revenge, punishment, force after the danger has ceased, force on persons to protect property alone and force to correct, educate or hold back someone one lives with are not defence. A person may enter someone else’s Circle 0 without consent only to stop ongoing physical or sexual violence, notifying the Civic Protection Corps at once, leaving as soon as the danger has ceased and subject to review by the Guarantee Tribunal within twenty-four hours. After the fact, the person gives first aid and notifies the Corps or the Guarantee Circle; the Guarantee Tribunal makes the assessment. An error due to excusable fear is not an offence; excess is Grade 4, or Grade 2 if the harm is slight; for minors only restorative and educational measures apply. During the transition the law of the State applies, and those who have joined undertake to stay within the narrower limits of these rules.

Reason: The texts entrusted protection to the Civic Protection Corps alone and required an “unarmed and non-violent” defence, but did not say whether and how a person may defend themselves or others when the Corps is not there.
