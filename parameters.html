Parameters to be verified

Working translation. The Italian text is the authoritative one.

Calibration draft, not adopted. This page collects the numbers proposed by the texts and states, for each one, where it comes from and what would be needed to confirm or correct it. It does not amend any provision: the values remain those written in the Constitution and in the Codes, and they may become binding only when data exist and the competent bodies are able to approve them.

How to read it

  • Proposed value: the number written in the texts today.
  • Location: the article in which it is found.
  • Origin: an external source, where there is one; otherwise “drafting choice”, that is, a number set to make the provision applicable, without any data to support it.
  • Uncertainty: low, medium or high, according to how far the value is supported by data and how much it may vary from one place to another.
  • Evidence needed: what would have to be measured, calculated or tested.

Anyone who has data, experience or a criticism may open an issue or write to zerocirclesproject@gmail.com.

1. Parameters with external benchmarks

For these values there are data or rules against which they can be compared. The comparison does not confirm them: it shows where to look.

Parameter Proposed value Location Origin and benchmarks Uncertainty Evidence needed
Limit of necessary work (CBO) average not exceeding 15 hours a week, on a quarterly basis Code 1, Art. 3 Drafting choice. A first calculation based on needs (draft UCR Annex) gives between 9 and 13 hours a week, manufactured goods included. With today’s mode of production, an independent study (McElroy and O’Neill, 2025) estimates about 27 hours by adding the missing services to the bare minimum; the authors point out that none of their scenarios is realistic. High. It is the parameter on which the balance of the whole system depends. Actual Labour Balances; measured hours of collective kitchens, gardens and workshops; how much household work is counted in the CBO.
Water Basket at least 50 litres per person per day Code 8, Art. 4-bis The WHO (2020) indicates about 20 litres as basic access, 50 litres as intermediate access, with medium health risk, and over 100 litres as optimal access, with low risk. The Sphere humanitarian standard sets a minimum of 15 litres in emergencies and recognises that 50 may be needed in cities. Low as a health minimum; medium for climate and local uses. Actual consumption by climate and season; availability of the resource in the territory. Issue 6.
Size of the Base Community as a rule 30–500 persons Const. 5.1 “Dunbar’s number” (about 150) is disputed: a 2021 review finds estimates between 16 and 109 with confidence intervals from 2 to 520, and concludes that stating a number is pointless. In Vermont town meetings an average of about 20% of voters take part in towns with fewer than 5,000 registered voters. High. Participation data from assemblies, cooperatives and ecovillages, broken down by size. Issue 4.
Threshold for a small Community fewer than 150 persons entitled to vote Const. 6.5; Code 3, Art. 12-bis, para. 12 As above: the threshold of 150 has no solid basis. High. Verify below what size it is no longer possible to staff all the Councils without overloading people.
Quorum of the Assembly none for ordinary resolutions; 1/3 of those entitled to vote for qualified decisions; 1/2 for those with special guarantees Const. 35.1; Code 3, Art. 12 In Vermont town meetings average participation is around 20%: a quorum of one third is higher than open assemblies usually reach. High. Actual participation in assemblies of similar size; effect of the republication provided for when the quorum is not reached. Issue 5.
Minimum autonomy of the Care Nucleus 72 hours for vital goods and energy Code 1, Art. 39 For vital goods it matches the objective of the European Preparedness Union Strategy (JOIN(2025) 130, 26 March 2025): self-sufficiency of the population for at least 72 hours. The EU, however, speaks of supplies (food, water, medicines, a power bank), not of energy autonomy: 72 hours of energy for every Nucleus require storage or generators, and that is a far more demanding objective. Low as a minimum. Adequacy for the climate and isolation of the place; stocks actually needed.
Validation of precautionary measures within 24 hours Code 4, Art. 34 Stricter than the time limit of the Italian Constitution (Art. 13: 48 hours for notification to the judge and 48 for validation). Medium: the time limit is protective; it must be verified that a body drawn by lot is able to meet it. Test case on Code 4. Issue 2.
Isolation and quarantine no more than 14 days per person and per exposure, one extension of 14 on certification by two L3 persons; beyond 28 days only the Guarantee Tribunal, for periods of 14 Code 6, Art. 2-bis, para. 3-quater Duration in common use, linked to incubation. Medium: incubation varies from one agent to another; the text refers to the dossier and the cap may prove short or long. Comparison with the durations recommended for the most likely agents; test case on validation within 24 hours.
Coercive measure in psychological crisis 72 hours at a time, renewable with a new validation; no more than 14 days in all and 28 days in 90; reassessment every 12 hours and every hour during physical restraint Code 6, Art. 8, para. 10 Stricter than the Italian TSO (compulsory health treatment, Law 180/1978: 7 days, validation within 48 hours). High: the cap of 28 days in 90 is new and leaves only voluntary measures for those who remain in danger afterwards. Opinion of health persons with experience of psychiatric emergencies; test cases.
Protected educational reception of minors no more than 12 months, renewable once; review at least every 3 months Code 4, Art. 25, para. 9 Within the range of Italian juvenile probation (messa alla prova, 1–3 years). Medium Comparison with educational pathways for minors who have committed offences; test case.

2. Drafting parameters

These are numbers set to make the provisions applicable. They have no external source: they must be tested on concrete cases.

Technical Councils 3–5 persons, one year, staggered renewal, not eligible to be drawn by lot again before 12 months Const. 6.5; Code 3, Art. 12-bis Medium Continuity of competences with one-year terms of office; suitable persons available in small Communities.

Time limits of the Autonomous Coexistence Pact conclusion within 30 days of the request; search for a place within 30 days in Circle 2 and a further 30 in Circle 3; cessation for non-use beyond 12 months Code 10, Art. 4-bis, paras. 1, 6 and 8 Medium In the worst case the wait exceeds six months: verify whether the handover time limit can be reduced.

3. Values the texts call for and that are missing

The Codes refer to these values, but no text sets them yet.

What is missing Where it is required References to start from
Content of the Sufficiency Basket: goods, quantities, units, period Code 1, Art. 18 Proposed in the draft Basket Annex, with the sources of each quantity.
Food Basket: nutritional values and days of stock Code 9, Art. 4 The planning figure used by the WHO, UNHCR and WFP in emergencies is 2,100 kcal per person per day: it is a survival minimum, not a standard of living.
Energy Basket: guaranteed minimum share Code 8, Art. 4 A 2020 study estimates that a decent life for 10 billion people is possible with less than 40% of today’s world energy. It must be translated into a share per person and per climate.
Housing: minimum floor area Const. 1.2; Code 10 Proposed in proposal 2, version 3: at least a living room, a bedroom and a bathroom; 30 m² for one person, 45 for two, 60 for three. The Italian rule on habitability (Ministerial Decree of 5 July 1975) requires, for a dwelling with bedrooms, 14 m² for each of the first four occupants (10 for each further one) and a living room of at least 14 m²; 28 m² for one person and 38 for two are the minimums for a studio flat. Since 2024 the “Salva casa” decree (Law 105/2024) allows, in existing buildings and under certain conditions, studio flats of 20 and 28 m². The Sphere standard for emergencies is 3.5 m² of covered space per person (4.5–5.5 in cold climates): it is an emergency minimum, not a dwelling.
Basic mobility: guaranteed distances and times Code 11; Code 1, Art. 25 No reference yet.
UCR calculation method Code 3, Art. 9 Proposed in the draft UCR Annex, with a worked example.
Public CdR table Code 1, Art. 46 Model proposed in the draft table. It is local: each Community approves it.
