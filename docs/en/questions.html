Frequently asked questions

The answers say what the texts provide for, not what will happen: the project is not in force anywhere and no community has tried it yet. Under each answer are the articles it comes from.

In short

What is the Circles Project?

A proposal for organising society, written out in full: a Constitution, 16 Codes and 4 Annexes. It is not in force anywhere.

Where to read it: The register of texts · Const. 0.1 · Const. 0.2

Is it communism?

Here there is no State, no party and no central power. People join by choice and can leave whenever they wish; those who grow up in it are called to choose at eighteen. What is personal stays personal, and those who contribute more are recognised. The project does not come from an ideology, but from logic and common sense. It does not ask anyone to sign up to a label: it asks to be judged on its rules.

Where to read it: Const. 0.2 · Const. 38.1 · Const. 4.8 · Const. 26.4 · Const. 10.3 · Const. 8.1

How is it built?

It starts from the person. Everyone lives in a Care Nucleus (Circle 0): alone, with family or with whoever they choose, up to fifteen people. The Community cannot enter it or dissolve it, except to protect a person. Nuclei form Base Communities of 30 to 500 people, which decide in assembly: everyone votes for themselves. Communities federate into wider circles, up to the planet, which coordinate and do not command. Roles are assigned by lot among volunteers, are of limited duration and cannot be held twice in a row.

Where to read it: Const. 5.1 · Const. 4.2 · Const. 4.3 · Const. 6.5 · Const. 4.4

What does it guarantee?

To every member a home, food, water, energy, health care, education and connectivity: these are not payment for work. Goods are taken from the Common Storehouses according to need. Among members money is abolished: it remains only in Common Funds, for dealings with the outside world. Land, water, energy and the means of production are commons, which cannot be bought or sold. The home one lives in and one's own belongings remain personal.

Where to read it: Const. 1.2 · Const. 1.3 · Const. 3.1 · Const. 3.4 · Const. 3.5 · Const. 3.5-bis · Const. 10.3

What does it ask?

Of every adult who is able, a rotating share of the work everyone needs. And giving up private ownership of the means of production, accumulation, weapons and the exploitation of animals, slaughter included. Anyone who does not agree can leave at any time and ask to come back.

Where to read it: Const. 7.1 · Const. 10.1 · Const. 10.4 · Const. 16.1 · Const. 13.1 · Const. 4.8

Does it work? Has it been tried?

We do not know. No community has put it to the test yet. Many thresholds and quantities are hypotheses to be verified. How to get there from the present system, and whether it holds at large scale, is written but never tried. This is why everything is public and the rules can be corrected.

Where to read it: Register of parameters to be verified, in Italian · How to contribute

Deciding

Who decides? Is there a government or a leader?

According to the Constitution there is no government and no leader. The assembly of each Base Community decides, and every person votes for themselves. The wider circles coordinate and do not command. Coordination roles are assigned by lot among volunteers, last a limited time and cannot be held twice in a row.

Where to read it: Const. 4.1 · Const. 4.2 · Const. 4.3 · Const. 5.1 · Code 3, art. 12, para. 1

Who can vote, and how?

Every person from the age of fourteen votes, and the vote does not depend on the Recognition Contribution (CdR). It cannot be delegated, bought or exchanged. Consensus is sought first; if it is not reached after two meetings, a vote is taken, and for the ordinary decisions of the Community a simple majority of those taking part is enough. People take part in person or through the Network, and each vote is anonymous.

Where to read it: Const. 36.1 · Const. 34.2 · Const. 35.1 · Const. 34.4 · Code 3, art. 12

What stops a group from taking power?

The texts provide these obstacles. Coordination roles are assigned by lot among volunteers and last no more than ninety days in a row; standing as a candidate is forbidden. Parties and stable groups that accumulate mandates, resources or consensus are forbidden. The vote cannot be delegated or bought, and the Assembly can dismiss the members of the Councils at any time. Nobody has yet tested whether this is enough.

Where to read it: Const. 4.4 · Const. 36.1 · Const. 6.5

Can the Constitution be changed?

Yes, by a long procedure. Only a Community that has applied the rule for at least twenty-four months can propose a change; then come twelve months of trial in at least five Communities and a vote with two thirds of the Communities and two thirds of the voters. Shorter times apply in the founding phase. The Inviolable Core can never be changed: among other things the rights of the person, the commons, disarmament and the right to leave.

Where to read it: Const. 37.1 · Const. 37.2 · Const. 37.3 · Const. 37.4 · Const. 38.2

Work and goods

Do I have to work? How many hours?

Yes: the texts ask every adult who is able for a Mandatory Base Contribution (CBO), a share of the work everyone needs. The written limit is an average of fifteen hours a week, calculated over the quarter, and there is no minimum. The texts themselves provide for checking this limit against real data. A home, food and care are not payment for this work.

Where to read it: Const. 7.1 · Code 1, art. 3, para. 2 · Code 1, art. 6 · Code 1, art. 2, para. 9

What happens to someone who does not do their share of the work?

The texts provide for gradual steps. First the tasks are changed and mediation is tried, and the person is informed of the right to an Autonomous Coexistence Pact. Then the Recognition Contribution (CdR) and non-essential goods can be suspended, but not housing, food, water and care. After sixty days of refusal without justification, a plan lasting up to twelve months is agreed with the person.

Where to read it: Const. 7.3 · Code 4, art. 29, para. 2 · Code 4, art. 29, para. 3

What do those who cannot work receive?

Housing, food, water, energy, care and education are guaranteed to every member without conditions. The texts exempt from the Base Contribution people over seventy-five, people with a severe disability and mothers up to twelve months after the birth. No compulsory work is assigned up to the age of fourteen. In case of illness the contribution is suspended, and the hours not worked do not become a debt.

Where to read it: Const. 1.3 · Code 1, art. 11 · Code 5, art. 6, para. 2

Is there money? How do people get food and goods?

The Constitution abolishes money among members. Food and necessary goods are taken from the Common Storehouses according to need and availability, without paying, and they do not depend on the work done. Goods that are not necessary and are scarce are obtained by booking or a waiting list. As long as an outside world exists, money is used only in dealings with the outside, and it is held by a Common Fund.

Where to read it: Const. 3.5 · Const. 3.4 · Const. 3.5-bis · Code 1, art. 18

What is the Recognition Contribution (CdR)? Is it a currency?

The Recognition Contribution (CdR) recognises work done beyond the Base Contribution: one validated hour is worth one CdR. For the texts it is not a currency: it cannot be passed on or inherited, it has a cap and it shrinks over time. It only gives priority over non-essential goods and services. It cannot buy or condition housing, food and care.

Where to read it: Const. 8.1 · Const. 8.2 · Const. 3.5 · Code 1, art. 43 · Code 1, art. 44 · Public CdR table, draft, in Italian

What are UCR?

Reproduction Cost Units (UCR) are a unit of account: they measure the time, materials, energy and transport needed to make a good again or to provide a service. One UCR corresponds to one hour of ordinary Base Contribution. They are not a price or a means of exchange, and they are not assigned to people. The method of calculation is in an annex that is still only a draft.

Where to read it: Code 3, art. 9 · UCR Annex, draft, in Italian

Can I keep my home and my belongings?

Personal belongings remain yours: the Constitution protects them as personal property. The home you live in is held in use: it cannot be inherited, but it cannot be taken from you without another adequate dwelling. As long as an outside world exists, what you own outside the Confederation remains yours. Putting it in common is a voluntary choice.

Where to read it: Const. 10.3 · Const. 3.7 · Const. 3.5-ter · Code 1, art. 47, para. 3 · Code 15, art. 19-bis

Can I have my own activity, a workshop, a field?

Yes, in use and not in ownership. The Constitution recognises "ownership of use": a stable right to use and look after a common good, for example a workshop or a field, as long as you really use it. You cannot sell it or rent it out, nor use it to make others work for you or to draw an income from it. What you produce you can contribute to the Common Storehouse.

Where to read it: Const. 10.2 · Const. 10.4 · Const. 3.2 · Code 1, art. 47

What happens to inheritance when a person dies?

According to the Constitution, personal belongings, clothes and work tools go to the person's Care Nucleus or to whoever the person named while alive. The home is not inherited: it returns to the Community, which protects those who lived there together. The Recognition Contribution (CdR) is extinguished. Digital memories and works go to the Open Archive and enter the public domain, unless the person decided otherwise.

Where to read it: Const. 3.6 · Const. 3.7 · Const. 3.9 · Code 1, art. 44, para. 5

And the machines?

Machines and automation are commons: they belong to no one. They are used first of all where work is repetitive, arduous or dangerous. The time they free stays with the Community: free time, learning, care. AI calculates and proposes, but does not decide: every decision stays with people, and the care of people is not automated.

Where to read it: Const. 3.1 · Code 7, art. 6 · Code 7, art. 2, para. 6 · Const. 19.6 · Proposal 8, robots, in Italian

Joining and leaving

Can I leave? And can I come back?

Yes. Under the Constitution every person can leave the Confederation at any time: they keep their personal belongings, but lose the vote and ordinary access to the Common Storehouses. They can ask to come back whenever they want, and return can be refused only in the very serious cases named by the Constitution. Someone who only changes Community is entitled to thirty days of transition and to accommodation in a Bridge House.

Where to read it: Const. 4.8 · Const. 5.2

What about those who live in the territory but do not want to join?

Nobody is obliged to join. Those who do not join can stay under an Autonomous Coexistence Pact: they receive in use a portion of land and, if there is one, a house to live autonomously, without ordinary withdrawals from the Common Storehouses. They must respect the ecological limits, the rules on animals and the ban on weapons. Someone who keeps refusing the Pact as well can be moved out of the common settlement, without violence, onto an assigned portion of land.

Where to read it: Const. 38.1 · Const. 4.9 · Const. 4.9-bis · Const. 4.9-ter · Const. 1.3

How does it start?

The texts provide for a start in phases. It begins with a pilot Circle of at least thirty people who join by choice, in compliance with local law. Then three or four neighbouring Circles come together, and no phase is passed before its thresholds have been checked. Nobody can be pushed to leave their outside job or to contribute property. The texts do not say how to obtain land and buildings.

Where to read it: Code 16, art. 3 · Code 16, art. 2 · Annex 16-C, art. 2

And relations with the State: taxes, purchases, trade?

As long as an outside world exists, the texts ask for local law and tax obligations to be respected. The Community's exchanges with the outside go through a Common Fund and serve to cover what is not yet produced, not to make a profit. Those who have an outside income keep it and pay a share to the Fund: in first application one tenth, never more than one fifth.

Where to read it: Code 15, art. 15 · Code 15, art. 17 · Code 15, art. 19-bis, para. 3 · Const. 3.5-ter

Life together

How are conflicts and crimes handled? Is there prison?

The texts aim to repair the harm and protect people, not to punish. The ordinary way is a restorative procedure; if that is not enough, people chosen by lot and in rotation decide. The word "prison" does not appear, but for serious violence there is containment, which is a form of confinement: reviewed every six months, and in first application lasting up to five or ten years. During the transition these measures do not apply: for crimes the public authorities remain competent.

Where to read it: Const. 30.1 · Const. 31.2 · Const. 1.3-bis · Code 4, art. 30, para. 3 · Annex 16-C, art. 7, para. 2

Are there police, an army, weapons?

The Constitution says there is no army and no permanent professional police. Protection is entrusted to an unarmed Civic Protection Corps, made up of volunteers chosen by lot, with shifts of ninety days at most. Weapons made to kill or seriously injure people are forbidden: they cannot be made or kept. The defence provided for is only civil and non-violent. The texts do not say how it would withstand an armed attack.

Where to read it: Const. 17.1 · Const. 17.2 · Const. 16.1 · Const. 16.4

Is religion allowed?

Yes. The Constitution guarantees the freedom to believe, not to believe and to change belief at any time. The Confederation is secular: no faith is an official religion and nobody can be obliged to fund one through common work or the Storehouses. Every Community makes available a neutral common space for reflection, meditation and celebration.

Where to read it: Const. 25.1 · Const. 25.2 · Const. 25.3

Why no meat and no exploitation of animals?

Because the Constitution regards animals as sentient beings with interests of their own: not objects, not goods, not means of production. For this reason it abolishes the slaughter of animals and trade in them, and meat is not produced. The food guaranteed to everyone is plant-based. A few products gathered without harm, such as eggs, milk or honey, remain allowed to a marginal extent.

Where to read it: Const. 13.1 · Const. 13.5 · Code 9, art. 6-bis

What role does artificial intelligence have? Can it decide?

In the texts artificial intelligence (AI) has only advisory or technical tasks: it processes data and makes proposals. The Constitution says it does not vote, judge, monitor or report people. Every binding decision belongs to people, who can always override the machine's proposal. A central AI commanding several territories is forbidden.

Where to read it: Const. 19.6 · Const. 19.2 · Code 7, art. 2, para. 8

Is there surveillance? What happens to my data?

The Constitution forbids using surveillance as a form of government or social control. Face or voice recognition and any biometric profiling are forbidden. Cameras in public spaces are allowed only if encrypted: the images are erased after seventy-two hours and can be read only after a serious event, with the validation of two neighbouring Communities. Every person can ask for their data to be removed.

Where to read it: Const. 18.1 · Const. 18.2 · Const. 18.3 · Const. 18.4 · Const. 21.6

How do school and children's education work?

The Constitution abolishes compulsory schooling that is the same for all, classes by age and marks. Children learn in the life of the Community, through practical activities, mentoring and play, accompanied by three reference persons who change every three months and are not from the family. By the age of eleven at least reading, writing and basic mathematics are guaranteed. There is no professional teacher.

Where to read it: Const. 29.1 · Const. 29.3 · Const. 29.4 · Code 5, art. 6

How does health care work?

For the texts health is a right, and it is neither a commodity nor a paid service. Three levels are provided for: a personal artificial intelligence with a preventive function, the carers of the Community and centres of high specialisation for wider territories. No treatment can be imposed without free and informed consent, except in strictly necessary emergencies. Life-saving emergency care is guaranteed to anyone.

Where to read it: Const. 28.1 · Const. 28.2 · Const. 1.3 · Code 6, art. 1

How big is a Community, and how are Communities linked?

A Base Community normally has from thirty to five hundred people, so that everyone can decide together. At least three neighbouring Communities normally form an Intermediate Circle, which coordinates among other things transport, energy and the district hospital. Above it are the Bioregion, for railways and specialised centres, and the Global Circle, which is only a common protocol. The texts do not say how a large city would be organised.

Where to read it: Const. 5.1 · Const. 4.2

What happens if something runs short or there is an emergency?

If a necessary good is scarce, the texts provide for temporary and reasoned rationing, approved by the Assembly. When there are not enough hands for everything, vital emergencies come first. For catastrophes or serious shortages a Solidarity Protocol can be activated, moving stocks and work towards the affected areas. An emergency does not suspend fundamental rights and does not last more than thirty days without a new vote of the Assembly.

Where to read it: Code 1, art. 19, para. 3 · Code 1, art. 32 · Const. 36.3 · Const. 36.4

The project

How can I criticise, propose a change or contribute?

You can open an issue on GitHub or write to zerocirclesproject@gmail.com: one line is enough. Say which text and article, what the problem is and, if you have one, the change you propose. Changes of substance stay open for discussion for at least fourteen days; today they are assessed by whoever maintains the repository, publicly and with reasons. Proposals that contradict the Inviolable Core are not accepted.
