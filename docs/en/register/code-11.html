Art. 1 — Subject matter and definitions

  1. This Code governs the organisation, procedures and responsibilities relating to:

    a) the physical movement of material goods;

    b) the delivery of the Basket and of common supplies;

    c) the basic mobility necessary for access to the fundamental rights and services of the Confederation;

    d) the mobility of persons, Bridge Houses and hospitality, pursuant to Articles 11 and 12 of the Constitution.

  2. The following fall within the scope of this Code:

    a) the delivery of the Basket, of PPE and of the supplies of the Health Storehouse;

    b) the transport of spare parts, components and tools necessary for the continuity of the Network provided for by Code 7, of the energy provided for by Code 8 and of the production provided for by Code 9;

    c) the basic mobility of frail persons within the meaning of Code 12, limited to access to the Basket, to health services and to List A CBO, where such access cannot be secured independently on foot or by bicycle.

  3. This Code pursues the following purposes:

    a) to guarantee the delivery of the Basket without logistics rent;

    b) to reduce and eliminate critical dependencies on fossil fuels and external couriers for level 1–4 services;

    c) to favour reuse, repair and light vehicles.

  4. For the purposes of this Code, the following definitions apply:

    a) basic logistics infrastructure: Logistics Basket depots, cargo bikes, hand carts, repair kits, exchange points and Basket lockers. It is an inalienable common good of Circle 1, which may be neither monetised nor transferred;

    b) shared advanced vehicle: an electric van or motorised trailer managed in federated form. It is a federated common good that guarantees effective access and not ownership by the individual Circle;

    c) Logistics Basket: the set of procedures for delivering the Basket to the home or to an accessible exchange point, in accordance with established times and priorities;

    d) Logistics STV: a technical validation sheet relating to a vehicle, a type of route, a load category or a delivery procedure.

Art. 2 — Principles

  1. Hierarchy of means. For every delivery, the least impactful means suitable for meeting the need shall be used, in the following order:

    a) travel on foot;

    b) human-powered bicycle or cargo bike;

    c) pedal-assist bicycle or cargo bike, powered by the microgrid provided for by Code 8;

    d) federated shared electric vehicle.

  2. Recourse to a means of a higher level shall be reasoned and recorded with reference to the distance, the weight or volume of the load, the frailty of the recipient or safety requirements.

  3. Reuse before new acquisition. Before a vehicle or tool is acquired, the availability of reusable, repairable or adaptable alternatives in the Circle or in neighbouring Circles shall be verified.

  4. Service priorities. In compliance with the hierarchy of Article 32 of Code 1, logistics allocation shall follow this order:

    a) Basket, PPE and supplies of the Health Storehouse intended for frail persons within the meaning of Code 12;

    b) spare parts necessary for the continuity of the critical infrastructure for Network, energy and production;

    c) Basket intended for the rest of the population;

    d) tools and materials for List A CBO;

    e) other commons.

  5. The priorities referred to in paragraph 4 are coordinated with Articles 18, 19 and 32 of Code 1 and with Codes 8 and 9.

  6. Non-profiling transparency. The Delivery Register contains data aggregated by category, time slot and outcome, without individual identifiers. It is managed in the Network in accordance with Code 7, Article 2.2. It is prohibited to track persons for purposes other than guaranteeing the service and the technical management of deliveries.

  7. Powering of electric vehicles. Electric vehicles intended for ordinary services shall be powered by the microgrid of Code 8 in accordance with the applicable priorities and allocation limits.

  8. In the event of a documented emergency, interruption of the microgrid or material impossibility, the temporary use of an external energy source is permitted exclusively in order to guarantee the continuity of the level 1–2 Basket. The use shall be documented, limited in time, accompanied by a plan for return to the microgrid and recorded in the Dependency Register of Code 15. It shall cease when the microgrid becomes available again.

Art. 3 — Logistics Council

  1. Each Circle 1 shall establish a Logistics Council composed of three to five members with validated L2 or L3 competences in light logistics, bicycle and cargo bike mechanics, road safety or load handling.

  2. Its members shall be drawn by lot in accordance with Article 12-bis of Code 3, shall hold office for one year and may not be drawn by lot again for the same Council until twelve months have elapsed from the end of their term of office.

  3. The Logistics Council:

    a) manages the Logistics Basket depot and the basic fleet;

    b) draws up and updates the Logistics STVs and the Safety Sheets within its competence;

    c) is responsible for the logistics part of the Dependency Register and feeds data into the Register provided for by Code 15;

    d) coordinates its activity with the Production, Energy and Water, Network and Health Councils;

    e) proposes to the Assembly the operational thresholds of the Logistics Basket;

    f) coordinates the shared vehicles for the mobility of persons and the management of the Bridge Houses.

  4. The Logistics Council has no allocative powers over goods outside logistics and no disciplinary powers. It may not access individual health data.

  5. The Council may not moderate, remove or modify deliberative or political content on grounds of merit, orientation or dissent.

  6. The acts of the Council are divided into:

    a) bound technical acts: the implementation of an STV or Safety Sheet in force, documented and published in accordance with the applicable access levels;

    b) discretionary proposals and decisions: adopted in accordance with the consensus method provided for by Code 3, subject to prior publication of the proposal, a period of not less than seventy-two hours for the submission of objections and the recording in the minutes of the outcome and of the reasons.

  7. Silence does not constitute consent if the proposal has not been published or if the prescribed period has not elapsed.

Art. 4 — Logistics Basket and delivery times

  1. Each Circle 1 shall guarantee the ordinary service standards established in the Logistics Basket STV:

    a) delivery of the Basket to frail persons within the meaning of Code 12 within twenty-four hours of the request;

    b) delivery of the Basket to the rest of the population within seventy-two hours of the request.

  2. The time limits run from the recording of the request in the Network provided for by Code 7. If the Network is not available, the request shall be recorded by an alternative procedure and entered in the Network when the service is restored.

  3. Material impossibility. In the event of documented material impossibility, simultaneous emergency, interruption of the Network or unavailability of energy, the Logistics Council shall:

    a) record the impediment, the date, the cause and the goods involved;

    b) activate without delay federated mutualism with the neighbouring Circles;

    c) guarantee continuity by means of a substitute procedure and a recovery plan with a fixed deadline.

  4. Failure to deliver a level 1–2 service, where it is not documented and federated mutualism has not been activated, shall be reported to the competent bodies for assessment in accordance with Codes 1, 3 and 4.

  5. The Logistics Basket may not be made subject to the recipient’s ability to collect it. For level 1–2 services, delivery to the home or to an accessible exchange point is guaranteed.

  6. The operational thresholds, including the days of autonomy of the depot, the minimum number of cargo bikes and the stock of reusable parcels, shall be resolved by the Assembly on a proposal from the Logistics Council, with the mandatory opinion of the Health Council and publication of the criteria.

Art. 5 — STVs, reuse, open sources and proprietary dependencies

  1. Logistics STV. Every vehicle, type of route, load category and delivery procedure shall have an STV in force.

  2. The STV shall indicate at least:

    a) maximum mass and volume of the load;

    b) type of route and permitted gradients;

    c) PPE required;

    d) loading and unloading procedures;

    e) risks and limit weather conditions;

    f) maintenance and competences required.

  3. Each individual delivery shall be recorded in accordance with the standardised procedure and shall refer to the applicable STV. It does not require a new STV to be drawn up.

  4. Verification of reuse.

    a) For levels 4–5, a documented verification of the availability of reusable or repairable bicycles, cargo bikes, carts or vehicles is mandatory before a new good is acquired.

    b) For levels 1–3 reuse is strongly preferred. Derogation is permitted only for certified safety requirements or for documented unavailability of suitable alternatives.

  5. Open sources. The STVs and the Logistics Safety Sheets shall be published in the Network provided for by Code 7 under a free licence. For levels 1–4 they may not be subject to a paywall.

  6. Critical proprietary dependencies. For levels 1–4 it is prohibited to introduce a vehicle or logistics component intended for the Basket that depends structurally:

    a) on proprietary components that cannot be repaired locally;

    b) on closed, non-inspectable firmware;

    c) on a mandatory external cloud service;

    d) on a single supplier with no practicable alternative.

  7. A reasoned transitional derogation, of a duration not exceeding twelve months, is permitted only in order to guarantee the continuity of the Basket and with:

    a) a binding Exit Plan;

    b) a fixed final deadline;

    c) publication of the decision;

    d) the opinion of the Production Council and of the Network Council, where relevant.

  8. Extension is prohibited, except in the case of documented material impossibility and a new reasoned decision of the competent body.

Art. 6 — Logistics infrastructure

  1. Basic logistics. Each Circle 1 shall have at least:

    a) a Logistics Basket depot with shelving and reusable parcels;

    b) two cargo bikes, hand carts and a complete bicycle repair kit;

    c) a notice board for logistics requests on the Network;

    d) Basket lockers in residential buildings or proximity exchange points;

    e) a load register and a maintenance register.

  2. The minimum equipment may be met through the reuse of existing goods if they comply with the applicable STVs and Safety Sheets.

  3. Federated advanced logistics. The Confederation guarantees every Circle 1 effective access to a shared electric vehicle within a reasonable territorial catchment area.

  4. As a rule, this guarantee is ensured by at least one shared electric van for every three or four Circles. Number and capacity are sized on the basis of population, territory, Basket demand and energy capacity. The guarantee concerns effective access and does not entail ownership or the permanent presence of the vehicle in every Circle.

  5. The advanced vehicle may be booked through the Network and is accompanied by a vehicle STV and a specific Safety Sheet. Driving it requires validated L2 competence. Consumption and charging are tracked in accordance with Code 8.

  6. Ordinary charging takes place on the microgrid provided for by Code 8, except for the emergency derogation of Article 2, paragraph 8.

  7. Distributed nodes. The logistics network comprises:

    a) exchange points at building and block level;

    b) standardised reusable Basket parcels, defined with the Production Council in accordance with Code 1 and C.1-Annex A;

    c) a traceable procedure of request, acceptance, delivery and confirmation that uses only the necessary logistics metadata.

  8. Redundancy. No Circle may depend structurally on a single other Circle for the delivery of the Basket. Each catchment area shall provide for at least two alternative logistics routes, where technically possible.

Art. 6-bis — Mobility of persons

  1. Freedom of movement is guaranteed. Mobility is a right and not an industry, pursuant to Article 11 of the Constitution.

  2. For the travel of persons, the following order shall be followed, according to the distance and the condition of the person:

    a) travel on foot and by bicycle;

    b) shared light vehicles of Circle 1;

    c) caravans and coastal transport under sail of Circle 2;

    d) electric and hydrogen railway network of Circle 3.

  3. No type of vehicle is prohibited as such: the limits depend on the energy available and on carrying capacity. Air transport is permitted exclusively for medical or civil protection emergencies.

  4. Shared vehicles may be booked through the Network or by a non-digital procedure. Access does not require CdR where the travel is necessary for care, health, the CBO, learning, participation in deliberations, functions conferred by sortition, relocation or an emergency. Frail persons are given priority.

  5. For non-essential mobility with limited availability, booking and the waiting list apply; any use of CdR shall follow Article 45 of Code 1.

  6. The rule whereby, ordinarily, eighty per cent of annual mobility takes place within the Circle 3 to which a person belongs, provided for by Article 12.2 of the Constitution, is a planning criterion verified on aggregated data. It does not constitute an individual limit and does not authorise the tracking of persons’ movements.

  7. Visits and tourism are regulated on the basis of carrying capacity and ecological impact. Each Assembly shall determine each year its own reception capacity, in compliance with Article 12.2 of the Constitution. For-profit accommodation facilities are prohibited.

Art. 6-ter — Bridge Houses and hospitality

  1. Each Circle 1 shall allocate to Bridge Houses from ten to fifteen per cent of its housing, pursuant to Article 12.1 of the Constitution. Bridge Houses are Commons; their location follows Code 10. Voluntary rotation in their management does not constitute additional CBO.
  2. Bridge Houses may be booked through the Network or by a non-digital procedure, according to actual availability.
  3. Hospitality does not require CdR where it is due to care, emergency, relocation pursuant to Article 49 of Code 1, transit towards another Circle, CBO in mobility, functions conferred by sortition or learning, and where it concerns non-confederated persons pursuant to Article 4.9 of the Constitution. Ordinary non-essential hospitality may require the use of CdR in accordance with Article 45 of Code 1.
  4. Hospitality between members has an ordinary minimum duration of fourteen days, except in cases of care, emergency, relocation or transit. Hospitality for non-confederated persons shall not exceed fourteen days and may not be extended.
  5. Guests shall not ordinarily exceed fifteen per cent of residents, except for care, emergency, relocation or transit.
  6. The exhaustion of the Bridge Houses may not prevent entry. The Community shall ensure an equipped stopping area and access to the Storehouse according to actual requirements.
  7. CBO in mobility may be requested at the host Community only if it is compatible with the person’s condition and is counted towards that person’s CBO.
  8. The guest’s Basket is ensured by the Storehouse of the host Community; differences are offset through the Balance provided for by Code 15.
  9. The hospitality register contains only aggregated and non-profiling data, in accordance with Code 7, Article 2.2.

Art. 7 — Safety

  1. No logistics shift may be carried out without a Safety Sheet in force.

  2. The Logistics Council validates the aspects relating to the vehicle, the route and the logistics procedures. The Health Council gives the mandatory opinion on loads, lifting, fatigue, PPE and weather conditions. The competences of the Safety Council provided for by Code 14 apply.

  3. Logistics PPE, including helmets, gloves, jackets and load restraint systems, is supplied by the competent Storehouses in accordance with the coordination between Codes 1, 6 and 14. It is free of charge, maintained and tracked.

  4. The absence of an item of PPE required by the Sheet entails the immediate suspension of the shift concerned and does not constitute a violation attributable to the person to whom the PPE was not supplied.

  5. The following are required:

    a) L1 training in bicycle safety, load management and urban traffic for basic shifts;

    b) L2 training for driving electric vans, handling fragile loads, delivering the Health Basket and towing trailers.

  6. Accidents and near misses shall be recorded in accordance with the confidentiality rules of Codes 6, 7 and 14. The information published shall be anonymous and aggregated.

  7. After a significant accident or near miss, the STV shall be reviewed within thirty days, unless a serious risk requires immediate intervention.

Art. 8 — Logistics Dependency Register

  1. The Logistics Council shall feed data into the Dependency Register provided for by Code 15, distinguishing:

    a) material dependencies: fuels, batteries, critical tyres and spare parts that cannot be sourced;

    b) technological dependencies: vehicles with closed firmware, proprietary diagnostics or remote locking;

    c) logistics dependencies: dependency on private external couriers, on a single external supplier or on non-federated infrastructure.

  2. Every critical dependency that affects the Basket shall be accompanied by an Exit Plan with a person responsible, resources, intermediate checks and a fixed final deadline.

  3. Dependencies that affect the delivery of the level 1–2 Basket have priority.

Art. 9 — Prohibitions and guarantees

  1. It is prohibited to:

    a) apply rents, tolls or surcharges to the delivery of the Basket, of PPE or of the supplies of the Health Storehouse;

    b) impose paywalls or proprietary licences on life-saving Logistics STVs and on Safety Sheets;

    c) claim patents or exclusive trade marks over the standard reusable Basket parcel;

    d) accumulate vehicles or logistics tools individually beyond the technical limits resolved by the Assembly, where this compromises the availability of the Basket, maintenance capacity or the balance of the workshops;

    e) refuse delivery of the Basket for disciplinary or punitive reasons;

    f) transfer to a party external to the Confederation operational control of the basic depot or of the basic fleet;

    g) use the Basket fleet for purposes unrelated to the priorities of Article 2, paragraph 4, without a resolution of the Assembly.

  2. Violation of paragraph 1 constitutes a serious procedural defect pursuant to Code 4.

  3. The suspension of the decision or of the shift shall be proportionate and accompanied by the obligation to guarantee the continuity of level 1–2 services by means of mutualism or a substitute solution.

  4. An appeal may be lodged with the Guarantee Circle within fourteen days, in accordance with Code 4.

Art. 10 — Transitional and final provisions and links

  1. This Code is coordinated with:

    a) the Constitution, Articles 11, 12, 32 and 33;

    b) Code 1, Articles 18–19 for the Basket, Article 27 for safety and Article 32 for the priorities;

    c) Code 2, for the STVs;

    d) Code 3, for resolutions;

    e) Code 4, for Guarantee and appeals;

    f) Code 5, for L2 and L3 competences;

    g) Code 6, for health and PPE;

    h) Code 7, Article 2.2, for the Network and non-profiling;

    i) Code 8, Article 4, for the Energy Basket;

    j) Code 9;

    k) Code 12, for the protection of frail persons;

    l) Code 14, for general safety;

    m) Code 15, for the Dependency Register.

  2. Within three months of approval, each Circle 1 shall activate the basic logistics depot, the minimum two cargo bikes, the Network notice board and the maintenance registers, including by means of the reuse of existing compliant equipment.

  3. Within twelve months, effective access to a shared electric van shall be guaranteed in accordance with Article 6, in coordination with the transitional provisions of Codes 8 and 9.

  4. An extension not exceeding six months is permitted exclusively in the event of a documented overlap of the transitional obligations provided for by Article 10, paragraphs 2 and 3, of Code 8 and by Article 10, paragraphs 2 and 3, of Code 9. The extension requires a binding operational plan, a fixed final deadline and the continuity of the Basket.

  5. Within twenty-four months the Exit Plans from critical logistics dependencies that affect the level 1–2 Basket shall be completed.

  6. Vehicles or procedures that do not comply with Article 9 shall be brought into compliance or deactivated within ninety days. If deactivation compromises a vital function of the Basket, a continuity plan shall be prepared with a compliant substitute solution and a fixed final deadline.
