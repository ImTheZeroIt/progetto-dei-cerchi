Public CdR table

Working translation. The Italian text is the authoritative one.

Calibration draft 0.1, not adopted. Code 1 (Art. 46) requires each Community to have a public table indicating the supplements, the limits of the utility recognition, the cap, the decay and the cost in UCR of non-necessary goods and services. It is approved by the Assembly of Circle 1, within the parameters that the General Assembly calibrates every year (Code 3, Art. 14). This page proposes a model to start from and checks with a simulation whether the cap and the decay do what they are meant to do: prevent accumulation without emptying the recognition of meaning.

The answer, in brief. Yes. With the cap of 240 CdR the most active person cannot get ahead of someone who works a little over three hours a week beyond the Base Contribution, and the most active twentieth of the Community stays below the concentration threshold set by the Code. Without the cap it would exceed it by far. Two points remain to be clarified in the texts, set out at the end: the curve used here depends on one of them.

1. What the CdR is, in four lines

  • It is the recognition of the contribution rendered beyond the Base Contribution (CBO). It is not money: it is not transferred, lent or inherited, and it does not bear interest (Code 1, Art. 42).
  • It cannot condition the Basket, the vote, health care, learning or sortition (Art. 42, para. 3).
  • It is used for three things only: precedence for non-necessary and scarce goods and services, prolonged exclusive use of a common space, ordinary hospitality in the Bridge Houses (Art. 45, para. 1).
  • Precedence applies to at most half of the availability: the other half remains for those without CdR, on the waiting list. CdR that have been used are cancelled and pass to no one (Art. 45, paragraphs 3 and 4).

3. Supplements

The Code sets the range: from thirty to one hundred per cent (Art. 10, para. 2). The draft proposes three classes. The list of tasks in each class is drawn up by the Health Council and the Storehouse Council, with the approval of the Assembly (Art. 8, para. 1).

Class Proposed supplement Examples
Discomfort 30% Sanitary cleaning, compost management, repetitive work
Heavy work or night 50% Physically heavy work, shifts between 22 and 6 o’clock
Risk, or several conditions together 100% Tasks with significant risk; heavy work performed at night

The percentages are drafting choices within the Code’s range.

4. Utility recognition

For works and services of particular common utility the Assembly may add a reasoned and published recognition, never exceeding the hours actually spent (Art. 43, para. 3). The table must set its limit.

Proposed limit Value Why
For each work or service no more than half of the hours spent Leaves room for recognition without doubling the value of the hour
Per person, in one year no more than 60 CdR A quarter of the cap: no one reaches the cap through recognitions alone
Who decides the Assembly, with those who have benefited from the work Const. 9.2

These are drafting choices. They serve to prevent recognition from rewarding popularity more than contribution.

The curve. The text says “one quarter each quarter” but does not say how. There are three possible readings.

Gradual: from the twelfth month it declines in proportion to time, one twelfth per month 12 months 24th month 3.1

Only the first respects both sentences of the paragraph: twelve months of full value and extinction at the twenty-fourth. It is the one used in this draft and written into Code 1 with amendment proposal 9, accepted on 19 October 2026. The first version of this page used the second, more generous one.

What it means. Someone who accrues CdR regularly and never uses them keeps, at steady state, the equivalent of 18 months. The cap of 240 is therefore reached by accruing 13.3 CdR a month: 3.1 hours a week beyond the CBO. Beyond that threshold, working more does not increase the balance.

One CdR is worth one UCR, that is, one hour of work (Art. 45, para. 2). With the coefficient of the draft UCR Annex, 240 CdR correspond to about 12,000 euros of goods bought outside, or to sixteen weeks of Base Contribution.

6. What non-necessary goods and services cost in CdR

One CdR used is worth one UCR (Art. 45, para. 2), that is, one hour of work. The cost of each good is its real cost: the hours of those who produce it, the share of the means of production and transport, calculated with the method of the draft UCR Annex. One rule applies: an hour is worth an hour, whoever works it and wherever.

  • For what the Community produces itself, the hours, materials and energy are counted.
  • For what is bought outside, the actual hours are used, where they are known; otherwise the price multiplied by the hours of work per euro of the country of production.

The values are examples to show orders of magnitude: prices and shares produced abroad are assumptions. Each Community draws up its own table. The last column shows what the price alone, converted at the European average, would give: the difference is real work that the price does not reveal.

Four things to bear in mind when reading the table.

  • The CdR gives precedence, not a purchase. The same goods remain accessible without CdR, by booking or waiting list, and precedence applies to at most half of the availability (Art. 45, para. 4).
  • One CdR is one hour. A kilo of coffee is worth more than three hours of voluntary work, because it contains three hours of work; a musical instrument made in Europe, eight.
  • What requires more work costs more, not what comes from far away. Coffee is worth three hours because it is picked by hand, with few means of production. Distance weighs only through transport, which by sea is a small matter: about 20 cents per kilo from China to Italy, less than a hundredth of a CdR. The same good produced with better tools would cost fewer hours, wherever it was produced.
  • No variable prices. The cost is the reproduction cost, the same for everyone: auctions, markets and exchanges of CdR between persons are prohibited (Art. 45, para. 5).

Is the cap of 240 right or low?

Compared with costs it is not low: the most expensive item in the table is worth 50 CdR, the cap is almost five times as much. And the cap limits how much can be set aside, not how much can be accrued: those who use their CdR accrue more without limit.

As regards concentration it is close to the maximum that holds. In the Community simulated in section 7:

Cap Reached by working beyond the CBO Share of CdR of the most active 5%
120 1.5 hours a week 13%
180 2.3 hours a week 15%
240 3.1 hours a week 17%
300 3.8 hours a week 20%: beyond the Code’s threshold
360 4.6 hours a week 24%: beyond the Code’s threshold
480 6.2 hours a week 29%: beyond the Code’s threshold

The Code requires the cap to be reviewed when 5% of persons hold more than 20% of the CdR (Art. 46, para. 3). In this simulation, at 300 the threshold is just exceeded. A lower cap, for example 120, would make balances more equal but would be reached with less than an hour and a half a week. 240 is a reasonable choice: there is no reason to raise it.

8. What can be drawn from it

  1. The cap does its job. It stops accumulation at a level that a person reaches with a little over three hours a week: those who work much more do not obtain more power of precedence than others.
  2. Decay does not empty the recognition of meaning. A CdR keeps its full value for twelve months and fades out within two years: there is time to use it, not to hoard it.
  3. Those who perform many unpleasant tasks soon reach the cap. From that moment the supplement is no longer recognised for them. The Code prevents this through sortition and the rebalancing of unpleasant tasks (Art. 8, para. 3): the table works only if that rotation really works.
  4. The value of the cap is defensible, not proven. 240 CdR are sixteen weeks of Base Contribution. It must be reviewed with the quarterly data that the Code requires to be published (Art. 46, para. 2).

9. Two points to be clarified in the texts

They are not corrected here: they have become two amendment proposals, 9 and 10, accepted on 19 October 2026.

  1. How the value declines. Article 44, paragraph 3, says that CdR retain their value for twelve months, then lose one quarter per quarter and are extinguished “at the end of the twenty-fourth month”. It does not say whether the quarter is lost all at once or a little at a time. Proposal 9 chose gradual reduction, in proportion to time, the only one that respects both sentences, and added two missing rules: the oldest CdR are used first, and each CdR counts for the value it has at that moment.
  2. What happens to the supplement of someone at the cap. Article 44, paragraph 2, says that once the cap has been reached further activities do not give rise to CdR. Does this also apply to the supplement for unpleasant tasks performed within the CBO? The text does not say. Proposal 10 confirmed that it does, and defined how the balance is measured.
