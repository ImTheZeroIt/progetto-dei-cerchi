Art. 1 — Subject matter, definitions and temporary nature

  1. This Code governs the phases, the thresholds, the transitional competences and the launch procedures of the Confederation, as well as the passage from the pre-confederal state to the operational confederal state. It governs two paths leading to the same Confederation: the pilot Circles path, with Phases 0–4 of Articles 3 and 4, and the networks path, with Phases A–E of Articles 11–20.

  2. For the purposes of this Code, the following definitions apply:

    a) transition: for each pilot Circle, the period between its establishment and the declaration of closure provided for by Article 10 for the minimum Federation of which it is part;

    b) phase: the transitional period in which specific objectives, Codes and procedures are activated, with the related exit thresholds;

    c) pilot Circle: a Base Community, corresponding to Circle 1, established pursuant to Articles 4–6 of the Constitution for the initial experimentation;

    d) Basin: an aggregation of three or four pilot Circles that are territorially contiguous and ecologically connected, corresponding to the Intermediate Circle or Circle 2 referred to in Article 5 of the Constitution;

    e) minimum Federation: an aggregation of at least three Basins;

    f) Basket 1-2: the goods and services of the Sufficiency Basket indicated by Article 32, paragraph 1-quinquies, of Code 1;

    g) point of no return: the resolution that makes irreversible the designated use of ecological or territorial commons;

    h) Social Start-up Protocol: Technical Annex A to this Code, adopted in accordance with Article 19 of Code 3, which defines a methodology for verifying the institutional social thresholds without introducing autonomous individual obligations or sanctions;

    i) risk levels R0-R4: the levels defined by Article 1, paragraph 3, of Code 14. Until that Code is activated, the same levels shall be assigned provisionally by the Health Council in the Safety Sheets provided for by Article 4 of Code 6;

    j) First-Year Operating Charter: Technical Annex C to this Code, adopted in accordance with Article 19 of Code 3, which brings together the minimum rules necessary for the launch of a pilot Circle without introducing autonomous obligations;

    k) pilot Circles path: the path of Phases 0–4 of Articles 3 and 4, in which the pilot Circles already live according to the rules and test them before they serve everyone;

    l) networks path: the path of Phases A–E of Articles 11–20, in which people build mutual-aid networks where they already live, in compliance with the legal order in force, until their conversion into Circles;

    m) network: a neighbourhood, village or building mutual-aid network connected to the networks path pursuant to Article 12, paragraph 1;

    n) neighbourhood storehouse: the common stock of a network, such as long-life food, basic medicines, tools and spare parts, registered in the name of an entity pursuant to Article 12, paragraph 3;

    o) participant: a person who declares that they participate in a network, including without a public name; a dependent person is a participant, from completion of the fourteenth year, if they so declare; for the purposes of the threshold of Article 14, the persons who live with them and depend on them are also counted;

    p) Emergency Basket: the goods indispensable to the life and health of every person, namely food, drinking water where the network must guarantee it, basic energy for cooking and heating where the network must guarantee it, basic medicines and hygiene goods, in the quantities of the public list of Article 14, paragraph 2;

    q) essential supply chains: the activities that ensure water, energy, food and health care in the territory, including the transport and distribution that serve them;

    r) country: the territory in which the same State legal order applies; a network that extends over several countries connects in only one of them, indicated in the declaration of Article 12, paragraph 1.

  3. This Code is temporary in nature and shall cease to produce normative effects, for each minimum Federation, with the declaration of closure referred to in Article 10, except for the memory provisions laid down therein. It continues to apply to the pilot Circles, Basins and minimum Federations that have not closed the transition, including those that are constituted or join later, pursuant to Article 21 of Code 15; to the associated Community of Article 21-bis of Code 15 it applies with the limits of Article 3, paragraph 7, letter f), and Articles 2 and 15, paragraphs 2 and 3, apply in any case. It applies to the networks from their connection to the networks path until their conversion pursuant to Article 17; to the Circles born from the networks it applies as to the pilot Circles, pursuant to paragraph 8 of that Article. It may not establish permanent rights, obligations or competences beyond the transition period, nor modify the substantive content of Codes 1–15. Its provisions do not constitute an amendment of the Codes pursuant to Articles 37 and 38 of the Constitution. In the event of conflict, the Constitution prevails.

  4. The transitional classification of a good, a function or a competence does not determine its permanent regime.

  5. During the transition, the references in the Codes to Circle 1 shall be understood as referring to the pilot Circle and those to Circle 2 as referring to the Basin. The minimum Federation exercises the functions of Circle 3 until it is constituted as an Extended Circle pursuant to Article 14-bis of Code 3. Until the minimum Federation is constituted, the declaration of the Circle 3 concerned provided for by Article 36.2 of the Constitution shall be adopted by the General Assembly of the affected Basin, within the limits of Article 36.4 of the Constitution.

  6. During the transition, the Balance provided for by Code 15 operates at the scale of the Basin and of the minimum Federation. The activation of the operational confederal phase does not imply that the Confederation comprises the whole of humanity.

  7. The revision of the Constitution and of the Codes follows the founding regime under the conditions of Article 38.2 of the Constitution, that is for as long as there are not at least three Extended Circles, even when part of the Confederation is still in transition.

  8. Technical Annexes A, B and C to this Code are adopted and amended in accordance with Article 19 of Code 3, with the following rules:

    a) each Annex, or its part that applies to a phase, is adopted by the Assembly of the unit that is in that phase pursuant to Article 3, paragraph 7: the Assembly of the pilot Circle for Phase 0, the General Assembly of the Basin for Phase 1 and that of the minimum Federation for Phases 2 and 3, pursuant to Articles 12 and 14 of Code 3;

    b) an amendment resolved by a unit applies to it and to the units that join it, pursuant to Article 3, paragraph 7, letter c); it is published in the federated Knowledge Storehouse, so that the other units may adopt it;

    c) no Annex and no amendment to it may alter the phases, the thresholds, the competences or the guarantees of this Code and of Codes 1–15, pursuant to Article 19, paragraph 5, of Code 3.

  9. Until a Code is activated pursuant to Article 3, the references to it in this Code and in its Annexes apply in so far as applicable with the bodies and procedures already active. The opinions of Councils not yet established follow Article 8, paragraphs 2 and 3. The prohibitions and guarantees that directly implement the Constitution always remain unaffected.

Art. 2 — Principles of the transition

  1. Continuity of the Basket. No change of phase shall be resolved if it entails the interruption of levels 1 and 2 of the Basket or a risk of interruption that is not adequately managed.
  2. Reversibility. Before a point of no return is adopted, every intervention shall provide, where technically applicable, for a reversibility plan.
  3. Proportionality and gradualness. The activation of functions, Codes and constraints shall be commensurate with the competences available, the resources surveyed and the risks actually present.
  4. Effectiveness. The merely formal constitution of Circles, Basins or Federations lacking territorial continuity, actual activity or a verifiable census is prohibited.
  5. Transparency. The phases, thresholds, censuses, resolutions and plans shall be published in accordance with Article 15 of Code 3 and submitted to a period for observations of at least seventy-two hours. Personal data shall be excluded or aggregated in accordance with Code 7, Article 2.2.
  6. Non-commodification. It is prohibited to establish rents on the launch of the Confederation, on the commons conferred during the transition or on life-saving competences.
  7. Legality and coexistence. During the transition the Circles shall operate in compliance with the legal order in force in the territory, pursuant to Article 38.6 of the Constitution. Article 37 of Code 4 and Chapter IV of Code 15 apply. The networks, the entities that hold their goods and the promoting groups shall operate in the same way: this Code governs only lawful activities; it neither prescribes nor authorises the boycott, which remains the political choice of the persons provided for by Article 15, and governs only the vote by which the persons express that choice, pursuant to Article 14.
  8. Voluntariness. Accession is individual and voluntary. No one may be induced to cease external activities, to confer goods or to waive rights recognised by the legal order in force. This is without prejudice to the proportional contribution on external income provided for by Article 19-bis of Code 15, which shall be made known before accession.
  9. Pooled external income. As long as external means of payment exist, in compliance with the legal order in force pursuant to Article 38.6 of the Constitution, the members of an associated Community pursuant to Article 21-bis of Code 15 may voluntarily pool the income and assets that they hold outside the Confederation pursuant to Article 3.5-ter of the Constitution and redistribute them among themselves for personal external expenses. The fund so formed consists of personal external income and assets: it is distinct from the Common Fund of the Circle provided for by Article 3.5-bis of the Constitution and by Article 17 of Code 15 and does not form part of it. It gives no access to the Storehouses, the CdR, the Common Goods or decisions; it is not distributed in proportion to work or as payment for it and may not be used to purchase work or consent, pursuant to Article 19-bis, paragraph 2, of Code 15. The fund is not registered in the name of the Circle’s entity and is held by the members according to the law in force. Every member may withdraw from it at any time and takes back the part not yet spent, according to the agreement among the members. The proportional contribution to common external expenses under Article 3.5-ter of the Constitution and Article 19-bis of Code 15 remains unaffected. The option ceases when external means of payment cease, pursuant to Article 19-bis, paragraph 8, of Code 15.

Art. 3 — Phases of the transition

  1. Phase 0 — Pre-confederal. The indicative duration is from zero to six months. In this phase:

    a) at least one pilot Circle shall be constituted with no fewer than thirty persons having joined;

    b) Codes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 and Chapter IV of Code 15 shall be activated;

    c) an initial census, aggregated and non-profiling, shall be carried out of the available land, of the L1 and L2 competences, of the machines and equipment and of the needs of Basket 1-2;

    d) a provisional minimum Storehouse in accordance with Articles 13–22 of Code 1 and a minimum Knowledge Storehouse in accordance with Article 3 of Code 5 shall be activated;

    e) basic L1 life-saving training in accordance with Code 6 shall be started;

    f) the Guarantee Circle and the Councils provided for by the active Codes shall be established, including the Storehouse Council, the Health Council and the Competences Council, also in merged or shared form or in the three Area Councils provided for by Article 12-bis, paragraph 12, of Code 3;

    g) the pilot Circle shall operate in accordance with the First-Year Operating Charter.

  2. Phase 1 — Basin. The indicative duration is from six to eighteen months. In this phase:

    a) three or four contiguous pilot Circles shall aggregate into a Basin;

    b) Codes 7, 8, 9 and 10 and C.1-Annex A shall be activated, in addition to the Codes of Phase 0;

    c) the provisional minimum federated Land Register provided for by Article 7 of Code 10 shall be started;

    d) the Basin food balance and a joint minimum Basket STV of Codes 9 and 10 shall be prepared;

    e) the land eligible to become a sanctuary shall be surveyed on a preliminary basis, in preparation for the activation of Code 13;

    f) the Network, Energy and Water, Agroecological, Habitat and Territory and Production Councils shall be activated;

    g) the Balance provided for by Code 15 shall be activated at the scale of the Basin and the Basin Storehouse Council provided for by Article 9-bis of Code 15 shall be established.

  3. Phase 2 — Minimum Federation. The indicative duration is from eighteen to thirty-six months. In this phase:

    a) at least three Basins shall be aggregated;

    b) Codes 11, 12, 13 and 14 shall be activated, in addition to the Codes already activated;

    c) Chapter III of Code 15 and the Balance at the scale of the minimum Federation shall be activated, with the Storehouse Council provided for by Article 9-bis of Code 15 for Circle 3;

    d) at least one sanctuary per Basin shall be established, pursuant to Article 4 of Code 13;

    e) the critical machines, the PPE and the collective protections shall be surveyed in accordance with Code 14;

    f) the Logistics, Care, Biodiversity and Safety Councils shall be activated;

    g) the first version of the Dependency Register shall be established, integrating the registers of the sectoral Codes.

  4. Phase 3 — Operational Confederation. The indicative duration is from thirty-six to sixty months. In this phase:

    a) Codes 1–15 and C.1-Annex A shall be activated in full operation, within the limits of the conditions of effectiveness provided for by the individual Codes;

    b) ecological, safety and food autonomy audits shall be carried out by independent L3 members drawn by lot in accordance with Article 36 of Code 4 and the sectoral Codes;

    c) the overcoming of at least fifty per cent of the registered critical dependencies shall be completed, on the basis of verifiable objectives and time limits;

    d) the node of the federated Network provided for by Code 7 shall be completed for each Basin.

  5. Phase 4 — Closure. The transition ends with the declaration of closure adopted in accordance with Article 10.

  6. The durations indicated are for guidance and do not replace the exit thresholds of Article 4. No phase may be passed before the related thresholds have been verified.

  7. Scale of the phases. Phase 0 belongs to each pilot Circle, Phase 1 to each Basin and Phases 2, 3 and 4 to each minimum Federation. The following rules apply:

    a) the passage from Phase 0 to Phase 1 is resolved jointly by the pilot Circles that constitute the Basin, pursuant to Article 14, paragraph 10, of Code 3; the passage from Phase 1 to Phase 2 by the General Assembly of the Basins that constitute the minimum Federation; the subsequent passages and the closure by the General Assembly of the minimum Federation;

    b) a pilot Circle that is constituted or joins when others are already in a later phase goes through Phase 0. Once the thresholds of Article 4, paragraph 1, have been exceeded, it joins a contiguous Basin or constitutes a new one. A Basin that has exceeded the thresholds of Article 4, paragraph 2, joins a minimum Federation or constitutes a new one with at least two other Basins;

    c) a pilot Circle or a Basin that joins a Basin or a minimum Federation assumes its phase when it meets, for its own part, the thresholds of the passages already resolved by that unit. The verification is carried out by the Transition Council of the receiving unit and may not introduce further assessments, pursuant to Article 4.5 of the Constitution. The Codes already activated apply to the entrant from the day of entry, and the transitional time limits of the individual Codes run from that date;

    d) if the minimum Federation has already closed the transition, the verification of letter c) includes the thresholds of Article 4, paragraphs 3 and 4, and the closure of the transition for the entrant is resolved by its General Assembly pursuant to Article 10, paragraph 1; entry into the Extended Circle follows Article 14-bis, paragraph 4, of Code 3;

    e) the entry of a new pilot Circle or Basin does not make the receiving unit go back a phase;

    f) the associated Community pursuant to Article 21-bis of Code 15 does not go through the phases for its own internal life and is not required to activate the Codes and Councils of the phases, except for the tools it adopts. It enters a Basin, or contributes to constituting one, when the equivalence at the boundary has been verified. It counts as a pilot Circle, if it has at least thirty acceding persons, or otherwise together with other contiguous associated Communities until that number is reached, for the purposes of Article 1, paragraph 2, letters a) and d), and paragraph 5, of Article 3, paragraph 7, letter a), and Article 5, paragraph 1, and of the references of the Codes to the pilot Circle that concern the common bodies and the guarantees of persons, including Article 37 of Code 4. The thresholds of Article 4 are verified, for its part, on the data and commitments that it provides at the boundary pursuant to Article 21-bis, paragraph 2, of Code 15; the thresholds of Article 4, paragraph 1, and, for its part, those on the Labour Balance and on the Social Start-up Protocol do not apply to it and are verified on the other units of the Basin. In the Basin and in the minimum Federation it follows the rules of the common tools activated in the phase.

  8. Two paths. Phases 0–4 of paragraphs 1–5 are the pilot Circles path. Alongside it, the networks path follows Phases A–E of Articles 11–20. The two paths meet with the conversion of Article 17: from that moment every Circle 1 born from a network is in Phase 0, goes through Phases 0–4 as a pilot Circle, with the thresholds of Article 4, and, once the thresholds of Article 4, paragraph 1, have been passed, enters a Basin, including one already constituted, pursuant to paragraph 7, letters b) and c).

Art. 4 — Activation and exit thresholds

  1. Passage from Phase 0 to Phase 1. The following are required:

    a) the continuous guarantee of levels 1 and 2 of the Basket for at least three months in the pilot Circle;

    b) a verified census and the functioning of the minimum Storehouse and of the minimum Knowledge Storehouse;

    c) the absence of unmanaged critical violations pursuant to Article 9, paragraph 2;

    d) the documented application of Level 1 of the Social Start-up Protocol;

    e) a Labour Balance, drawn up pursuant to Article 2 of Code 1, documenting for at least one quarter the coverage of levels 1 and 2 of the Basket within the limit of Article 3 of the same Code.

  2. Passage from Phase 1 to Phase 2. The following are required:

    a) self-production in the Basin of at least seventy per cent of levels 1 and 2 of the Basket, calculated according to the criteria of the applicable Basket STV;

    b) the functioning of the minimum Land Register;

    c) at least one area that is a candidate sanctuary, attested by the Habitat and Territory Council. In the absence of the Biodiversity Council, the provisional biological assessment shall be given by the Transition Council pursuant to Article 8, paragraph 2, with an explicit indication of its provisional character;

    d) the absence of unmanaged critical violations pursuant to Article 9, paragraph 2, verified in accordance with Codes 4, 9 and 10;

    e) the documented application of Level 2 of the Social Start-up Protocol;

    f) Basin Labour Balances that respect the limit of Article 3 of Code 1 for at least two consecutive quarters.

  3. Passage from Phase 2 to Phase 3. The following are required:

    a) the functioning of the provisional minimum Biodiversity Register;

    b) the functioning of the register of general risks;

    c) the establishment of at least one sanctuary per Basin;

    d) Safety Sheets in force for all activities classified R2 or higher;

    e) the absence of R4 incidents that are unmanaged or lack the required analysis of causes;

    f) the documented application of Level 3 of the Social Start-up Protocol.

  4. Passage from Phase 3 to Phase 4. The following are required:

    a) a positive federated audit on autonomy, carried out after at least six continuous months of operation without non-substitutable critical dependencies that compromise the continuity of the Basket;

    b) the entry in the Dependency Register of an exit plan with a definite time limit for each critical dependency still present;

    c) the functioning of the nodes of the federated Network in all the Basins;

    d) the documented maintenance of Level 3 of the Social Start-up Protocol;

    e) Labour Balances that respect the limit of Article 3 of Code 1 for four consecutive quarters or, failing that, the submission of a reasoned proposal for revision of that limit pursuant to Article 38 of the Constitution.

  5. The verification of the thresholds shall be carried out by the Transition Council, with the mandatory opinion of the Councils competent for the subject matter. The change of phase constitutes a constitutive decision pursuant to Article 3, paragraph 3, of Code 3 and shall be resolved by the General Assembly indicated by Article 3, paragraph 7, letter a), in accordance with the convening, quorum and procedure provided for by Codes 3 and 4. The resolution shall be published in aggregate and non-profiling form.

  6. The social opinion provided for by the Social Start-up Protocol may bear exclusively on the institutional verification of the threshold and on the possibility of proposing or resolving the change of phase. It may not produce individual sanctions, exclusions or limitations, nor make conditional the access to Code 4, to the Guarantee, to vital goods or to Basket 1-2.

Art. 5 — Transitional government

  1. In each phase a distinct Transition Council shall be established, with a duration limited to the phase of reference, at the scale provided for by Article 3, paragraph 7: one for each pilot Circle in Phase 0, one for each Basin in Phase 1 and one for each minimum Federation in Phases 2 and 3. The Transition Council of the Basin and of the minimum Federation is a shared Council within the meaning of Article 12-bis, paragraph 6-bis, of Code 3, with its members drawn by lot from among the persons eligible pursuant to paragraph 2 of the pilot Circles that are part of it; the rules of paragraph 3 remain in force.

  2. The Council is composed of three to five members with L2 or L3 competences relevant to the phase, including organisation, confederal law, agroecology, territory, safety and biodiversity.

  3. Appointment and duration are governed as follows:

    a) the members shall be drawn by lot from among suitable volunteers who have fulfilled the required CBO and the specific training, with the right to refuse the office, in accordance with Articles 4.3 and 4.4, letter d), of the Constitution;

    b) no member shall perform the function for more than ninety consecutive days or for more than one hundred and eighty days in total in three hundred and sixty-five days;

    c) renewal shall take place by staggered sortition, with a minuted handover, so as to guarantee the continuity of the Council throughout the phase;

    d) between two terms of office in the Transition Council there shall elapse at least twelve months of exclusive activity in ordinary CBO;

    e) simultaneous membership of the Transition Council and of the Storehouse Council in the same phase is prohibited;

    f) each member shall declare his or her conflicts of interest and shall abstain when there is a conflict or a direct coincidence between controller and controlled, in accordance with Code 3, Article 16.

  4. The Council shall operate with a quorum of at least two thirds of its members. Decisions shall be taken by consensus in the absence of reasoned objections, in accordance with Code 3, Article 4. Meetings shall be minuted and the minutes shall be kept for thirty-six months.

  5. The Transition Council shall not resolve the change of phase or the adoption of a point of no return. It may formulate proposals to the General Assembly, to which the decisions fall: the change of phase in accordance with Article 4; the point of no return in accordance with Article 2, paragraph 2, Article 6 and Article 4, paragraph 7, of Annex A.

  6. The Transition Council:

    a) coordinates the censuses;

    b) draws up the transitional plans;

    c) verifies the thresholds;

    d) proposes the activation of the Codes;

    e) feeds the Dependency Register;

    f) coordinates the Councils activated in the phase;

    g) verifies the application of the Social Start-up Protocol solely with regard to the institutional thresholds.

  7. The Transition Council may not exercise competences reserved to other Councils, cede to external parties the control of commons or assume external financial commitments that give rise to critical dependencies.

Art. 6 — Commons during the transition

  1. The goods conferred during the transition shall be entered in a federated provisional inventory according to the applicable competence:

    a) Code 10, Article 7, for land and territorial goods;

    b) Code 5, Article 3, for essential knowledge;

    c) Articles 13–22 of Code 1 for the goods held in custody in the Storehouses;

    d) C.1-Annex A and Codes 9 and 14 for machines, seeds, PPE and collective protections, from their respective activation.

  2. The nature as inalienable common good of the goods provided for by Article 3.1 of the Constitution is not suspended during the transition. This Article governs exclusively custody and provisional entry.

  3. The transfer to the definitive regime of custody shall take place:

    a) for territorial and ecological goods, at the closure of Phase 2;

    b) for critical technological goods, at the closure of Phase 3.

    The transfer requires a resolution of the General Assembly and the mandatory opinion of the competent Council.

  4. Until the definitive regime is activated, acts of disposal exceeding ordinary maintenance and use in conformity with a Sheet in force are ineffective. Acts of sale and purchase, letting or mortgage of the commons remain prohibited, in accordance with Articles 3.1 and 3.2 of the Constitution.

  5. The provisional Storehouses shall keep separate accounts for goods in transition and for commons already entered in the definitive regime. Traceability and access follow Code 1; for the Knowledge Storehouse, Code 5 applies.

  6. The legal ownership of the goods during the transition is governed by Article 16 of Code 15.

Art. 7 — Persons, competences and transitional training

  1. L1 life-saving training shall be provided free of charge from Phase 0 in accordance with Code 6 and, from the activation of the respective Codes, in accordance with Codes 13 and 14.
  2. Prior competences shall be recorded in accordance with Code 5, Article 10, paragraph 1. The provisional validation of continuity, the related time limits and the shortening of training paths are governed exclusively by Article 10, paragraphs 2–7, of Code 5. The Transition Council may not extend or derogate from those time limits.
  3. Every function classified R3 or higher requires mentoring by a person with verified L2 or L3 competence, until sufficient internal coverage is available.
  4. The transitional registry of competences shall be maintained in aggregate and non-profiling form, in accordance with Code 7, Article 2.2.
  5. The Transition Council shall consider the Social Start-up Protocol exclusively for the purposes of verifying the social thresholds of institutional sustainability and within the limits established by Technical Annex A.

Art. 8 — Conflicts and Guarantee during the transition

  1. Code 4 applies from Phase 0.
  2. Until a competent Council is activated, the Transition Council may give, on a provisional basis, the non-binding mandatory opinions concerning the subject matter not yet covered. The opinion shall be noted as provisional and submitted to review within six months of the activation of the competent Council.
  3. The substitute function of paragraph 2 does not apply to the binding negative opinions provided for by Article 8 of Code 3 or to the authorisation of Article 13.3 of the Constitution. Until the competent Council is activated, decisions requiring one of those opinions or that authorisation shall be suspended. The other decisions not dependent on that opinion may proceed.
  4. Suspensions necessary to deal with a serious and immediate R4 risk are immediately enforceable in every phase, in compliance with the procedures of the competent Codes.
  5. An appeal to the Guarantee is permitted within fourteen days in accordance with Article 17 of Code 3 and Article 17 of Code 4. No provision of this Code or of the Social Start-up Protocol may make conditional, delay or limit access to the Guarantee.
  6. During the transition, Chapter V of Code 4 applies within the limits of its Article 37. Until a Basin is constituted, the bodies of Code 4 that require members external to the pilot Circle, including the Review Panel of Article 17 and the substitute Guarantee Circle of Article 15, paragraph 3, are formed in accordance with Article 37, paragraph 5, of Code 4.

Art. 9 — Prohibitions during the transition

  1. During the transition it is prohibited to:

    a) establish rents on the launch, on the commons entered in the provisional inventory or on L1 life-saving training;

    b) sell, mortgage or encumber the land designated as a candidate sanctuary or as a candidate ecological corridor;

    c) patent, register, file, claim or maintain exclusive titles over wild or local genetic material or over the essential knowledge surveyed;

    d) assume external financial debts or conclude supply agreements that give rise to a critical proprietary dependency pursuant to Article 5 of Code 2, in the absence of an exit plan with a definite time limit entered in the Dependency Register;

    e) cede to external parties the control of the provisional Storehouse, of the provisional Land Register, of the provisional Biodiversity Register or of the Dependency Register;

    f) constitute Circles or Basins that are fictitious or lack the minimum requirements provided for by Article 2, paragraph 4, and by Article 3;

    g) impose paywalls on the STVs and on the Sheets essential for Basket 1-2, biodiversity or general safety.

  2. For the sole purposes of this Code, violations of paragraph 1, letters a), b), c), d) and e), are classified as critical; those of letters f) and g) are classified as substantial.

  3. The ascertainment, the precautionary and restorative measures and any remedies are governed by Code 4. The effects on decisions shall be determined in accordance with Article 18 of Code 3. The technical exclusion of a good or service remains within the competence of the Quality Council pursuant to Code 2.

Art. 9-bis — Gradual application of technological sovereignty

  1. In implementation of Article 38.6 of the Constitution, until the closure of the transition the obligation to possess physically the hardware of every system used, provided for by Article 19.4 of the Constitution, and the obligation to use only open-source AI, provided for by Article 19.5, apply gradually as follows:

    a) the use of a system hosted on external infrastructure or incorporating an AI model that is not open source is permitted only if the dependency is admitted as residual and surmountable pursuant to Article 5, paragraph 2, letter b), of Code 2, or as temporary pursuant to Articles 2, paragraph 1, 8, paragraph 2, and 10, paragraph 3, of Code 7, and is entered in the Dependency Register with the Substitution Plan or the Exit Plan;

    b) gradual application does not allow closed code or models that are not open source to be used for Management AIs, for the algorithms of Article 2, paragraph 5, of Code 7, for the personal AI and for the consultation and voting systems of Article 34.4 of the Constitution;

    c) the systems of letter a) installed at the Community do not receive automatic or remote updates; every update is verified by the Network Council before installation and is reversible, pursuant to Article 5-ter, paragraph 7, of Code 7;

    d) gradual application ceases for each system on the expiry of its Plan and, in any case, with the closure of the transition.

  2. The use of systems whose source code is not available follows, during the transition and after it, Article 19.4 of the Constitution, Article 5, paragraph 2, letter b), and Article 12, paragraph 5, of Code 2.

Art. 10 — Closure of the transition, memory and precedence

  1. The closure of the transition shall be resolved, for each minimum Federation, by its General Assembly by a qualified majority of two thirds, in accordance with Articles 13 and 14 of Code 3. The resolution shall be preceded by:

    a) the mandatory opinion of the federated Councils;

    b) the positive federated audit provided for by Article 4, paragraph 4;

    c) the verification of compliance with the conditions for closure.

  2. The declaration of closure produces the following effects:

    a) this Code shall cease to produce normative effects for the minimum Federation that has resolved the closure, pursuant to Article 1, paragraph 3, and the minimum Federation is constituted as an Extended Circle pursuant to Article 14-bis of Code 3;

    b) the provisional inventories, the minutes of the Transition Councils and the resolutions on changes of phase shall remain preserved as historical memory in accordance with Code 7;

    c) the goods already transferred to the definitive regime remain subject to the sectoral Codes;

    d) the goods not yet transferred because the thresholds have not been reached shall be returned or transferred by resolution of the General Assembly and with the opinion of the competent Council.

  3. During the transition, in the event of conflict between this Code and Codes 1–15, this Code prevails exclusively to the extent strictly necessary to govern the activation sequence and the thresholds referred to in Article 4, guaranteeing the continuity of Basket 1-2. Such precedence does not create permanent competences and does not apply:

    a) to the Constitution;

    b) to the mandatory rules of the Constitution and of the Codes, that is, those that do not admit derogation by the deciding bodies, referred to in Article 2, paragraph 2, of Code 3;

    c) to the binding negative opinions provided for by Article 8 of Code 3.

  4. After the closure of the transition, the precedence clauses of the sectoral Codes apply, including those of Code 10, Article 10, of Code 13, Article 11, and of Code 14, Article 10.

  5. The main normative links of this Code are: Constitution, Articles 1.3, 3.1, 3.2, 3.5-bis, 3.5-ter, 4–6, 10.2, 16.4, 17.9, 19, 23.1, 26, 32, 36.1 and 38; Code 1, Articles 13–22, 32, 47 and 47-ter; Code 2, Article 5; Code 3, Articles 3, 4, 8, 12, 13–19; Code 4; Code 5, Articles 3 and 10; Code 6, Articles 4 and 11; Code 7, Articles 2.2, 7 and 7-bis; Codes 8 and 9; Code C.1-Annex A; Codes 10–14, including Article 8-sexies of Code 14; Code 15, Chapters III and IV.

Art. 11 — Networks path: phases and principles

  1. The networks path brings people to the Confederation where they already live. It consists of the following phases:

    a) Phase A — Seeds: mutual-aid networks and neighbourhood storehouses are born, pursuant to Article 12;

    b) Phase B — Oil spot: the networks connect with one another, map the essential supply chains, sign pacts with those who work in them and carry out lawful trials, pursuant to Article 13, up to the preparedness threshold of Article 14;

    c) Phase C — Boycott: the persons of the networks may make the choice of Article 15;

    d) Phase D — Conversion: those who accede form the Circles 1 and the storehouses become Common Storehouses, pursuant to Article 17; every Circle 1 born from a network is in Phase 0;

    e) Phase E — Confederation: the Circles born from the networks go through Phases 1–4 up to the closure of Article 10, pursuant to Article 17, paragraph 8.

  2. In the networks path:

    a) participation and accession are individual and free and everyone may leave at any time, pursuant to Articles 4.8 and 38.1 of the Constitution and Article 2, paragraph 8;

    b) no one is in command: the offices of the networks, of the entities that hold their goods and of the promoting groups are conferred by lot among volunteers, are short and rotate, pursuant to Articles 4.3 and 4.4 of the Constitution and, for the entities, within the limits of Article 16, paragraph 3, of Code 15;

    c) defence is civil and unarmed, pursuant to Article 17.9 of the Constitution and Article 8-sexies of Code 14, including its paragraph 13, and is exercised within the limits of the law in force, pursuant to Article 38.6 of the Constitution; sabotage, damage, unlawful blocking of traffic and unlawful occupation are excluded in all cases, as in Article 5, paragraph 5, of Annex B; until the conversion, the report of Article 8-sexies, paragraph 10, of Code 14 is replaced by the call to the emergency services and the competent authorities;

    d) decisions, accounts and aggregated data are public pursuant to Article 2, paragraph 5, except for data that expose persons or infrastructure to risk;

    e) the neighbourhood storehouse distributes the Emergency Basket to every participant who needs it, for whatever cause, according to public rules approved by the assembly of the network and independent of the choice of Article 15; the threshold of Article 14 measures only whether the stocks are enough for all participants;

    f) essential services remain open to everyone, pursuant to Article 16.

  3. Until the choice of the persons no one stops paying. In Phases A and B, and in every phase for those who do not make the choice of Article 15, everything takes place in compliance with the legal order in force: rents, utility bills, debts, taxes and contractual obligations are paid and performed normally. The networks, the entities and the promoting groups shall not ask anyone to suspend a payment, and the trials of Article 13, paragraph 5, shall not involve any default.

  4. The networks are not Circles and the participants are not members of the Confederation until they accede pursuant to Article 17. Articles 11–20 apply to the networks and, insofar as compatible, Article 2, paragraphs 2–9, and Article 9-bis; in the networks path the guarantee of Article 2, paragraph 1, is replaced by the threshold of Article 14 and by Article 17, paragraph 4. The other provisions of this Code and of Codes 1–15 apply from the conversion, according to the phases of Article 3. The networks may first adopt individual tools of the Codes, such as the minimum Storehouse, the Labour Balance, the drawing by lot and rotation of offices, decisions in assembly and the voluntary restorative procedure of Articles 5–8 of Code 4, without thereby becoming Circles. Facts that constitute criminal offences remain within the competence of the authorities, pursuant to Article 37, paragraph 2, of Code 4.

Art. 12 — Phase A: networks, neighbourhood storehouses and legal forms

  1. A network is born when people of a neighbourhood, a village or a building begin to pool part of their things and their time: shared shopping, help for those in difficulty, exchange of time and skills, a neighbourhood storehouse. It connects to the networks path with a public declaration on the portal of Article 20, by which it accepts Articles 11–20.

  2. The network:

    a) decides in an assembly open to the participants, with the individual vote of each person and without delegation;

    b) keeps a public inventory, in aggregated form, of the neighbourhood storehouse and of the funds;

    c) makes agreements with producers, such as farmers, craftspeople and small workshops that sell directly to the network, giving preference to the parties of Article 18, paragraph 1, of Code 15;

    d) reduces dependence on the outside with its own energy, such as energy communities, with stocks of medicines and spare parts and with the know-how to repair;

    e) stores and distributes food, water and medicines in accordance with the health regulations in force; medicines are procured and distributed only in the forms permitted by law, with the opinion of the competent persons of Article 14, paragraph 2.

  3. Legal forms from the start. The neighbourhood storehouses, the stocks, the funds and every good placed in common are registered, from Phase A, in the name of an association, a cooperative or another non-profit entity recognised by the legal order, constituted by the network or already existing. The statute shall incorporate, within the limits of the law, the constraints of Article 16, paragraphs 1–3, of Code 15: indivisible assets, no distribution of profits, individual vote, offices drawn by lot and rotating and, in the event of dissolution, devolution to another network or to an entity with the same purposes. The statute admits as a member every participant who so requests, provides that the assembly of members incorporates the decisions of the network within the limits of the law and governs the transformation of the entity or the transfer of the goods to the entity of a Circle after the vote of Article 17, paragraph 1. The assets of the entity are separate from those of the persons, within the limits of the law; they are not liable for the debts of the participants, except in the cases in which the law allows creditors to act on the goods conferred.

  4. The conferral of goods and money to the network is voluntary and documented, pursuant to Article 16, paragraph 4, of Code 15. No one may be induced to confer goods or to leave external activities, pursuant to Article 2, paragraph 8. The entity does not accept conferrals or payments that remove goods from the reach of the creditors of those who make them; anyone who confers goods or money is informed in writing of this limit. Goods conferred for temporary use are entered in the public inventory of the network and returned to those who leave the network.

  5. Communities that already exist. The solidarity communities that already live by rules close to those of the Confederation, such as communities with a common purse, ecovillages and their networks, are the first nodes of the networks path. They take part as they are, without changing their own internal order, and may at any time join as associated Communities pursuant to Article 4.5 of the Constitution and Articles 21 and 21-bis of Code 15. They enter a Basin, or contribute to constituting one with the Circles born from the networks or with the pilot Circles, in accordance with Article 3, paragraph 7, letter f); until a Basin exists, the equivalence at the boundary is verified pursuant to Article 21-bis, paragraph 6, of Code 15. At the conversion of the network the community chooses whether to enter the Circle 1 born from it or to remain, or become, an associated Community; in the latter case it remains a distinct Community with its own territory and enters the Basin when this is constituted.

  6. People who wish to live by the rules already establish a pilot Circle and follow the pilot Circles path. The pilot Circle may be a node of the network of its territory and offer it the knowledge and the rules it has tested.

Art. 13 — Phase B: connection, essential supply chains and lawful trials

  1. The networks connect with one another in the municipality, then between municipalities, regions and countries. The connection takes place through written and revocable agreements and creates neither a centre nor bodies with power over the networks: each network decides for itself. The networks pool knowledge, open standards and reserve stocks and build the common structures on a small scale, such as the knowledge archive, shared stocks and transport agreements; at the conversion these pass to the Circles and to the competent Councils according to the phase.

  2. Map of the supply chains. The networks of a territory jointly draw up the map of the essential supply chains: where food, water, energy and medicines come from, how many stocks there are and who knows how to do what. The map is public in aggregated form; data that expose persons or infrastructure to risk are excluded from it.

  3. Pacts with those who work in the essential supply chains. Anyone who works in an essential supply chain may sign a pact with the network of their territory. With the pact the person undertakes to continue their work and to serve everyone even if the persons of the networks make the choice of Article 15; the network undertakes to provide the protections of Article 16. The pact is personal, voluntary and revocable at any time, contains no penalties and does not oblige anything contrary to the law or to the employment contract. It is kept confidential; only the aggregated counts by supply chain and by territory are public. The data of the pacts are processed with the explicit consent of the person and in compliance with data protection legislation; they are kept as long as the pact is in force and deleted upon its revocation or at the conversion.

  4. Training and relief. The networks teach the skills that serve needs, in accordance with Code 5 insofar as applicable, and for every essential job they train in advance other persons who can provide relief, within the limits of the qualifications required by law.

  5. Lawful trials. The network verifies whether it holds up through trials in which no one stops paying anything, such as a weekend in which the neighbourhood lives only on the network’s storehouses, a month in which the participants buy only from the network’s producers or a trial distribution of the Emergency Basket. Participation in the trials is voluntary. Within thirty days the network publishes on the portal, in aggregated form, the number of participants, the goods distributed and what was lacking; the results serve the verification of Article 14.

Art. 14 — Preparedness threshold, verification and vote

  1. Threshold. The network is ready when the following conditions are verified together:

    a) the Emergency Basket is guaranteed for six months to all participants: the stocks in the network’s storehouses, plus what the network’s producers have undertaken in writing to deliver in the same period, cover the Emergency Basket of all participants for six months, and the network’s funds cover the remaining costs, including storage and transport;

    b) in the territory of the network pacts have been signed pursuant to Article 13, paragraph 3, in each of the supply chains of water, energy, food and health care, by a number of persons who, according to the map of the supply chains and with the trained relief, is enough to keep those services running for everyone.

1-bis. Each person is a participant of only one network for the purposes of the threshold and of the vote, and participants who are not entered in the register of those entitled kept by the network pursuant to paragraph 6 are not counted. Pacts and producers’ commitments that serve several networks are divided among them, by written and public agreement, so that each is counted only once; the territory of the network is the one declared on the portal pursuant to Article 12, paragraph 1.

  1. The list of goods and of the quantities per person of the Emergency Basket is adopted by the connected networks, with the opinion of persons competent in nutrition and health; it takes account of age and health conditions, according to the criterion of Article 19, paragraph 4, of Code 1, and is public.

  2. A country that starts alone. The verification ascertains the six-month threshold of paragraph 1, letter a), and, if it is reached, also the twelve-month threshold, which includes a public plan for imports pursuant to Article 18, paragraph 2, and the support of the networks of other countries, declared by a vote of the networks that give it in the forms of paragraph 6. The date voted by the network is valid only if, by the day before, the same date is approved by the networks of at least one other country pursuant to paragraph 6; otherwise it is valid only if the network has had the twelve-month threshold verified.

  3. Public verification. The threshold data are published on the portal in aggregated form. The verification is carried out by three persons drawn by lot among the volunteers of networks other than the one verified, who do not participate in it and who abstain in the cases of Article 5, paragraph 3, letter f); they may check by sample, under a duty of confidentiality, the pacts and the producers’ commitments. Anyone may submit observations for at least seventy-two hours, pursuant to Article 2, paragraph 5. The outcome is reasoned and published; the verification is repeated if before the vote the data change so that they no longer meet the threshold and, in any case, after ninety days. The verification is also repeated between the vote and the chosen date, not earlier than the thirty days preceding the date. If the threshold is no longer met, the outcome is published immediately and the vote loses effect; the network may vote again after a new positive verification. The list of volunteers for the verification is public on the portal; the drawing by lot is public and reproducible. Members of pilot Circles and of associated Communities may also be drawn. If the list does not allow the drawing by lot, the verification is suspended until it does.

  4. The verification establishes only the figures of the threshold: it neither decides nor recommends the boycott.

  5. Vote. When the threshold has been verified, the participants of the network may vote on the choice of Article 15 and on its date. The proposal is submitted by at least one tenth of the participants entitled to vote and is published on the portal for at least fourteen days before the opening of the vote, which remains open for at least seven days. Participants who have reached the age of fourteen vote, pursuant to Article 36.1 of the Constitution; the vote is individual and secret, without delegation, and takes place in person or through the portal with a system that respects the guarantees of Article 7-bis, paragraphs 2, 3, 5 and 7, of Code 7, in which the register of those entitled to vote is kept by the network; the software is open source and is checked, before the first vote, by competent persons drawn by lot among the volunteers of other networks. The register of those entitled to vote and the ballot box are entrusted to different persons, drawn by lot among the volunteer participants. The vote is counted separately for each network and the proposal is approved in a network if the majority of all the participants of the network entitled to vote, including those who do not vote, votes in favour. A rejected proposal may be resubmitted after ninety days. A common date is sought: each network may propose it on the portal and the proposal is voted on in each network under the same rules. Before the first vote in a country, a local professional legal opinion is obtained on the forms of the vote, on the protection of the participants and on the risks for the entities of Article 12, paragraph 3, for those who hold offices in them and for those who run the portal; the opinion is requested by persons drawn by lot among the volunteer participants and is published. If the opinion indicates that the entities or the portal would be liable, the vote takes place with tools separate from them.

  6. The vote expresses a collective political choice and obliges no one, pursuant to Article 15, paragraph 2. No one may be excluded from the neighbourhood storehouse, from the solidarity funds or from the network because of how they voted or for not having made the choice.

Art. 15 — Phase C: the boycott as a choice of the persons

  1. The boycott means stopping working and consuming for profit, not for needs: what serves only the market stops and what serves needs continues. Its forms may include the consumer boycott, the strike of work for the market, staying at home, the rent and utility bill strike and the suspension of debts to banks; the persons of the networks have indicated that, if the choice is made, it is made together and once only.

  2. The boycott is not a rule of this Code. It is a political, non-violent and collective choice, which belongs to the persons of the networks through their vote pursuant to Article 14, paragraph 6. Each person decides freely whether to make it and answers for it personally according to the law. This Code neither prescribes it, nor authorises it, nor governs its forms, except for the vote of Article 14, paragraph 6, and no consequence may follow from not making it.

  3. The entities of the networks, the promoting groups and, after the conversion, the Circles born from the networks and their entities shall not resolve on the boycott, shall not finance it and shall not use the common goods, the Storehouses or the Common Fund for acts contrary to the law. Before and after the choice of the persons, only the lawful activities of Articles 12, 13, 16 and 18 continue, including the neighbourhood storehouses, the distribution of the Emergency Basket to the participants, the solidarity funds, legal assistance and the essential services open to everyone.

  4. Solidarity funds and legal assistance. From Phase A the network may set up, within the entity of Article 12, paragraph 3, a solidarity fund and a legal assistance network. They help, within the limits of the law, anyone in difficulty, for whatever cause, according to need. The rules of the help are public and the help does not depend on how the person voted.

  5. After the conversion, the Common Fund may bear the legal costs of members and, in the cases of Article 17, paragraph 3, of Code 15, the settlement of their debts towards third parties, paying the creditor or the professional directly pursuant to Article 3.5-bis of the Constitution.

Art. 16 — Essential supply chains and services open to everyone

  1. Anyone who works in an essential supply chain and has signed the pact continues to serve everyone, including those who do not participate in the networks and those who do not accede to the Confederation, on the same conditions for all, during the boycott and after the conversion. The Circles born from the networks ensure to anyone who is in their territory the guarantees of Article 1.3 of the Constitution and to those who are not members the rights of Article 4.9.

  2. For those who work in an essential supply chain the network, and after the conversion the Circle:

    a) delivers the Emergency Basket first to the person and to those who live with them, without reducing the share of the other participants and in compliance with Article 19, paragraph 4, of Code 1;

    b) organises short shifts with the relief of the persons trained pursuant to Article 13, paragraph 4, within the limits of the employment relationships and of the law;

    c) supports with the solidarity fund and with the legal assistance of Article 15, paragraph 4, those who risk their job or a lawsuit;

    d) from the conversion, counts as CBO the work carried out in the essential supply chain in the service of the territory, pursuant to Article 19, paragraph 3, of Code 15; in the same way, the pilot Circle counts as CBO the work in the essential supply chain of its members who have signed the pact of Article 13, paragraph 3.

Art. 17 — Phase D: conversion; Phase E: Confederation

  1. Conversion. The conversion of a network is decided by its participants with a vote in the forms of Article 14, paragraph 6, second to sixth sentences; participants who do not then accede also vote. The conversion does not presuppose the threshold of Article 14 or the choice of Article 15. It has effect only for those who accede: each person accedes individually, pursuant to Articles 4.8 and 38.1 of the Constitution, and the choice of the majority does not bind those who do not accede. For a minor under sixteen, accession is made by the person who has responsibility for their care, in their interest, after informing and hearing them, pursuant to Article 26.5 of the Constitution; from the sixteenth year the minor decides for themselves.

  2. Circles 1. The networks become Circles 1 starting from what exists, neighbourhoods and municipalities, with the size of Article 5.1, letter b), of the Constitution; a larger network forms several contiguous Circles 1. The first offices are drawn by lot among those who accede, pursuant to Articles 4.3 and 4.4 of the Constitution. The entity of Article 12, paragraph 3, becomes the entity of the Circle pursuant to Article 16 of Code 15 or transfers the goods to it, according to its statute.

2-bis. Goods at the conversion. Before the conversion the network publishes the inventory of the neighbourhood storehouse. Goods conferred for temporary use are returned to those who do not accede, if they request them; of the common stocks, the part corresponding to the participants who do not accede remains at their disposal for six months or is devolved, according to the statute, to another entity with the same purposes. If the accessions are not enough to form a Circle 1 pursuant to Article 5.1, letter b), of the Constitution, the network continues as a network and those who have acceded may establish a pilot Circle pursuant to Article 12, paragraph 6. If several Circles 1 are formed, the goods are divided in proportion to the acceding persons of each.

  1. Common Storehouses. The neighbourhood storehouses, and the storehouses and supermarkets whose owners accede and confer them voluntarily pursuant to Article 16, paragraph 4, of Code 15, become Common Storehouses in accordance with Articles 13–22 of Code 1.

  2. Emergency Basket. Until the Common Storehouses guarantee levels 1 and 2 of the Basket, they distribute at least the Emergency Basket to every member, with the fair rationing of Article 19, paragraphs 3–6, of Code 1.

  3. No one loses the home they live in. The conversion takes from no one the home in which they live. Anyone who accedes and confers or entrusts to the Circle the home in which they live keeps its use as their main home, pursuant to Article 10.2 of the Constitution and Article 47, paragraphs 2 and 3, of Code 1; anyone who does not confer it keeps it according to the legal order in force. The Circle neither requests nor promotes the eviction of anyone; it gives the legal assistance of Article 15, paragraph 4, to those who risk losing their home, and housing pursuant to Article 47-ter, paragraph 4, letter a), of Code 1 to those who lose it. Anyone who confers the home in which they live is informed in writing that conferral for temporary use, pursuant to Article 16, paragraph 4, of Code 15, returns it to them if they leave the Confederation before the definitive regime, and that definitive conferral does not return it. A home encumbered by a mortgage or by a lease is not conferred while the encumbrance lasts; those who live there keep its use according to the legal order in force.

  4. Recognition by law. Where the majority also wins in the institutions, through elections, a referendum or a constituent assembly, and the law of the State recognises the Communities, entrusts public goods to them, transforms entities or financial means into Common Funds or continues to guarantee services until the Circles cover them, the Circles born from the networks avail themselves of that law, which forms part of the legal order in force pursuant to Article 38.6 of the Constitution, within the limits of the Constitution and the Codes. The legal form provided for by that law may replace the entity of Article 16 of Code 15; the means entrusted to the Common Funds follow Article 17 of Code 15.

  5. Those who do not accede. Those who do not accede remain free, are not treated as adversaries pursuant to Article 15, paragraph 2, letter b), of Code 15 and keep the home, the goods and the rights that the legal order in force recognises to them. They do not perform the CBO and do not have access to the Common Storehouses, except for the guarantees of Articles 1.3 and 4.9 of the Constitution and the services of Article 16. They may accede whenever they wish or request the Autonomous Coexistence Pact of Articles 4.9 and 4.9-bis of the Constitution.

  6. Meeting with the pilot Circles path. From the conversion every Circle 1 born from a network is a pilot Circle for the purposes of this Code and of the references of the Codes to the pilot Circle, and is in Phase 0 of Article 3, paragraph 1. The census, the storehouse inventory and the map of the supply chains of the network count as the initial census of letter c) of that paragraph, and the neighbourhood storehouse as the provisional minimum Storehouse of letter d), if the Transition Council verifies them. Phase E comprises Phases 1–4: the Circles born from the networks form Basins and minimum Federations, or join those already constituted, pursuant to Article 3, paragraph 7, and close the transition pursuant to Article 10. No threshold of Article 4 is shortened.

Art. 18 — Imports

  1. As long as there are countries that have not changed system, the networks and the Circles born from them obtain the energy, medicines and components they do not produce through three routes together:

    a) reducing imports first: in Phases A and B the networks focus on their own energy, stocks of medicines and spare parts, pursuant to Article 12, paragraph 2, letter d);

    b) the Common Fund of Article 17 of Code 15 and, before the conversion, the funds of the network’s entity, fed also by the savings that persons pay in freely, solely for purchases from outside and within the limits of Article 12, paragraph 4. The payment is not an obligation and gives no advantage, pursuant to Article 19-bis, paragraph 2, of Code 15; those who do not pay in are excluded from nothing;

    c) the transfer to the outside of real surpluses, such as food, crafts and services, without profit, pursuant to Articles 15, paragraph 3, and 19 of Code 15, and direct exchanges with the networks and Circles of other countries.

  2. The plan for imports of Article 14, paragraph 3, indicates for twelve months the goods that the network does not produce, the quantities, the possible suppliers in accordance with Article 18 of Code 15, the means with which they are paid pursuant to paragraph 1 and the alternatives if a supply is interrupted, in accordance with Article 20 of Code 15.

Art. 19 — Promoting groups

  1. In every country promoting groups may be born that help the networks and the neighbourhood storehouses to be born, map the supply chains with the networks, collect the pacts of Article 13, paragraph 3, and spread knowledge and the rules. They prepare and do not govern: they do not decide for the networks or in their place.

  2. A group is a promoting group within the meaning of this Code if it is:

    a) without leaders: offices are drawn by lot among those who volunteer, last at most ninety days and rotate, pursuant to Article 4.4, letters a) and b), of the Constitution; it has no leaders, permanent spokespersons or party structures, pursuant to letter c) of the same Article;

    b) open and transparent: anyone may take part; decisions, accounts and documents are public, except for data that expose persons to risk;

    c) autonomous: it organises itself according to the conditions of its country; where organising is dangerous, the other groups support it without exposing people;

    d) connected by common rules, not by a centre: the groups exchange knowledge, standards and news according to a common and public protocol, and no group commands the others.

  3. The choice of Article 15 and its date belong to the persons of the networks, through the vote of Article 14, paragraph 6; the promoting groups do not decide them.

  4. A promoting group that needs a legal form follows Article 12, paragraph 3.

  5. They dissolve themselves. When the networks of their territory have converted into Circles, the promoting groups dissolve and their tasks, archives and tools pass to the Circles and to the common structures. No one keeps a role, a precedence or an advantage for having been part of a promoting group, pursuant to Article 4.4, letter e), of the Constitution.

Art. 20 — Networks portal

  1. The networks use in common a portal, and later an application, to learn about the rules and technical knowledge in all languages, find nearby networks and groups, declare their participation including without a public name, organise offers, needs, inventories of the neighbourhood storehouses and shifts, hold assemblies and votes and count the participants, the months of Emergency Basket guaranteed, the pacts signed and the results of the lawful trials.

  2. The portal:

    a) is federated: it is made of several connected servers, run by different networks, and continues to work if one of them is shut down; it has neither a centre nor a central AI, pursuant to Article 23.1 of the Constitution;

    b) collects little data: only what is necessary and for the minimum time necessary, pursuant to Article 7 of Code 7; participation may be declared without a public name: the identity is known only to those who keep the register of the network, under a duty of confidentiality, and the declaration of minors follows the age of consent laid down by the law of the country; counts are published in aggregated form and all profiling is prohibited, pursuant to Article 2, paragraph 2, of Code 7;

    c) is encrypted: messages are protected and votes follow Article 14, paragraph 6;

    d) has open code: anyone may inspect it, copy it and run it in their own country, pursuant to Article 19 of the Constitution; for voting, Article 9-bis, paragraph 1, letter b), applies;

    e) also works offline: local networks can organise, count and vote in person if the internet is cut off, and votes cast in person are counted pursuant to Article 7-bis, paragraph 7, of Code 7.

  3. The networks may begin with federated and open-source tools that already exist. At the conversion the portal becomes the coordination node of the Circle on the federated Network and merges into the federated Network of Code 7 when that Code is activated.
