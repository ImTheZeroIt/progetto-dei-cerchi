Adversarial review of the proposals

Working translation. The Italian text is the authoritative one.

This page sets out the objections found to the first version of the eleven amendment proposals, published on 5 October 2026. The proposals were then rewritten: version 2 states, for each one, what has changed. The quotations from the proposals therefore refer to the earlier text.

How it was done

The review was carried out with an artificial intelligence assistant: four independent readings of the texts, each with a single task, to find defects. Each reading compared the proposals with the Constitution, the sixteen Codes and the annexes, looking for contradictions, wrong cross-references, cases in which the proposal makes things worse and undeclared costs.

Every objection reported here cites an article in force. The 77 citations that support them were rechecked one by one against the texts: they are all accurate. The calculations in sections 9 and 11 were redone. The estimate in section 10 was not rechecked.

Three limits. The external sources (Millward-Hopkins 2020, WHO 2018) were not verified. A review of this kind finds the errors internal to the texts; it does not say whether a rule works among real people. And it does not replace the objections of readers: they are still needed.

Outcome in brief

None of the eleven proposals held up as it stood.

Proposal Outcome The defect that matters most
1 Clothing and hygiene to be corrected disposables clash with the prohibition of non-cyclable materials (Const. 14.1)
2 Adequate housing to be corrected it does not say whether substandard housing remains “adequate” for other provisions
3 Connectivity to be corrected the device “may coincide with the Node”, which under the Constitution is offline
4 Energy Basket to be corrected the list omits services already guaranteed: it reads as a step backwards
5 CdR for culture to be rewritten workshops and research are already Base Contribution; no one knows who validates
6 Subsoil to be rewritten it touches an article protected by the Inviolable Core
7 Raw materials to be rewritten it regulates extraction even if proposal 6 falls; it does not exclude coal and uranium
8 Robots to be corrected the definition includes the washing machine; it contradicts Art. 6, which it says it does not touch
9 CdR decay to be corrected it is not “the only reading”: there is one more faithful to the text
10 Supplement at the cap to be corrected “equal value” does not say whether initial or current
11 Thresholds to be corrected in small Communities it removes the 48-hour urgent route

Three common defects

No proposal declares the administrative time. Code 3, Art. 12-bis, para. 13, requires this of every new rule, and sets administrative time at no more than one tenth of the Base Contribution. The first hours balance already puts civic functions at that cap.

Whatever enters the Basket rises in priority. Under Code 1, Art. 32, when time is insufficient the distribution of Basket goods comes before the care of frail persons and the maintenance of water and energy. Proposals 1, 2, 3 and 4 add items to the Basket without saying so. The sentence “none restricts a right” does not hold under scarcity.

A Basket good bought outside is a critical dependency. Code 15, Art. 12, paras. 4 and 5, requires for each one an Exit Plan with a fixed deadline. Code 16, Art. 4, requires seventy per cent of levels 1 and 2 of the Basket to be produced in the Basin before moving to the next phase. Textiles, shoes, nappies and electronics widen that base. Proposals 1 and 3 speak only of “entry in the Register”.

Errors of fact in the texts of the proposals

They were wrong in the first version, whatever the decision.

  • Proposal 1. “Unconditional” is in Article 1.3 of the Constitution, not in 1.2.
  • Proposal 2. The “emergency reception” attributed to Code 11, Art. 6-ter, para. 6, does not exist in any text: that paragraph speaks of “an equipped stopping area”.
  • Proposal 5. Art. 43, para. 3, does not limit the CdR to one per hour: it is an additional recognition, which can double it.
  • Proposal 8. “It does not amend Article 6” is false: paragraph 7 contradicts Art. 6, para. 6. Article 19.4 of the Constitution speaks of source code; schematics are in 19.3. Art. 16 prohibits weapons, not “coercing and restraining”.
  • Proposal 9. “It is the only reading” and “it costs nothing” are not true (see below).

Proposal by proposal

1 — Clothing and hygiene

  • Medium. Disposables and leather. Pads, nappies and incontinence products are largely disposable: the Constitution prohibits non-cyclable materials (14.1) and landfill (14.5). Footwear is often made of leather, prohibited by 13.5. Correction: prefer washable and compostable items; disposables only under the conditions of 14.1; refer to 13.5.
  • Medium. Ownership. Under Annex A, Art. 1, para. 4, an object of the Material Basket is a common good. The note says garments are personal, the text does not. Correction: from delivery they are Personal-Use Goods (Const. 10.3).
  • Medium. Privacy. Withdrawals from the Storehouses are tracked (Const. 10.5). Menstrual and incontinence products reveal personal conditions. Correction: classify them as sensitive goods (Code 1, Art. 19, para. 5).
  • Medium. Seat. A “Textile and Housing Storehouse” of the Storehouse Council already exists (Code 1, Art. 14). The proposal places goods that are almost all purchased under a production Council.
  • Low. “Suited to the tasks performed” overlaps with PPE; thresholds “in days of autonomy” are not needed for durable garments.

2 — Adequate housing

  • High. What counts as “adequate”. Paragraph 4 prohibits refusing substandard housing. But Code 1, Art. 47, para. 3, and Code 4, Arts. 29 and 34, guarantee “adequate housing” to those who are transferred or subjected to a precautionary measure. If substandard housing counts, the definition is empty; if it does not, protection measures become impossible to carry out where housing that meets the standard is lacking. A maximum deadline for compliance is also missing. Correction: say so expressly, with a maximum deadline and appeal to the Guarantee Circle.
  • Medium. Private space. The 15 m² include the common spaces of the Nucleus, with no reserved minimum: in a cohabitation of fifteen people a room can shrink to a few metres. Minors have no space of their own.
  • Medium. Competence. Floor area and temperature do not fall within the functions of the Habitat and Territory Council (Code 10, Art. 3). The Energy and Water Council, the only one with thermal competence, is missing.
  • Medium. Building. Where space is lacking the standard can be reached only by building, against the prohibition of land take (Code 10, Art. 2).
  • Low. “Degrees” without a unit; heat has no threshold; after the first application nothing prevents going below 15 m² and 18 °C.

3 — Connectivity and learning materials

  • Medium. The Node is offline. Const. 19.1: “personal computing Node that is offline”. A communication device cannot coincide with the Node. Correction: “distinct from the Node”.
  • Medium. Impossible obligation. Code 2, Art. 5, absolutely prohibits devices that do not work offline or escape community control; Code 1, Art. 35, prohibits planned obsolescence. With today’s telephones the Community might have no lawful device to give. Correction: within the limits of Code 2; otherwise shared and assisted access.
  • Medium. Age. The texts already use twelve years (Code 5, Art. 6, para. 9), not ten. The paragraph referred to for minors concerns the personal AI, not the device.
  • Medium. Materials. “The pathways to which the person is admitted” introduces an admission that Code 5 does not know, and makes the materials of any pathway part of the Basket. Correction: limit to literacy and to compulsory and safety training.
  • Low. Every person already has a Node: the shared device per Nucleus is partly a duplicate. In the same Code “Art. 3, para. 6-bis” and “Art. 6-bis” would come into being.

4 — Energy Basket

  • High. Step backwards. Art. 4, para. 1, of Code 8 already also guarantees cooling, the Health Storehouse and the Network node. The list “in first application at least” omits them. Correction: “without prejudice to all the services of paragraph 1”, and add only the two new values: 20 litres of hot water and 18 °C.
  • High. Inconsistency with proposal 2. In proposal 2 the 18 °C is a target to be reached “with a deadline”. In proposal 4 it enters the minimum share: under Art. 4, para. 3, its absence is a level 1 emergency.
  • Medium. Fossil fuels. Level 1 makes the transitional use of fossil sources lawful (Code 8, Art. 5, para. 3) and triggers the six-month deadline of Art. 10. The proposal does not say so.
  • Medium. The same temperature would be set by two different bodies (proposals 2 and 4).

5 — CdR for cultural activities

  • High. Already Base Contribution. Under Code 5, Art. 2, para. 3, time devoted to “teaching or documenting knowledge” is Base Contribution, even when the activity is free. Open workshops and research made public would be counted twice. Correction: remove them, or exclude time already counted.
  • High. Who validates. Validation requires those who use, those who have “the relevant competence” and those who manage the Storehouse (Const. 9.2). None of the sixteen Competence Circles concerns the arts. What remains is audience approval. Correction: validation establishes only that the activity took place, that it was open to all, and the hours; not its merit.
  • Medium. Which hours. Rehearsal, composition and study cannot be verified. With three hours of rehearsal a week anyone reaches the cap. The simulation of the cap assumes that half of the people accrue no CdR: with this proposal that no longer holds.
  • Medium. The “never”. Today no text excludes spiritual and affective activities: the proposal removes a possibility and does not declare it. Who decides whether a liturgical choir is culture or worship?
  • Medium. Preparation of events. The final clause would reward the preparation of a religious celebration: Const. 25.2 prohibits allocating common resources to religious denominations.
  • Medium. Incentive. CdRs serve to cover peaks and emergencies (Code 1, Art. 6, para. 6). If they accrue in the same way through pleasant activities, the pull weakens.
  • Medium. “Works intended for common spaces” remain personal property (Const. 10.3): the author accrues CdR and can take them back.

6 — Subsoil as a common good and extraction as an exception

  • High. Inviolable Core. Const. 37.4 removes “the regime of the Commons” “from any revision procedure”. The text does not distinguish between extending and reducing: “extends, does not reduce” is the proposal’s own reading. Correction: do not touch Art. 3.1. That same article already admits “every other good classified as common” by the Codes: the subsoil can be so classified in Code 10.
  • High. Regression. Art. 14.2 opens with “precaution and of environmental non-regression”. Writing a new exception there is a regression in the very paragraph that prohibits it. 14.3 declares living soil and waters an “inviolable limit”.
  • High. “Beyond the site”. By implication, within the site everything is permitted, and “site” has no definition: whoever proposes draws its boundary. Air and ecosystems disappear from the list; forests and animal sanctuaries are not excluded; “temporary” has no time limit.
  • High. Zero Waste. A mine produces tailings and sludge: Const. 14.5 prohibits landfill. Extraction would remain unlawful even if the proposal were adopted.

7 — Raw materials, recovery and extraction

  • High. Unwritten dependence on proposal 6. If 7 passes and 6 does not, the Code contains a complete procedure for opening extraction sites, without the constitutional limits. Correction: split into two articles: one on reduction, recovery and inventory, valid immediately; one on extraction, which takes effect only if 14.2 changes.
  • High. Coal and uranium. They are “mineral resources” and serve “for energy”. With Code 8, Art. 5, para. 3, a legal route opens to new fossil extraction. Correction: exclude them expressly.
  • High. Too early. Code 10 is activated in Phase 1; sanctuaries, the Biodiversity Council and the Safety Council in Phase 2 (Code 16, Art. 3). Annex C prohibits irreversible decisions on the territory in the first year. Before Circle 2 the “double consensus” is a single vote of the same Assembly (Code 3, Art. 14, para. 10).
  • High. Consensus is weak. In Code 3 “consensus” is the absence of reasoned objections, ordinary resolutions have no quorum, and a majority vote can be used. Opening a mine would require less than establishing a sanctuary. Correction: quorum and qualified majority.
  • High. Who votes. The General Assembly is territorial (Code 3, Art. 14, para. 1), not “of the Circles that will use the material”. The beneficiaries would vote; the downstream Communities that do not use the material would be left out.
  • High. The push to extract. Once metals are entered in the Dependency Register, the Exit Plan with a fixed deadline is mandatory. Once reuse and recycling are exhausted, the only way out is the mine, and the refusal of one Community blocks the phase transition of the whole Basin. Correction: for raw materials the Exit Plan may consist only of reduction, reuse and recycling.
  • High. Who restores. Paragraph 8 says “the site is restored”, without a subject. Failing that, it falls on the host Community. Correction: borne by those who use the material, in proportion.
  • High. Seat. Under Code 10, Art. 10, para. 8, each Code prevails only in its own subject matter. Deliberation, labour, distribution and purchases belong to Codes 3, 1 and 15: the derogation from sortition written into Code 10 risks being invalid.
  • High. Short exclusions. Ecological corridors, woodland, infiltration areas and buffer zones are not named; no distance from inhabited areas.
  • Medium. No maximum time limit. “Essential requirement” is not a term of the texts. “Voluntary” and “rotating among the Communities” do not fit together. A well requires two mandatory opinions, a mine none. Excavating a landfill to recover materials has no safeguards. Smelting and refining are not regulated. No sanction.

8 — Robots and autonomous machines

  • High. Definition. “Any machine that moves or acts in physical space without the continuous command of a person” includes a washing machine, a 3D printer, a pump on a timer. Code 7, Art. 1, already defines “automation” by the same criterion. Correction: a robot is a machine that moves or manipulates objects in a space shared with people, choosing its trajectory or action by itself.
  • High. Personal property. Const. 10.3 declares that light individual tools “may not be collectivised”. A personal motorised aid would become a common good. Correction: robots used in the Base Contribution or in production are common goods.
  • High. Time saved. Art. 6, para. 6, leaves to the Community the choice of how to use the time freed. Paragraph 7 imposes a reduction in everyone’s hours. Two incompatible rules would apply to robots. Correction: refer to Art. 6, para. 6, or amend it expressly.
  • Medium. Remote control. A drone piloted continuously is not a “robot”: the prohibitions on arming it and on surveillance do not apply to it. Correction: those prohibitions apply to every machine.
  • Medium. Stop. “Reachable by anyone” allows anyone to stop without reason a machine that sustains a vital function. For a machine at height an instantaneous stop may be the danger. Correction: a stop that brings the machine to a safe state, accessible in accordance with the Safety Sheet.
  • Medium. Surveillance. The prohibition on “identifying or following” also prevents recognising the authorised operator, the trolley that follows a worker, and the search for a missing person.
  • Medium. Three different Sheets under the name “the STV” and two Councils. Undeclared derogation from sortition. No sanction or deadline for bringing existing machines into compliance. Nothing on the software that guides the robot.
  • Low. Stop, priority for dangerous tasks and the Dependency Register already exist: four paragraphs repeat rules in force.

9 — CdR decay

  • Medium. It is not the only reading. The text in force says: full value for twelve months, then a quarter less each quarter, extinction at the twenty-fourth. There are three possible readings.
Reading Full value for Extinction Hours a week to reach the cap
Reduction at the end of the quarter (proposal) 15 months 24th month 2.8
Gradual reduction, one twelfth a month 12 months 24th month 3.0
Reduction at the start of the quarter 12 months 21st month 3.4

Only the gradual one respects both sentences. The proposal chose the most generous and presented it as obligatory.

  • Medium. What a reduced CdR is worth. Whoever uses the oldest CdRs first uses the reduced ones. No text says whether a CdR at a quarter covers one UCR or a quarter of a UCR. Correction: each CdR counts for the value it has at that moment.
  • Low. Curve and order of use are two amendments in one. “Accrual” is not defined: day of the activity or of the validation?

10 — Supplement for those at the cap

  • High. “Equal value”. The oldest CdRs are the most reduced. If “equal value” is the initial value, a new CdR extinguishes one worth a quarter and the balance exceeds the cap. If it is the current value, it cancels four. The Code does not even say how the balance is measured. Correction: “for equal current value”; the balance is the sum of current values.
  • Medium. It does not do what it says. Those at the cap see an identical balance: they gain only duration. The difference shows when they stop. Estimate not rechecked: after twelve months of inactivity the balance would be 193 CdR instead of 81.
  • Medium. Transparency. Every replacement counts as both awarded and extinguished: the quarterly data of Art. 46 swell. Correction: distinguish those extinguished by decay from those extinguished by replacement.
  • Medium. Disparity that remains. Travel within the Base Contribution, also not chosen, remains outside. And sortition is among those “available”: “not chosen” is not always true.

11 — Thresholds in proportion to size

  • High. Urgency. Convening within 48 hours exists only in the special procedure, for high-risk operations below 500 UCR (Art. 7-bis). In a Community of thirty people, the urgent replacement of a 200 UCR pump would end up above the high threshold: ordinary Assembly, with no urgent route. Correction: for high risk below 500 UCR, paragraphs 2, 3 and 4 of Art. 7-bis remain in every case.
  • High. Goods that cannot be divided. The price of a pump or a vehicle does not fall with the Community; the threshold does. For thirty people the low threshold is worth about 1,500 euros of purchases and the high one about 7,700. Medicines, kitchen, workshop and vehicles would almost all go to the Assembly. Possible correction: a fixed part plus a proportional one.
  • High. Newly founded Community. “The number of members on the first day of the year” is zero for a Community founded in March: every operation would be “high”. It hits precisely the pilot Circle. Correction: the number from the latest quarterly balance or from the act of accession; minimum equal to thirty people.
  • Medium. Who is counted. The proposal says “member”, the tables “people”, Code 3 “those entitled to vote”. Minors, guests, suspended persons?
  • Medium. Other points to align. Code 2, Arts. 15 and 23, and Code 1, Art. 40, speak of “the threshold” in the singular. Code 15, Arts. 17 and 18, applies the matrix to the Fund of “every Circle”: with thresholds per member the cross-reference is no longer defined for a Circle 2.
  • Medium. Calibration. “These values” may indicate the coefficients or the maxima. A calibration at 17 UCR per member keeps the proportion and brings everything back to today’s level.
  • Medium. Recurring supplies. Under the rules against splitting they are added together: a year’s medicines for thirty people exceed the high threshold.
  • Low. The percentages are accurate but calculated only on the internal labour of scenario A. The test cases required by Art. 21 are missing.

What held up

  • All the insertion points exist and no numbering collides.
  • The starting versions are accurate.
  • The Dependency Register, the prohibition of Extractive Accumulation, the General Safety Sheet and the Dossier exist under those names and in those articles.
  • The curve of proposal 9 is arithmetically consistent and matches the published simulation.
  • The figures of proposal 11 are accurate.
  • The veto of the host Community (proposal 7) is consistent with Arts. 4.1, 4.2 and 32.1 of the Constitution.
  • No other text presupposes a different decay curve.

What followed

Version 2 of the proposals follows these choices, the most cautious among those possible.

  • Proposal 6. No amendment to the Constitution. The subsoil is classified as a common good in Code 10. The exception that permitted extraction is withdrawn: the question remains open.
  • Proposal 7. Only what holds in any case remains: inventory of recoverable materials, custody of deposits, safeguards for recovery excavations. The article states expressly that it does not authorise new extraction.
  • Proposal 9. Gradual reduction, in proportion to time, the only one that respects both sentences of the text. The public CdR table and the simulation have been redone.
  • Proposal 10. The rule in force is confirmed, written expressly, with the definition of the balance. The replacement mechanism is withdrawn.
  • Proposal 11. Thresholds with a fixed part and a per-person part, today’s safeguards retained, test cases attached.
  • Proposal 5. Restricted to what takes place before the Community, without judgement of merit, with an annual limit.
  • Proposals 1, 2, 3, 4 and 8. Corrected point by point.
  • Deadline. The fourteen days start again from version 2.

Not all the objections are resolved. Those that remain are listed, proposal by proposal, under the heading “Open points” or “What it costs”.

Second reading

Version 2 too was reread in the same way, before being published. The citations and calculations almost all held; another forty objections emerged, mostly new contradictions introduced by the corrections. The most serious, and what was done about them:

  • Proposal 9. The reduction “at the end of each month” left full value for thirteen months, not twelve: the same defect criticised in the first version. Now the value falls in proportion to time, and the calculations have been redone: 18 months retained at steady state, cap reached with 3.1 hours a week.
  • Proposal 5. “Validation does not assess merit” contradicted Article 9.1 of the Constitution, which requires every service to be assessed. Now validation remains that of the Constitution; if it is positive, only the hours count.
  • Proposals 2 and 4. The same temperature had two deadlines: thirty-six months in Code 1 and six in Code 8. Now Code 1 refers to Code 8.
  • Proposal 6. A new prohibition on “digging or removing mineral resources” would, literally, also have hit foundations, wells and remediation. It has been removed: the prohibition of the Constitution remains.
  • Proposal 8. Recognition of the operator, written without limits, clashed with the prohibition of biometric recognition in Article 18 of the Constitution. Now it is permitted only by non-biometric means.
  • Proposal 7. Recovery excavation required fewer safeguards than the decision matrix already gives today. Now the matrix remains unchanged, and excavations are not permitted before Codes 13 and 14 exist.
  • Proposal 11. Some medium-risk operations moved up a level but lost the binding opinion and the audit. Now they retain them.
  • Proposals 1 and 3. The rule on priority was written in the wrong place. Now it is an express amendment to Article 32 of Code 1.

The objections that have no answer have remained in the proposals, under the heading “Open points”. An adversarial reading always finds something: two rounds do not make the texts right, they make them less wrong. That is why the proposals remain open.
